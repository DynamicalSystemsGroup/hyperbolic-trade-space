# WP23 round 3: the two views, the heatmap and the coverage table of `cubesat_r2.sysml`

Screenshots for this record: commit `2a669de`, `git show 2a669de:<path>` with `<path>` under `work-packages/WP23-rounds/gate/`.

The model is round 2's (round 3 changed nothing in it: `WP23-review.md`, "Round 3: the three metrics
that never bind"); what changed are the views, by the dispositions of the round-1 cold reads. The
views are generated by `docs/superpowers/notes/spike-sysml-diagrams/project.py` revision 4
(`--revision 4`; the default stays 3, which reproduces the committed views of the adopted model
until the data gate) and rendered by its `render.sh` (PlantUML, 700 px wide; the type measures
9.3 px on the behavioural view and 9.0 px on the assembly view at that width, against 6.9 px in
round 2). The heatmap and `k3_record.md` are `examples/adcs/model_v2/diagnostic_pair.py` on the
round-1 datasets (the model and the data are unchanged, so the pair is round 1's, regenerated);
`coverage.md` is the committed table, regenerated in `hts-paper` (unit 1 of this round). The two
datasets are the **primary regime** (`adcs_designs_cubesat_gains.csv`, the aggressive gain box) and
the **baseline regime** (`adcs_designs_cubesat.csv`, the baseline gain box), by those names
throughout; `k3_record.md` and `heatmap_stock.png` still say "stock" for the baseline regime because
`case.py`'s CaseSpec keys the regime so and the pair generator prints that key (neither file is in
this round's blast radius; an item for the PM). The intent of each view is written before the cold
read, as the sentence a reader should be able to say after one look.

## assembly_view.png

After one look a reader says: "a configuration is a cubesat of nine parts in eight kinds (the two
solar panels are one kind, drawn once with both usages named); two kinds are swapped for one of three
alternatives (the wheel class, the tracker grade), four numbers are tuned within their ranges (panel
span, battery capacity, the gains Kp and Kd), and the three untagged kinds (bus, antenna, IMU) are
fixed; that is the whole configuration space". Changes from round 2 (the dispositions): the legend
says what an untagged part means (fixed) and names them, read from the model; the gain ranges are
given per regime (primary, baseline), the regime of each gain box read from
`experiments/definitions.py` (the experiment `v2-primary` samples the aggressive box), in place of
"baseline gains / aggressive gains".

## behavioural_view.png

After one look a reader says: "every traded metric, grouped by the analyst into controls, power and
energy, and mass, is an MoE or an MoP (the stereotype in its box) that one requirement reads through
the element named in the metric's box (its calc or its measured attribute; the headless orange line
joins the two); each metric comes from the analysis or from the measured slew (the grey arrow); the
total mass is read through the launch cost, the derived MoE beside it; three requirements read no
traded metric, and each says in its own words what it reads (the available power, the peak momentum
against the momentum capacity, the eclipse peak load against the pack's peak power)".

What the caption carries, moved off the image (the round-1 cold reads: the legend at 6 to 7 px was
illegible, and the boilerplate hid the one difference between the requirement boxes):

- Colour, one rule: orange is a requirement, green a traded metric, yellow a derived quantity that is
  not traded (the launch cost). MoE or MoP is the stereotype in the box, never a colour.
- Lines: an orange line without a head joins a requirement to the metric it reads, through the
  element named in the metric's box (the quantity is shared, so the line has no direction); a grey
  arrow with a head goes from the source (the analysis def `SlewExperiment`, lilac; the measurement
  `SlewMeasurement`, grey) to the metric it produces. Only the source arrows have heads.
- Requirement boxes: every requirement in the model is a `requirement def` satisfied by
  `cubesatBaseline`, so neither is repeated in a box; a box carries its name and the one thing that
  differs between the requirements, the verification case that verifies it (`ConfigurationVerification`
  reads the configuration alone; `SlewVerification` needs the measured slew). The generator prints
  the satisfying configuration in the boxes again if the model ever makes them differ.
- The three requirements that read no traded metric (top group) carry their `require constraint` in
  the model's words: `c.availablePower > 0.0 [W]` (PowerRequirement), `peakMomentum <=
  c.momentumCapacity` (MomentumRequirement), `EclipsePeakLoad(c, peakTorque) <= BatteryPeakPower(c)`
  (BatteryPeakPowerRequirement).
- The lanes are the analyst's grouping of the metrics (the CaseSpec's subsystems), as their titles
  say; the model has no subsystem structure of its own, and a requirement is placed in the lane of
  the metric it reads.

## heatmap.png (and heatmap_stock.png)

After one look a reader says: "in the primary regime five of the nine metrics contend at every
temperature of the sweep, the total mass most, then the energy balance, the pointing error, the
depth of discharge and the settling time, and three never do (power margin, eclipse reserve, peak
slew power); the control effort fades out by the selected temperature". The three white rows are the
round's finding, not a defect of the fit: each of the three is carried by another metric or by the
swaps (`WP23-review.md`, "Round 3": the eclipse reserve and the depth of discharge are one quantity;
the power margin and the energy balance are both affine in the generated power, and in the baseline
regime's heatmap the fit carries the power margin instead; the peak slew power is a step function of
the two swaps, flat inside each cell). The colourbar cap at 0.30 still hides the total mass's 0.358
(round 2's note for the gate's redraw).
