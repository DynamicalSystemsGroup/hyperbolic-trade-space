# WP23 round 4: the two views, the heatmap pair and the record of `cubesat_r2.sysml`

Screenshots for this record: commit `2a669de`, `git show 2a669de:<path>` with `<path>` under `work-packages/WP23-rounds/gate/`.

The model is round 2's (rounds 3 and 4 changed nothing in it: `WP23-review.md`, "Round 3" and
"Round 4"); what changed are the views, by the dispositions of the round-3 cold reads (a fresh P2
and P3, who found nothing that blocks). The views are generated by
`docs/superpowers/notes/spike-sysml-diagrams/project.py` revision 5 (`--revision 5`; the default
stays 3, which reproduces the committed views of the adopted model until the data gate, and
revision 4 reproduces round 3's text) and rendered by its `render.sh` (PlantUML, 700 px wide; the
type measures 9.2 px on the behavioural view, 9.3 px in round 3, and 9.0 px on the assembly view).
The heatmap pair and `k3_record.md` are `examples/adcs/model_v2/diagnostic_pair.py` on the round-1
datasets, unchanged since round 1 (`README.md` here); `coverage.md` is the committed table. The two
datasets are the **primary regime** (`adcs_designs_cubesat_gains.csv`, the aggressive gain box) and
the **baseline regime** (`adcs_designs_cubesat.csv`, the baseline gain box), by those two names in
every file of this folder: the heatmap files, the record's headings, the figure titles and the
assembly caption (the CaseSpec of `case.py` keys the baseline regime `stock`; the pair generator maps
the key to the word where it writes). The intent of each view is written before the cold read, as
the sentence a reader should be able to say after one look.

## assembly_view.png

After one look a reader says: "a configuration is a cubesat of nine parts in eight kinds, drawn as
eight boxes (the solar panel kind is used twice, panelPlus and panelMinus, both named in its box);
two kinds are swapped for one of three alternatives (the wheel class, the tracker grade), four
numbers are tuned within their ranges (panel span, battery capacity, the gains Kp and Kd), and the
three untagged kinds (bus, antenna, IMU) are fixed; that is the whole configuration space".

Changes from round 3 (the dispositions): the legend keeps the two tag keys and the untagged rule
("untagged: fixed (Bus, Antenna, Imu)"); the levels and ranges are the caption's, below (the
generator still reads them from the model and writes them beside the views as
`assembly_caption.md`), so the legend is no longer half the frame. The pair of panels is drawn as
one box, not two: a box is a kind (a `part def`), and the tune `panelSpan` is one variable bound to
both usages, marked once; two boxes would carry the tag twice or on one of two identical boxes.

Caption, the levels and ranges (read from the model by the views generator):

- WheelClass: small | medium | large
- TrackerGrade: coarse | medium | fine
- panelSpan: 0.3 m .. 1 m
- batteryCapacity: 20 W*h .. 200 W*h
- Gains Kp and Kd: one range per regime
- Kp: 0.03 .. 1 (baseline regime); 0.2 .. 2 (primary regime)
- Kd: 0.3 .. 1.5 (both regimes)

## behavioural_view.png

After one look a reader says: "every traded metric, grouped by the analyst into controls, power and
energy, and mass, is an MoE or an MoP (the stereotype in its box) that one requirement reads through
the element named in the metric's box (its calc or its measured attribute; the headless orange line
joins the two); each metric comes from the analysis or from the measured slew (the grey arrow); the
total mass is read through the launch cost, the derived MoE below it, the short line between them
carrying `totalMass`; three requirements read no traded metric, and each says in its own words what
it reads (the available power, the peak momentum against the momentum capacity, the eclipse peak
load against the pack's peak power)".

Changes from round 3 (the dispositions): the chain requirement -> launch cost -> total mass was a
5 px stub between two stacked boxes and was missed cold; the line from the launch cost to the total
mass now carries the element the two share, `totalMass`, and the gap between boxes at one rank is
16 px in the layout (8 in round 3), so the line is about 30 px long with its label beside it; the
requirement's line to the launch-cost box stays unlabelled, because it lands on the box that names
`LaunchCost` and a label between ranks widens the whole layout (measured: 8.6 px of type against
9.2; the two boxes side by side, an extra rank, 7.0 px). The dashed group is titled "requirements
that read no traded metric", in black (its title was body size already, 9.3 px bold, but grey
`#808080`, which read as smaller type).

What the caption carries, moved off the image (the round-1 cold reads: the legend at 6 to 7 px was
illegible, and the boilerplate hid the one difference between the requirement boxes):

- Colour, one rule: orange is a requirement, green a traded metric, yellow a derived quantity that is
  not traded (the launch cost). MoE or MoP is the stereotype in the box, never a colour.
- MoE or MoP, the model's rule (its top comment): an MoE is a measure a stakeholder states without
  knowing the design, an MoP a measure of the system's performance under the scenario the engineer
  states. The pointing error is the one MoE among the traded metrics because the payload's need to
  point is stated before any design; the launch cost is the other MoE, on the total mass, which is
  itself an MoP (the launch mass is the engineer's number, what it costs to launch is the
  stakeholder's).
- Lines: an orange line without a head joins a requirement to the metric it reads, through the
  element named in the metric's box (the quantity is shared, so the line has no direction); a grey
  arrow with a head goes from the source (the analysis def `SlewExperiment`, lilac; the measurement
  `SlewMeasurement`, grey) to the metric it produces. Only the source arrows have heads.
- Requirement boxes: every requirement in the model is a `requirement def` satisfied by
  `cubesatBaseline`, so neither is repeated in a box; a box carries its name and the one thing that
  differs between the requirements, the verification case that verifies it (`ConfigurationVerification`
  reads the configuration alone; `SlewVerification` needs the measured slew). The generator prints
  the satisfying configuration in the boxes again if the model ever makes them differ.
- The three requirements that read no traded metric (top group) carry their `require constraint` in
  the model's words: `c.availablePower > 0.0 [W]` (PowerRequirement), `peakMomentum <=
  c.momentumCapacity` (MomentumRequirement), `EclipsePeakLoad(c, peakTorque) <= BatteryPeakPower(c)`
  (BatteryPeakPowerRequirement).
- The lanes are the analyst's grouping of the metrics (the CaseSpec's subsystems), as their titles
  say; the model has no subsystem structure of its own, and a requirement is placed in the lane of
  the metric it reads.

## heatmap.png (primary regime) and heatmap_baseline.png (baseline regime)

After one look a reader says: "in the primary regime five of the nine metrics contend at every
temperature of the sweep, the total mass most, then the energy balance, the pointing error, the
depth of discharge and the settling time, and three never do (power margin, eclipse reserve, peak
slew power); the control effort fades out by the selected temperature". Each heatmap panel is
titled with its regime ("K = 1 exponent of each metric across tau, primary regime"; the title is
set on the figure `case.tau_sweep_figure` returns, the function itself unchanged); the last column
is the tau = infinity limit. The three white rows are the round's finding, not a defect of the fit:
each of the three is carried by another metric or by the swaps (`WP23-review.md`, "Round 3": the
eclipse reserve and the depth of discharge are one quantity; the power margin and the energy
balance are both affine in the generated power, and in the baseline regime's heatmap the fit
carries the power margin instead; the peak slew power is a step function of the two swaps, flat
inside each cell). The drawing's own defects named by the round-3 cold readers (the colourbar
unnamed, the selected tau not marked on the grid, the total-mass row clipped at the cap of 0.30,
the row order, the top panels' intent, the two axis titles) belong to `case.tau_sweep_figure`,
read-only for WP23, and are listed for the post-gate figure unit in `WP23-review.md`, "Heatmap
redraw, after the gate".
