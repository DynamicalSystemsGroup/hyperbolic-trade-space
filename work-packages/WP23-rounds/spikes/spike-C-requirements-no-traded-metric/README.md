# Spike C: the three requirements that read no traded metric (WP23)

Question (the supervisor on the paper's Figure 3, the behavioural view; verbatim in `decisions.md`): why do
`BatteryPeakPowerRequirement`, `MomentumRequirement` and `PowerRequirement` read no traded metric, and should their
quantities be traded and sit in the existing groups? Read-only on the model, the data, the paper and the docs. Data
of record `examples/adcs/data/v2/` (primary 706, baseline 780 feasible of 1000 each), through `case.py`. Script
`analysis.py` (this folder; `uv run python work-packages/WP23-rounds/spikes/spike-C-requirements-no-traded-metric/analysis.py`
from the repository root); every number below is in `analysis-output.txt`. The model was read from the files
(`cubesat.sysml`, `stakeholders.sysml`), not in the view editor.

**Answer in one paragraph.** Two of the three are already in the trade under another name and one is missing.
The power requirement's quantity, the available power, is one axis with the energy balance (Spearman +0.9996
primary, +0.9997 baseline, above the case's one-axis rule of 0.99): both are affine in the generated power less the
tracker load, so panel span orders both (Spearman +1.00 with span). Added to the fit, it takes the energy balance's
exponent and changes no trade. The battery peak power requirement's quantity is the battery capacity over the eclipse
peak load. It is ordered by the capacity (Spearman +0.97 to +1.00 with `battery_wh`) and is a near-duplicate of the
depth of discharge (+0.970 to +0.997). Added to the fit, it gets exponent 0. Neither requirement fails on any of the
2,000 designs, because the design space's ranges meet both: the model proves it for the power requirement
(`PowerRequirementHoldsInRange`). For the battery peak power requirement the smallest battery's 20 W covers the
largest eclipse peak load, 12 + 7.4 = 19.4 W. The momentum requirement's quantity, the momentum headroom (the wheel's
momentum capacity over the slew's peak momentum), is the one candidate. It is positive on every feasible design,
spread over a factor 18 (primary) and 97 (baseline), a near-duplicate of no traded metric (largest |Spearman| 0.62),
and the fit uses it. It enters the contending set at 0.154 and 0.229, and in the baseline regime it moves the leading
trade: control effort 0.432 to 0.041, total mass 0 to 0.204. So the box is an honest view of the model: it holds two
requirements whose quantities the study already trades under another name and one quantity the study does not trade.

## Where the box comes from

The behavioural view is generated by `docs/superpowers/notes/spike-sysml-diagrams/project.py`. A requirement gets a
line to a metric only where it reads the metric's own calc or measured quantity, or the MoE computed from it. A shared
upstream calc does not count (`PowerMargin` calls `PeakSlewPower`). A requirement with no such line goes into the box
"requirements that read no traded metric" (`no_line`, line 883). The coverage table (`model_v2/coverage.py`) lists the
same requirements under the group of the calc they read: power and battery peak power under power and energy,
momentum under controls.

## (1) The quantity each requirement reads

| requirement | constraint (`cubesat.sysml`) | quantity (larger is better) | where computed | unit | sign on the data |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PowerRequirement | `c.availablePower > 0 W` | available power = generated power − housekeeping 12 W − tracker power | calc `AvailablePower`; oracle column `available_power_w` | W | positive on all 2,000 (11.1 to 69.1 W) |
| MomentumRequirement | `peakMomentum <= c.momentumCapacity` | momentum headroom = capacity / peak momentum (capacities 0.010 / 0.030 / 0.100 N.m.s; the inverse of WP4b's `momentum_utilisation`) | measured by the slew (`peak_momentum_nms`); capacity from the wheel part def | 1 | below 1 on 188 primary and 81 baseline designs (the `momentum_ok` failures); 1.004 and up on the feasible designs |
| BatteryPeakPowerRequirement | `EclipsePeakLoad <= BatteryPeakPower` | battery peak power over eclipse peak load = (1.0 /h × capacity) / (12 W + peak slew power) | calcs `BatteryPeakPower`, `EclipsePeakLoad`; not a flag column, rebuilt from `battery_wh`, `peak_slew_power_w` | 1 | 1.04 and up on all 2,000; never fails |

All three would be MoPs: each measures performance under the engineer's scenario, and no stakeholder states it
without knowing the design. Each also has a difference form (limit minus load, in W or N.m.s). The script reports it
too. The ratio is used here because it is dimensionless and is 1 at the requirement. The momentum difference spans a
factor of 1,500 to 2,400 because the capacity differs tenfold between wheel classes; WP4b amendment 5 chose the ratio
for this reason (`docs/superpowers/notes/adcs-tensions-probe.md`, lever (ii)).

Adding available power swaps one name for another on one axis; the association step (`case.association_all_pairs`)
would name it one axis with energy balance, so the exponent reading sums them. Adding the battery peak power ratio
adds a column the fit gives no weight to. Adding the momentum headroom adds an axis. In the baseline regime the
frontier's leading trade then changes from control effort against settling time to momentum headroom, mass and
settling time. A heavier wheel stores more momentum, so the headroom favours the wheel classes that the total mass
penalises. This trade is the one WP4b amendment 5 looked for. It was not adopted then because lever (i) already made
mass trade ("the headroom attribute is not needed").

The groups become controls 4 (settling time, pointing error, control effort, momentum headroom), power and energy 4,
and mass 1. Nine traded metrics. The behavioural view's box loses `MomentumRequirement`; the other two stay unless
the view is also changed to place them.

## Recommendation per requirement

- **MomentumRequirement: add the momentum headroom as a traded metric (MoP, controls).** It is the only one of the
  three whose quantity is a separate axis of this model. The fit uses it, it reaches the operator's
  `pointingAndSlewing` concern, and without it the study cannot see the wheel's momentum storage, which the model
  states as a requirement.
- **PowerRequirement: keep as a requirement only.** Its quantity is the energy balance's axis (Spearman 0.9996), and
  every in-range configuration meets it (the model's own claim). The study already trades it as the energy balance.
- **BatteryPeakPowerRequirement: keep as a requirement only.** Every in-range configuration meets it (20 W ≥ 19.4 W at
  the range ends), its quantity is set by the battery capacity and tracks the depth of discharge (0.97), and the fit
  gives it exponent 0.
