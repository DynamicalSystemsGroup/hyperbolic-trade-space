Scaling Decision Analysis with Hyperbolic Trade Space Identification
Michael Zargham
IN THIS ARTICLE
Abstract¶

A trade study must produce an answer and a justification an accountable decision-maker can follow. Machine learning techniques can make principled scientific workflows scalable, reproducible and interpretable. For example, principal component analysis (PCA) identifies basis vectors which explain the variance in a dataset. Our method, hyperbolic trade space (HTS), explains trade-offs in a dataset of designs and measurements of their performance on real-valued attributes. Trade-offs trace piecewise hyperbolic frontiers: in order to get more of one objective, we must tolerate less of another. When supplied with a dataset we first condition the attributes into metrics that are strictly positive and “all else being equal, more is better.” We do not assume the design space is well behaved, as it is often a mix of categorical and numerical values, on different scales and with different units. While we apply conditioning to ensure the solvers behave in practice, we do not make strong assumptions about the sampling distribution, only that the frontier is log-convex. To discover trade-offs among many designs and many metrics, we define the family of hyperbolic trade spaces by the level sets of a posynomial function of the metrics. The monomial case is a convex optimization problem, and the posynomial case is compatible with generalized geometric programming. Like PCA, HTS is an unsupervised learning method: PCA fits a subspace and projects the data onto it; HTS fits a surface one dimension below the data and places each design by its position on it and its level above or below that surface. By assuming this hyperbolic shape and solving for the “energy” level sets, the resulting fit describes the trade-off space. Expanding on the concept of a dilemma or trilemma, a monomial term in n metrics characterizes an n-lemma (“nye-LEM-uh”). Pareto improvements increase energy, and a design that maximizes energy is Pareto-efficient. White space within an occupied level set may be an under-explored region of the design space. The paper provides a literature review, formal results and a worked example to demonstrate HTS’s applicability to decision analysis. Our proofs are scripted with SymPy, and HTS is implemented with a scikit-learn-compatible API, so it integrates with existing pipelines and tools. The worked example is a cubesat design optimization problem. The underlying design problem is characterized by a SysML v2 model, and a simulation study scores each design on thirteen metrics. High-dimensional n-lemmas are detected and used to inform a design decision by stakeholders who care primarily about cost and performance, without losing the thread back to the source designs. A point of the trade space pulls back to the design that produced it; a point without data is a conjecture about a design, proposed by a rule and settled by building and measuring it. Future work has three thrusts: (1) searching the design space for designs that push the frontier outward, (2) finding and filling the holes within it by topological data analysis, and (3) formal verification and performance, moving beyond Python into Rust and using theorem provers.

1The decision problem¶

Decision analysis has two products: an answer, and a justification an accountable executive can follow and defend. It seeks responsible choice among conflicting objectives (the measures the decision-maker wants more of) Keeney & Raiffa, 1993 and clear conversation with the decision-maker Howard, 1988. The justification is the hard product: the process turning much data into little must be recognized, rigorous and reproducible.

A trade study is a document: a grid, a table, a ranking by weighted criteria; surveys compare them Baker & Whalen, 2012. The weights are human judgments, susceptible to bias in trade studies Smith et al., 2007Chilli et al., 2026. Systems engineering is moving from document-based Henderson & Salado, 2021 to model-based practice Madni & Sievers, 2018. But the computational alternatives are bespoke and opaque. To displace the document, a method must handle real data’s scale, complexity and noise while remaining interpretable.

Techniques from machine learning such as feature engineering and dimensionality reduction provide interpretability at scale. For example, principal component analysis (PCA) identifies basis vectors which explain the variance in a dataset Jolliffe & Cadima, 2016. However, identifying how linearly independent vectors explain variance tells a decision-maker very little to judge a trade-off.

Objectives may align in one region and oppose in another. The fitted reference surface, a posynomial fitted to the data underlying the table, gives them a coordinate system. The chart is a reversible rewriting of the metrics as a position along the surface and a level relative to it. Each monomial term, a piece of the surface and not a set of designs, names the metrics that contend on it with elasticities: an n-lemma (by analogy with dilemma and trilemma). The workflow has two uses: design optimization, the setting of multidisciplinary design analysis and optimization Martins & Lambe, 2013Martins & Ning, 2021, and configuration management, where a system in service is changed in response to a changed context Fulcoly et al., 2012. In both, a configuration is evaluated into attributes whose trade-offs the fit reads. Our contribution is interpretability at scale, and reuse of the decision analysis pipeline via unsupervised learning.

The paper proceeds as follows. Section 2 places the method among the families it borrows from, Pareto methods, data envelopment analysis, geometric programming and PCA. Section 3 fixes notation and Section 4 defines the objects: the invariant value, the reference surface, the chart, the term and the n-lemma. Section 5 states the conditions of the theory and the fitting procedure, and Section 6 gives the results: the one-term fit as a convex program with elasticities and a reversible chart, Pareto efficiency of a maximizer, the several-term fit with its levels of guarantee, and the open questions, with scripted proofs. Section 7 builds the cubesat case from its SysML v2 model and Section 8 reads it: what contends, with what elasticities, where designs sit, and the decision the executives make. Section 9 discusses interpretation and limits, Section 10 gives future work, and Section 11 closes with the use of AI assistance and the reproduction of every result.

2Prior art¶

A trade study asks which objectives trade and ends in a choice that can be defended; decision analysis Howard, 1988Keeney & Raiffa, 1993Parnell, 2016, model-based systems engineering Madni & Sievers, 2018, multidisciplinary design analysis and optimization Martins & Lambe, 2013 and multiobjective optimization Miettinen, 1999 are the forms that question takes. Answering it at the scale of a generated table calls for a recognized, rigorous and reproducible technique, which is what unsupervised learning offers: dimensionality reduction and kernel methods Jolliffe & Cadima, 2016Schölkopf et al., 1998 reduce a table to a structure a person can read, and data envelopment analysis Charnes et al., 1978 scores each design against the edge of the table. A method that finds trade-offs must then be fitted, and the convergence and performance of the fit rest on convex optimization and generalized geometric programming Boyd et al., 2007Hoburg & Abbeel, 2014. Pareto methods. Pareto methods return a nondominated set and find its members by scalarization, for example the weighting method Miettinen, 1999Ehrgott, 2005; evolutionary algorithms generate such sets Zitzler & Thiele, 1999Deb et al., 2002. A nondominated set says which designs survive. The fit says what trades against what. Dominance is a hard comparison that stops discriminating, on this case, as metrics are added. The two meet in one result: a design that maximizes the fitted value is Pareto-efficient (Theorem 10).

Data envelopment analysis. DEA is the closer relative Charnes et al., 1978Banker et al., 1984. It scores each unit against the data by mathematical programming, with weights obtained from the data rather than set in advance Banker et al., 1984. We fit one weight vector for the whole table, or one per piece of the surface, where DEA obtains weights unit by unit. Our surface is piecewise log-linear, and our boundary is a softened maximum rather than one set by extreme units.

HoroPCA. HoroPCA Chami et al., 2021 is the closest existing analog in setting: unsupervised, with a table in and coordinates out. It is the farthest in what is estimated: it extends the components of PCA to hyperbolic space with horospherical projections, maximizing a variance. This work fits the one-sided edge of a table and is not a PCA of any kind. We borrow that setting and the estimator interface of scikit-learn, and use PCA as a downstream step (Appendix B.11). In a prospectively specified test, on an embedding of ours, the authors’ code, at its defaults and one direction at a time, recovered the planted trade-offs only where they were the direction of largest spread; the tables are clean, so it demonstrates a mechanism and does not measure how often recovery succeeds (Appendix C).

Lineage. The weighted geometric mean with exponents summing to one has the constant-returns Cobb-Douglas form Cobb & Douglas, 1928. Reading the fitted value as the factor that scales a design to the boundary is our distance-function reading, after Shephard’s characterization of a technology by its distance function Shephard, 1970Färe & Primont, 1995Farrell, 1957. The family of one-sided fits, frontier estimation by programming, corrected least squares and one-sided error, is reviewed in Battese, 1992 after Aigner & Chu, 1968Afriat, 1972Aigner et al., 1977. Our coordinates are in the spirit of log-ratio coordinates for compositional data Aitchison, 1983Egozcue et al., 2003. The model class and refit scheme come from geometric programming and max-affine fitting Boyd et al., 2007Hoburg et al., 2016Magnani & Boyd, 2009; the soft maximum is an entropic risk functional Föllmer, n.d.. Kernel PCA is the feature-engineering relative of the kernel direction in Section 9 Schölkopf et al., 1998.

The stopping declaration. Each returned fit carries the levels it meets (Appendix B.4). For a sharp fit, certified means that the assignment is fixed, the residuals and the term gaps are within tolerance, no cell is empty and the margin is positive, so Corollary 2 bounds the remaining improvement with the cells held fixed and Proposition 3 applies to the exact minimizers the fit approximates. For a soft fit, certified means that the second-order test holds (Theorem 13), and stationary that the term gaps hold and the Hessian is not positive definite on the tangent space of the active face. Certified is a status at the returned point, set by tolerances, and the returned point is polished: at finite 
𝜏
τ each term’s refit is followed by Newton steps that close the gap the solver leaves (Appendix E.2); Proposition 3 and Theorem 13 hold at the exact point that the fit approximates. Whether a fit is stationary within tolerance, and so its status, is measured, not proved.

On the primary regime the fits of record are sharp; the solver declares the status certified at K = 2 and 3, with the largest term gap below 1e-12.

On the cubesat case of Section 7 (primary regime, 2,134 feasible designs), at K = 2 the margin, a gap between term scores, is 0.0003768, against a band 1/tau of 0.0088 and a median best-minus-second-best score of 0.352: a margin below the band puts at least one design within the band of a seam, here 37 of the 2,134. For the two-term split: the local optimality certified covers changes of the term scores under half the margin, about 47 times smaller than the band, so which term those 37 designs fall to is not a finding; the contending sets themselves hold under rounding-level jitter (Appendix B.7). In the parameters the same condition is the stable radius, 2.552e-05: the certificate covers moves of each term's exponents and reach under it.

Appendix G records what the results leave open.

8Case analysis¶

Of 2,134 feasible cubesat designs, the nondominated set (on the conditioned metrics, larger is better) holds 236 on 4 attributes (settling time, pointing error, control effort and total mass) and 2,134 on all 13 (Appendix B.5; counts that hold over five regenerations of the data under rounding-level perturbation; a number that moves over them is printed as a range, Appendix B.7).

Nondominance keeps every feasible design on the thirteen metrics and so does not narrow the choice; the fit reads what contends, at what rates, and where each design sits (Appendix B holds the tables, B.7 the digits that hold).

One term. At K = 1 the fit reports one trade-off at the balanced point (Figure 7).

At K = 1 the exact-maximum fit has a 7-lemma across controls, power and energy, plus cost: unit cost, available power, pointing error, fleet cost, momentum headroom, settling time and depth of discharge contend, with exponents at the fit temperature from 0.04 to 0.26. At the fit temperature total mass also carries 0.02 and is not in the contending set, which is the exact maximum's support (Definition 6). The exact-maximum optimum is one point, checked by a test of the repository on both regimes, so the support is not a solver's choice.

Figure 7:Log-metric plane of the K = 1 fit (primary regime) in its two heaviest metrics, available power and unit cost; the slope of the line is their elasticity, the ratio of their exponents. The line is the surface, V = 1, in this plane. Each design is moved vertically by what its other eleven metrics add, weighed by their exponents, so that the line is the surface for every design: the distance below it is the design’s level, log V divided by the unit-cost exponent, and a tenth of all designs lie above it, by the fit’s convention.

Two terms. The package's checks for choosing K (Appendix B.10) point to K = 2 on the primary regime and the baseline regime, none deciding alone: the mean slack (in log V), read at the K = 1 temperature, drops most at K = 2 (0.08 and 0.05) and less at K = 3 (0.02 and 0.03), the largest drop read against the other two checks. The thinnest term holds 0.26 and 0.25 of the near-frontier designs at K = 2 and 0.06 and 0.06 at K = 3, the step where a thin term appears.

At K = 2 the trade-off separates into two terms with different contending sets (Figure 8 and Figure 9); the margin, gap and status of this fit are in Section 6 and Appendix B.4. Where the answer must be known it is planted, with the generator of Appendix F.7.

The fit recovers all three terms on 16 of 20 planted tables of 400 rows, 4 metrics and 3 terms each (Appendix B.9).

Two regimes. The same pipeline on the baseline regime, whose gains were sampled over a lower range, separates the problem’s contribution from the gains’ (Appendix B.2).

The regimes return a 7-lemma and an 8-lemma sharing 7 metrics (available power, depth of discharge, fleet cost, momentum headroom, pointing error, settling time and unit cost); the primary set lies inside the baseline set, which adds total mass. The baseline set spans controls, power and energy, mass, plus cost: mass enters only in the baseline regime.

Propose, predict, check. A regressor from configuration to chart coordinates predicts where proposals in the white space of the four-metric chart fall, and the proposals are then measured (Appendix B.6).

Of 3 proposals, the predicted energy differential lies within the regressor's own band 1/tau = 0.0935 (its four-metric fit's, not the case's) of the measured one for 3, and the predicted position within that band for 3; position errors run from 0.2 to 0.8 bands.

The proposals fail the depth-of-discharge feasibility condition: they reached the four-metric fitted reference surface by spending the battery.

Requirements after the fit. Fitting first and reading the requirements after is the unconstrained-then-constrained reading of a trade study.

Applied after the K = 1 fit as constraints on the solution space, the six requirements exclude 178 of its 238 near-frontier designs jointly; one at a time: peak slew power 108, control effort 95, unit cost 64, fleet cost 57, total mass 12 and pointing error 8 (Appendix B.12).

With every design set conditioned as the fit of record is, removing the infeasible designs or imposing the four performance thresholds before the fit leaves the one-term contending set of the primary regime (seven metrics) unchanged and changes that of the baseline regime by at most two of its eight metrics (Appendix B.12).

Three stakeholders. The decision gives each of three objectives to one stakeholder: the fleet cost to the sponsor, the unit cost to the manufacturer and the coverage probability to the operator, the probability that at least one satellite of the fleet covers a target in the planning period. The SysML v2 model defines it and its two constants (CoverageProbability), and a script of the case compares it with two other measures. The three objectives were not guaranteed to collapse to one trade-off. On this case they do (Figure 10): fleet cost is unit cost times fleet size, so the sponsor’s objective rides on the manufacturer’s and the fleet size, and the fit names the dilemma between fleet cost and coverage probability, with unit cost nearly flat.

On all 2,134 feasible designs fleet cost over unit cost times fleet size is one to within 6e-09. The two-term refit puts on fleet cost and coverage probability 0.14 and 0.86 in term 1 and 0.54 and 0.46 in term 2, and 0.000 on unit cost in both, so both terms lie on the one-term fit's edge.

Whether a manufacturing cost that scales differently with the fleet size pulls designs toward the middle of the triangle is checked by refitting: such a revision of the cost model enters as a new table, and the same calls test whether the collapse holds.

The decision. The requirements and the fits cut 2,134 feasible designs to a shortlist of 28 that pass all six requirements and lie in the near-frontier band of both fits (Appendix B.14). On it the manufacturer and the operator pick the same fleet: 25 medium-wheel, medium-tracker satellites at 234,000 USD each, 5.85 M USD for the fleet and a coverage probability of 0.98. The unit cost falls and the coverage probability rises with the fleet size. The sponsor picks 4 small-wheel, medium-tracker satellites at 1.13 M USD, with a coverage probability of 0.46. What remains is the question the fit names, and it is the executives': how much fleet cost a gain in coverage probability is worth. Of the 17 shortlisted designs that no other beats on both, the fleet of 10 small-wheel, medium-tracker satellites at 2.88 M USD, with a coverage probability of 0.76, has the smallest larger shortfall on the two, each scaled from its best to its worst on the shortlist.

9Discussion¶

Interpretation is human validation. The fit reports what contends; whether that is an explicable trade-off is for an engineer to decide. Machine verification shows that a formula follows from its hypotheses and that the code agrees; it does not show that the hypotheses suit a table. That is human validation, which remains for a human: the conditioning, the choice of K and the reading of every finding. On the case, the reading that the fleet’s three objectives reduce to one trade-off was not taken from the figure: it was traced to the SysML v2 model’s definition of the fleet cost and checked on every row of the data.

What the case showed. The contribution claimed in Section 1 is interpretability at scale, and reuse of the decision analysis pipeline via unsupervised learning. On the case, a table that nondominance leaves whole was read as two terms with their contending metrics, a tension matrix per term and a level for every design. The requirements, applied after the fit, cut it to a shortlist on which each stakeholder’s pick and the one question left to the executives can be stated. The pipeline was reused rather than rebuilt: the second regime and the fleet’s three objectives went through the same calls, and so would a revised SysML v2 model or attributes measured on a fielded system, because the evaluation is a black box to the method.

The three layers. The case used each layer of Section 2. The decision problem is the SysML v2 model’s requirements and measures of effectiveness, with one stakeholder per objective of the decision. The unsupervised learning is the fit, which reduced the table to terms and levels without a weight elicited from anyone. The solver is the convex program of the one-term fit and the alternation of the several-term fit, whose guarantees Section 6 states.

Limits. The fitted reference surface is log-convex, and one term is tangent at one place and conservative off it. The exponents and elasticities are features of the conditioned table: they depend on the conditioning, describe how the sampled designs trade and not a causal exchange that changing a variable would reproduce, and a small sample determines them weakly. Designed samples, such as grids, are not independent observations: the exponents describe the sample and are not estimates with a sampling error. The certificate is local, and that the fit holds for designs outside the table, or supports a decision, is a further claim that needs its own validation. The case’s hardware values are illustrative and its costs a lower bound on a full costing; the case has no schedule, no payload and a flat launch price, and the coverage probability’s two constants are mission assumptions the operator supplies (Appendix G). Trade-offs the sampling never exercised cannot appear, and white space in the chart is an opportunity only where the trade space is log-convex around it, which the data cannot prove.

10Future work¶

This release delivers the method as a scikit-learn-compatible estimator, the theory of the one-term fit with scripted proofs, the several-term fit with its guarantees, the cubesat case carried to a decision, and the method’s record on three public datasets.

Searching the design space. A chart point without a datum pulls back to a proposal: a configuration chosen by a selection rule, a conjecture until it is built and measured. Ascent directions chained back through the conditioning and the evaluation steer the proposals, and an outer loop evaluates them, appends the rows and refits. The next release scopes the proposal and its check as functions of the regressor.

Holes. Persistent homology on the chart’s distances would name candidate holes, regions the sample encloses and no design occupies, each a hypothesis until a design is sampled there; a spike located and settled a planted one. The next release builds the definition as code on planted tables, with the choice of topology library left open.

Formal verification and performance. The Lean theorem prover would prove the log-mean-exp bounds and the convexity of the loss over the reals, which backs the mathematics and not the floating-point computation. A Rust core compiled to WebAssembly would serve performance, its verification tools addressing crashes and overflow, not numerical accuracy. Governance checks written as requirements with verification cases would gate each release.
