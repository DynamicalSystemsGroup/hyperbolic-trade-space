## What each version covers

- **2.0 and 2.1** (https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/0/code_of_conduct/ and
  https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/): seven sections: Our Pledge, Our Standards,
  Enforcement Responsibilities, Scope, Enforcement, Enforcement Guidelines, Attribution. The standards are conduct between
  people: empathy, respect for differing views, accepting constructive feedback, taking responsibility for mistakes;
  unacceptable are sexualised language, trolling and insults, harassment, publishing others' private information. The
  enforcement guidelines are a four-step ladder: Correction, Warning, Temporary Ban, Permanent Ban, "inspired by"
  Mozilla's ladder (Attribution section). Scope: all community spaces, and when someone officially represents the
  community. The reporting contact is an unfilled placeholder: the text is a template the adopter completes.
- **3.0** (https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/3/0/code_of_conduct/): Our Pledge; Encouraged Behaviors (seven);
  Restricted Behaviors (seven) plus "Other Restrictions"; Reporting an Issue; Addressing and Repairing Harm (four rungs:
  Warning, Temporarily Limited Activities, Temporary Suspension, Permanent Ban; lower rungs may be skipped for
  serious violations); Scope; Attribution. Enforcement is by "Community Moderators", generally private. Reporting method
  is again a placeholder for the adopter.

## The two lines in 3.0 nearest to how work is produced

Under "Other Restrictions" the 3.0 page lists, verbatim headings with their sentences:

- "Misleading identity. Impersonating someone else for any reason, or pretending to be someone else to evade enforcement
  actions."
- "Failing to credit sources. Not properly crediting the sources of content you contribute."

These are the only places where the text touches the provenance of a contribution. The first is about people, in the
sense of impersonation and evading enforcement; it does not say who may be named as the author of a change. The second
is a conduct rule on credit, not a licence rule and not a rule about tools. Neither mentions AI, models or tools (the
OES announcement and the 2.1 page, read, do not either).

## Licence and attribution terms

- 3.0: "Contributor Covenant is stewarded by the Organization for Ethical Source and licensed under CC BY-SA 4.0"; an
  adopter writes "adapted from the Contributor Covenant, version 3.0" with the permanent URL
  (https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/3/0/code_of_conduct/). CC BY-SA 4.0 is attribution plus share-alike:
  an adapted text is shared under the same licence.
- 2.0 and 2.1: the pages read state an attribution line ("adapted from the Contributor Covenant, version 2.1") and
  state no licence for the text. The repository's `LICENSE.md` is the Hippocratic License 3.0 (October 2021)
  (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/EthicalSource/contributor_covenant/release/LICENSE.md); the site footer says its
  source code is "ethically licensed". The pages read do not say which licence covers the 2.x text; a use of 2.x would
  need that checked at the source before adoption.

## What it covers and what it does not

Covers: conduct between people in a community's spaces; who enforces, how, and with what ladder; scope of places and
representation. Does not cover (nothing in the pages read addresses): how the work itself is produced; who may be the
named author of a change; tools, models or agents; provenance and licensing of contributed content beyond the one
"credit sources" line; the review standard for a change. This is the gap the readout's options differ on.

## Adoption in a project this repository's readers know

The Linux kernel documentation carries both "Contributor Covenant Code of Conduct" and a "Linux Kernel Contributor
Covenant Code of Conduct Interpretation" page in its policy guides, beside its tool-generated-content guidelines
(https://docs.kernel.org/process/coding-assistants.html, sidebar). The kernel therefore keeps conduct (the Covenant,
with an interpretation) and the rules for AI-assisted work in separate documents, which is option A's shape in the
readout.
