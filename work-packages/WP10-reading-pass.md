## Absence claims

Sentences that assert something about what the literature does not contain, or about what a family does not do. For each: what a reader would need to check it.

1. Section 1: "the computational alternatives we meet are bespoke and opaque, which is why the document is sticky" (uncited). Check: a survey of computational trade-study tools (for example the Parnell 2021 survey of trade-off analytics, `parnell2021system`, whose abstract only was seen by the project) for any method that is generic, reproducible and interpretable; and evidence on why documents persist.
2. Section 1: "variance is analysis. It says where the data are free and nothing about trade-offs" (about PCA, uncited after the Jolliffe cite). Check: Jolliffe 2016 body and a treatment of PCA on positive data or of trade-off reading of loadings.
3. Section 1: "Today most trade studies are documents, grids and tables" (abstract of the paper). Check: an empirical study of trade-study format. The notes record that none was found (`paper-prior-art-sources.md`, 'Not found').
4. Section 2: "We claim no priority." and "[Appendix G] is not exhaustive". Check: nothing beyond the register; the claim is a disclaimer.
5. Pareto paragraph: "A nondominated set says which designs survive. The fit says what trades against what." Implies Pareto methods do not report trade-off structure. Check: the multiobjective literature on trade-off analysis and sensitivity of Pareto sets (Miettinen part on trade-offs; Ehrgott on efficiency), which the contents pages suggest includes trade-off concepts.
6. Pareto paragraph: "Dominance is a hard comparison that stops discriminating as the number of metrics grows" (uncited). Check: the many-objective optimization literature on dominance resistance (nondominated fraction rising with the number of objectives); the paper's own counts (671 of 706 on ten attributes) are evidence for this data set only.
7. Pareto table row `differs`: "Returns no coordinate system and no exponents". Check: scalarization and decision-support literature for coordinates or weights recovered from a front (for example inverse optimization, trade-off rates in Miettinen part I).
8. DEA paragraph: "We differ in what the envelope is ... a global named structure, where each unit in DEA may choose favourable weights". Check: the multiplier form of the CCR model and the peer-set/dual forms in Charnes et al. 1978 and Banker et al. 1984 (full text), and whether global-weight formulations exist (common-weights DEA). Rows 20 and 21 show the abstracts do not state it.
9. DEA paragraph: "We solve one convex program and K refits, not N linear programs". Check: DEA formulations that avoid one LP per unit (for example frontier-based or convex-hull formulations), and the paper's timing evidence.
10. DEA paragraph: "Our boundary is a soft maximum ... not a boundary set by extreme units". Check: stochastic and bootstrap DEA, and super-efficiency or order-m variants (Andersen and Petersen full text), which soften extreme units.
11. DEA table `shares`/`differs`: "coordinates and a pull-back to attribute units in place of a score and a peer set". Check: DEA visualization or projection literature (not cited).
12. Lineage and table, GP row `differs`: "The sources fit regression surfaces; this method fits an envelope". Check: Hoburg 2016 and Magnani and Boyd 2009 bodies for one-sided or bounding fits (Boyd et al. section 8 also treats bounding approximations).
13. Frontier-estimation `differs`: "A smooth soft maximum over a simplex-constrained geometric mean in place of a shifted intercept". Check: Battese 1992 and the frontier literature for smooth or entropic one-sided criteria.
14. Tradespace table `differs`: "These explore and display; this method fits and reports a coordinate system and a score." Check: Ross 2005, Ross 2010 and Spero 2014 bodies for any fitted structure or ranking score over the tradespace (Ross 2004 uses multi-attribute utility, a score).
15. Logratio `differs`: "The criterion is one-sided, and the weights lie on the simplex; the chart is paired with the level log V and is reversible". Check: Aitchison 1983 and Egozcue 2003 bodies (not accessible here).
16. Lineage, last two sentences: "Combining these pieces in one estimator is our design. The sources establish the pieces, not the combination." Check: a structured search (Scopus, Google Scholar, arXiv) for fitted posynomial or geometric-mean envelopes with simplex exponents read as exchange rates; the project's own list is in `docs/literature.md` ('What is the supervisor's combination'). A reading of the cited abstracts alone cannot establish absence.
17. Intro: "Contribution. Scaling up trade studies with a reproducible computational method that has a PCA-like feel." and the sentence that the n-lemma is "our analogue of a principal component". Check: not a literature claim; listed because it sits beside the PCA row.
