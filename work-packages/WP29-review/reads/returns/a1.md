# a1

## 1. What the Corollary 1 box claims

1. Suppose the conditioned (log) metrics carry Gaussian noise with covariance Sigma. Then the expected noisy objective is the clean objective J_tau plus a quadratic penalty (tau/2) w'Sigma w. I take this to be Proposition 2 applied with a general covariance. Proposition 2 as printed has only the scalar variance sigma^2.
2. The minimiser w therefore moves. To first order the shift is Delta w = -(PCP)^+ P Sigma w. When Sigma = sigma^2 I it is -sigma^2 (PCP)^+ P w.
3. C is the softmax-weighted covariance of the log-metrics at the clean fit. P is the orthogonal projector onto the tangent space of the active face.
4. If noise is specified as relative error delta_j on attribute j, it enters as sigma_j = delta_j / s_j. Here s_j is the log spread of attribute j, defined in L18, which I do not have. I read this as a diagonal Sigma with those entries.
5. The shift satisfies ||Delta w|| <= tau ||P Sigma w|| / conditioning_, where conditioning_ is some named quantity I cannot see.
6. The shift lowers ||w||^2.
7. The shift says how far noise moves the exponents. It does not say whether the rates are right.

The sketch derives the shift in one step. The noisy objective is J_tau plus the ridge. Its Hessian at the fit is tau C. Its gradient on the face vanishes at the clean fit. Linearising the first-order condition gives tau C Delta w + tau P Sigma w = 0 on the tangent space. This is the implicit-function / Newton step, and the tau cancels.

## 2. What it does not claim

- It does not claim consistency or unbiasedness of any estimator. The box says so itself ("not whether the rates are right"). The surrounding Proposition 2 line says "a population identity, with no consistency claim".
- It does not give a finite-noise or second-order result. "Noise small, the result first order" is a hypothesis, with no radius of validity.
- It does not give a corrected estimator. It does not say whether to subtract Delta w from a fitted w, or evaluate at the noisy fit or at the clean one.
- It does not treat the active face changing. R3 (interior to the face) rules that out for small noise, but nothing says how small is small.
- It does not say how Sigma is estimated.
- It does not claim noise-free-fit exactness when Sigma is not proportional to the identity beyond the displayed formula.
- I infer that finite-sample fitting error is not addressed. The statement is about the population minimiser of an expected objective.
- I infer that it says nothing about noise in the attributes the model is fitted on versus noise at prediction time. The box says only "noise on the conditioned metrics".

## 3. What a reader with a table and a fitted model would do

1. Condition the table into strictly positive metrics and take logs (u). Fit w (tau, normalisation and face structure from elsewhere in the paper).
2. Decide Sigma. If measurement errors are quoted as relative errors delta_j per attribute, compute s_j (L18) and set sigma_j = delta_j / s_j. Diagonal Sigma is presumably what this gives. Correlated errors need their own Sigma.
3. At the fit compute the softmax weights p_i proportional to exp(tau w.u_i), and C = Cov_p(u).
4. Identify which constraints on w are active and build P, the projector onto the face's tangent space.
5. Compute Delta w = -(PCP)^+ P Sigma w. Compare ||Delta w|| to the bound, and to ||w||.
6. Interpret: how far the exponents would move under this noise.

What the box does not tell them:
- What J_tau is (the objective, its sign convention, minimised or maximised) and what tau is.
- What the "active face" is and where the constraints on w come from.
- What conditioning_ is (a fitted attribute? a smallest eigenvalue? of what?).
- Whether the noise is already in the table being fitted. If so, the fitted w is the noisy minimiser, and the correction would be w_clean ~ w_noisy - Delta w, evaluated at the noisy fit.
- The units: natural log, and what "relative error" means (standard deviation over value?).
- What "small" means numerically, for example Sigma against the smallest eigenvalue of PCP.
- Whether the noise is independent across rows of the table. The expectation step needs independence of noise and data.

## 4. Are the hypotheses enough?

For the displayed formula, I think yes at first order, given what I infer. The pieces are: the expected objective is J_tau plus a quadratic. J_tau is smooth. The Hessian of a log-mean-exp in w is tau times a weighted covariance, which is the standard identity. The gradient on the face vanishes at the clean fit. R3 makes PCP invertible on the tangent space, so the pseudoinverse acts as an inverse there. Linearisation then gives Delta w. The ridge's own Hessian tau Sigma is dropped, which is consistent with "first order".

Steps I could not close:
- **The ridge for general Sigma.** Proposition 2 as printed gives only sigma^2 ||w||^2 / 2, with "independent sum" as its mechanism. The corollary needs w'Sigma w / 2 for a general covariance. For a Gaussian vector this is immediate, and independence is not needed. But it is not what Proposition 2 states, and A5 "or" A9 may cover it. I cannot check that from the material given.
- **Expectation versus log.** The identity is for (1/tau) log E exp(...), the log of an expectation. A noisy objective that is the expected log-mean-exp over a sample would differ. "Expected noisy objective is J_tau plus the ridge" needs the objective to be defined through the expectation of the exponentials. I cannot see that it is.
- **The bound.** If the Hessian is tau C and the forcing is tau P Sigma w, the tau cancels. The bound would then be ||P Sigma w|| / lambda_min(PCP), with no tau. The printed bound has a tau in the numerator. That is true only if conditioning_ already absorbs a factor of tau (conditioning_ = tau lambda_min?), or if J_tau differs from my guess. I could not reconcile it. I also cannot tell where Cauchy-Schwarz (I9) enters. Presumably in bounding the pseudoinverse action, but that is a guess.
- **"Lowers ||w||^2".** In the equal case, w.Delta w = -sigma^2 (Pw)'(PCP)^+(Pw) <= 0, so the first-order change of ||w||^2 is non-positive. That works even when Pw differs from w. For general Sigma, w'(PCP)^+ P Sigma w has no definite sign unless Sigma commutes suitably. So the claim as printed is closed for the equal case and not for general Sigma. Also "lowers" is first order, and non-strict when Pw = 0.
- **Smallness.** No quantitative threshold is given, so "first order" is a convention rather than a verifiable condition.
