I read the text section by section and opened each picture when the text reached its box. I cannot click links in the pictures, so "followed" below means I found the numbered box the sentence named and checked whether it was the one the sentence needed. ## 1. The thread, box to box

**Definition 1 (Three objects).** Three meanings of "frontier": the fitted surface V = 1 (finite τ), the supporting envelope (τ = ∞, nothing exceeds it), and the Pareto boundary (nondominated set of the attainable set). It hangs from nothing before it. V is not yet defined, so "the posynomial equals one" is a promise.

**Definition 2 (Invariant form).** V(m) = e^{-β} ∏ m_j^{w_j}, with log V = w·u − β. It takes β from Definition 7, which is forward. I could follow the formula but not where w and β come from until eq. (1) in Section 5.

**Definition 3 (Posynomial).** Several terms: V_K is a power of a sum of monomials, Φ_K is its log, and a term's "cell" is the designs where it is largest. It hangs from Definition 2 as "a sum of such terms". It introduces κ, which appeared earlier only as a name in A6.

**Definition 4 (Temperatures and band).** τ is the hardness of the maximum over designs and κ over terms. The near-frontier band is |log V| < 1/τ. It hangs from Definition 3 (κ) and from a τ that is only defined properly in eq. (1).

**Definition 5 (The chart).** Z = Bu with BBᵀ = I and B1 = 0, and the pair (Z, log V) is "a reversible rewriting". It hangs from Definition 2 for log V. B is not defined as a matrix (size, how it is built).

**Definition 6 (The n-lemma).** The contending set of term k is the support of the τ = ∞ fit to the term's cell. The thread frayed here and again after Theorem 10; see §5.

**Figure 1.** This is where Definition 6 became clear to me. The triangle of exponent vectors has corners (one metric), edges (a dilemma) and an interior (a trilemma), with the fitted point drawn next to the planted one. The caption's Shows / Does not show / Good for lines were useful.

**Definitions 7, 8, 9 (Reach and share; Elasticity; Ascent direction).**
- 7 says β_k is the soft maximum of scores, and that term share is the fraction of near-frontier designs in a cell.
- 8 says d log m_j / d log m_i = −w_ki/w_kj.
- 9 says the tangent space {v : w_k·v = 0} is "constant V" and w_k is the gradient of log V.

All three are readable at calculus level. I found no later box that uses Definition 9, or term share, in Sections 3 to 6.

**Section 5 (conditions R1, R2; eq. (1)).** Eq. (1) gives J_τ(w) as a log-mean-exp of scores, and w* minimises it over the simplex. I could follow this. R1, placed before eq. (1), I could not (see §5 stops).

**Theorem 1 (Homogeneity, L1).** V(km) = kV(m), because the exponents sum to one. It hangs from Definition 2 and A1/A2. Easy.

**Proposition 1 (Uniform shift, L2).** Adding c to every log-metric shifts J_τ by c and leaves w* alone. It hangs from Theorem 1's idea (weights sum to one). Easy.

**Theorem 2 (Gradient and KKT, L3).** ∇J = Uᵀp with p = softmax(τUw), and at a minimiser g_j ≥ λ with w_j(g_j − λ) = 0. I read this as "where w_j > 0, g_j is at its smallest". I had to work out for myself that λ is then min g. It hangs from eq. (1).

**Theorem 3 (Convexity, L4).** The second derivative along a line is τ times a p-weighted variance, so J_τ is convex. It hangs from Theorem 2 (the p). I could follow the sketch.

**Theorem 4 (LME against LSE, L5).** The sum-version differs from the mean-version by (log N)/τ, and the soft maximum sits between the mean and the maximum. It hangs from eq. (1). LSE_τ and LME_τ are used as symbols without a definition.

**Proposition 2 (Gaussian cumulant, L6).** (1/τ) log E e^{τ w·ε} = τσ²‖w‖²/2. It is a self-contained calculus identity, and I could follow it. Its link to the fit comes only through Corollary 1.

**Corollary 1 (Noise moves the exponents by a ridge, L20).** Noise adds ½τ wᵀΣw to J_τ and shifts the exponents by Δw = −(PCP)⁺PΣw. It hangs from Proposition 2, Theorem 3 (Hessian τC) and Theorem 2 (gradient on the face), per Proof 7. The thread broke here (see §5). I did not pick it up again.

**Theorem 5 (Elasticities and improvement factors, L7).** On the hyperplane w·u = β the slope is −w_i/w_j. Improving metric j alone by V^{-1/w_j}, or every metric by 1/V, reaches V = 1. It hangs from Definition 8, which is the same formula. Easy to read. The hypothesis "the frontier is the hyperplane" is an assumption, and only the sketch says tying it to the fit needs Theorem 2's hypothesis.

**Theorem 6 (The chart, L8).** Z is unchanged by shifts, and u ↔ (Z, mean-log-scale) is a bijection through an orthogonal Q. It hangs from Definition 5. Definition 5 names the chart as (Z, log V), but this box proves it for (Z, 1ᵀu/√d). I had to do the step myself (log V = (Bw)·Z + 1ᵀu/d − β, using w·1 = 1) to connect them.

**Theorem 7 (Pull-back, L9).** Substitute log m_j = α_j log(a_j + s_j) + b_j into log V. It hangs from Definition 2 and A4. Proof 10's "the offset enters only through Σ w_j = 1" confused me, because the visible effect is w·b.

**Theorem 8 (Posynomial extension, L10).** Φ_K shifts by c, and max_k x_k ≤ Φ_K ≤ max_k x_k + (log K)/κ. It hangs from Definition 3 and from Theorem 4, the same squeeze with K and κ in place of N and τ.

**Theorem 9 (Maximiser of V is Pareto-efficient, L11).** A maximiser is weakly efficient, and efficient when all w_j > 0. The sketch is readable: moving up in every coordinate raises V. "Weakly efficient" and "efficient" are not defined beyond Definition 1's "nondominated". "At K > 1, κ = ∞, through an active term" I could not parse.

**Section 6.2 prelude and eq. (2).** The several-term objective F(W) is a sum of per-term convex pieces f_k, using labels k(i). I could follow the algebra once I accepted J_τ(Uw) as the same J_τ with the scores spelled out. It hangs from Theorems 3 and 8 and eq. (1).

**Theorem 10 (Soft-weight alternation, L16).** (i) F is not convex in the terms, (ii) the soft slack is bounded by a fixed-weight objective, (iii) that objective is a generalized posynomial. It hangs from Theorem 8 (Φ_K) and the prelude. Part (i) is hard. Parts (ii) and (iii) use a "fixed-weight objective" that is never defined by itself.

**Theorem 11 (Level 1, descent, L15).** One iteration does not raise F. It hangs from eq. (2) and Theorem 3. "Step B" and "the penalty the package offers" came from nowhere.

**Lemma 1 (Level 2, term gap, L12).** G_k(w) = g·w − min_j g_j ≥ 0 bounds f_k(w) − f_k*, and G_k = 0 exactly at the KKT conditions of Theorem 2. It hangs from Theorems 2 and 3, and this link worked well: the quantity is computable from g alone.

**Lemma 2 (Assignment margin and stable radius, L13).** If the scores move by less than γ/2, each design keeps its maximising term. It hangs from R2 (γ). I could follow it.

**Proposition 3 (Level 3, local optimality, L14).** Inside the margin the objective equals F, so a minimiser of each f_k gives a local minimiser of F. It hangs from Lemma 2 and Theorem 11's setting.

**Corollary 2 (Approximate certificate, L14).** F − F*_fixed ≤ Σ (n_k/N) G_k. It hangs from Lemma 1 termwise and Proposition 3's setting. This one is a corollary in the usual sense, and it was clear.

**Theorem 12 (Soft-fit certificate, L17).** A second-order test, H = D − κM positive definite on the tangent space of the active face, gives a strict local minimiser. It hangs from Theorem 10 and the KKT idea of Theorem 2. "Critical cone" and "positive-multiplier bounds fixed" were opaque to me. D and M are given short readings (weighted within-term curvature, and designs' ambiguity between terms), which helped.

**Stopping declaration and the Numerical paragraph.** These summarise which levels count as "certified" and give measured numbers for K = 3. They hang from Proposition 3, Corollary 2 and Theorem 12 (all correctly named). I could follow the shape of the argument. I could not tell why "a margin below the band puts at least one design within the band of a seam" or what "primary regime" is.

**6.3 Open.** Four questions Q1 to Q4, each with a gap code.

## 3. Where the layout helped, and where it got in the way

**Helped**
- **The colour code.** Light blue boxes were definitions and propositions; purple were theorems and lemmas; orange was corollaries; grey was proofs; green was scripts. After two boxes I could skim for orange to find the corollaries and know where the results ended.
- **Statement, sketch, script.** The same triple under every result. It let me skip the green box once I understood it said which file backs the claim.
- **The "Derived. Hypotheses." line.** The first thing I could look at to see what a result stood on, and a reason to go back to A1 to A7. Where a code was glossed in brackets (Theorem 1: "A1 (metrics positive)"), I did not have to go back. Where the code was bare, I did.
- **Short lead-in sentences.** "The sentences between the boxes say what the next result is for" was true and helped ("The chart is a reversible rewriting of the log-metrics (L8), and under log-affine conditioning the pull-back to attributes is exact (L9)").
- **The "Level 1, Level 2, Level 3" labels.** In Theorem 11, Lemma 1 and Proposition 3, they gave me a path through the several-term guarantees.
- **Figure 1.** It earned its place; I could only understand Definition 6 through it. The legend (numbered coloured dots, rings for the planted answer), the rotated edge labels and the weights listed next to each term all read at once. "Shows / Does not show / Good for" was fair.
- **Equation numbers (1) and (2).** Used with sentences that point at them ("equation (2) at K = 1 is equation (1); otherwise they differ by ū₁·w"). That closed a gap.
- **Link colour.** "Theorem 2" in blue; abbreviations (KKT, LP, GP, PCA) with a dotted black underline, which I assume are hover glosses. I could not use them. Without hover, KKT, LP and GP were not explained to me.
- **Definition numbers.** One run, 1 to 9, from Section 3 to the end of Section 4.

**Got in the way**
- **Two numbering systems for results.** The box titles carry both ("Theorem 7 (Pull-back to attributes (L9))"), and the lead-ins speak only in L-numbers. The numbers do not line up: Theorem 7 is L9, Theorem 9 is L11, Theorem 5 is L7, Theorem 10 is L16, Theorem 11 is L15, Lemma 1 is L12. A reader who sees "L9" and goes looking for Theorem 9 lands on the wrong box. L-numbers also do not run in order on the page (L20 sits between L6 and L7, L16 comes before L15, L12 after L15).
- **Proof numbers.** One count, 1 to 19, across all result types, so "Proof 7" belongs to Corollary 1 and "Proof 15" to Lemma 1. Adjacency tells you which statement; the number does not.
- **Same colour for Theorem and Lemma, near-same colour for Definition and Proposition.** I could not tell by colour whether a box was a definition or a weaker result. I read the title.
- **"Lemma" has three meanings.** Boxed "Lemma 1, Lemma 2"; the "lemma_NN" scripts and L-codes (which cover theorems too); and the "n-lemma" (dilemma, trilemma) of Definition 6.
- **Corollary 1 is placed before its parents are complete.** It sits between Proposition 2 and Theorem 5, with a three-box dependency, and with L20 breaking the L-order. The "corollary" box carries the longest proof sketch in 6.1.
- **The Script box says a file name and "verify" and nothing about what the script checks.** It is a dead end for a reader without the code. "verify" in code font is unexplained.
- **"Derived means proved from its hypotheses by a plain-SymPy script" with "Proof (Sketch)" under it.** I was unsure whether the proof is the sketch or the script. "Derived" reads as "proved", but a sketch is not a proof.
- **The Numerical results.** The label is inline bold in Section 6's first line and again as a word in the stopping paragraph. The numbers sit in small type with no box. The structure that made Derived results easy to find (a coloured box) does not exist for them. "Open." in 6.3 is the same plain-text style.
- **The paragraph on ill-posed refitting** (after Theorem 10's script box) carries a claim ("observed on planted data") without a label.
- **Symbol collisions** (not layout, but they hit the layout where a reader checks across boxes):
  - n_k is the size of the contending set in Definition 6 and the number of designs labelled k in 6.2.
  - K is the number of terms, and k is both a term index and a scalar in Theorem 1 ("For k > 0: V(km) = kV(m)").
  - c is a configuration (Section 3), a shift constant (Proposition 1, Theorem 6, Theorem 8) and a coefficient c_k (Definition 3).
  - α_j is a conditioning slope, and α_ik a softmax weight.
  - I is the identity matrix and the prefix of the inherited-result codes.
  - F is the objective, and the prefix of the fallback codes.
  - "ridge" is the seam where terms meet (Words) and the penalty in Corollary 1.
  - "support" has three senses (nonzero exponents, support function, "support-weighted mean").
  - "level" is the value of V, and also "Level 1, 2, 3".
- **Each box's reference to an Appendix I do not have** (B, D, E) is a visible link but ends my reading.

## 5. The three places where I would have stopped first

1. **Section 5, R1, the first paragraph.** It comes before eq. (1), and uses "exact-maximum fit", "balanced point", "Uᵀp", "relative interior of a facet of the supporting envelope with positive normal" and "subgradient choice", in one sentence, each without a prior definition. It is the first place a reader without convex analysis meets facet, relative interior and subgradient, and p, and these cannot be skipped because R1 is a hypothesis that reappears in Theorem 2.
2. **Corollary 1 (L20).** A bare pile of A9, I9, I18, R3, noise Σ, C, P, a pseudoinverse, `conditioning_`, L18, and "first order", in a box whose sketch rests on three earlier boxes each with a gap. Nothing in the box tells me what an "active face" is. I would have stopped at the first line of the hypotheses.
3. **The refit paragraph after Theorem 10, with Definition 6.** Definition 6 says the contending set is the exact-maximum fit "to the term's cell" using "the term refit f_k of Section 6 on the cell's rows alone". The paragraph after Theorem 10 says refitting a term "on its own rows alone ... is ill-posed", that "the refit of record is f_k over all rows", and that the contending set's fit "is f_k itself on the cell's rows ..., not that plain fit". Eq. (2) defines f_k with J_τ(Uw), all rows, minus a cell mean. I could not tell which rows go into f_k for the contending set. This blocks Definition 6, Figure 1 and the n-lemma reading.

Theorem 10 part (i) and Theorem 12 would be next.
