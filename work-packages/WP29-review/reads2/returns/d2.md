## 2. In my own words from the script

(a) P. The script defines two projectors that agree where both apply. When every exponent of the clean fit is positive, `Pf(f, x) = f_x - (1/d) Sum_h f_h` is the mean-subtracting map, onto the sum-zero vectors. On the face (L20.55 to L20.58), P_T f = chi (f - (1/sf) Sum_h chi_h f_h), with chi_j = 1 on the support T of w* and 0 off it, and sf = Sum chi_j > 0. It projects onto the tangent space of the active face: {v : Sum_j v_j = 0, v_j = 0 off T}. What is proved is that P_T f sums to zero (L20.55), vanishes off T (L20.56), fixes any vector already in that space (L20.58), and Sum_j (P_T f)_j f_j = ‖P_T f‖² (L20.57), which is the orthogonality of f - P_T f to P_T f. The hypothesis on the fit is R3: w* is the minimiser of J_tau on the simplex, with T its positive exponents, the gradient exceeds lambda off T (strict complementarity: gapf > 0, L20.62), the gradient equals lambda on T (L20.52), and C is positive definite on that tangent space, with v'Cv >= m‖v‖², m > 0.

(b) The bound. The norm is the Euclidean 2-norm (I9 is stated in ‖.‖_2). The bound is ‖delta‖ <= ‖y‖ / m, where y = P Sigma w* (P_T Sigma w* on the face; sigma² P w* in the equal case). What divides it is the positive constant m with delta'C delta >= m‖delta‖² for tangent delta (the Rayleigh bound of R3, a slack s >= 0 in L20.45). The script proves nothing about m beyond treating it as a positive symbol that satisfies that inequality: it does not compute an eigenvalue, does not relate m to any property of the data, and "conditioning_ = tau m" is only a naming comment. The identity actually proved is ‖y‖/m - ‖delta‖ = (s + cs)/(m‖delta‖) >= 0 (L20.48), with s the Rayleigh slack and cs the Cauchy-Schwarz slack, valid when ‖delta‖ > 0.

(c) The shift. delta is the solution, on the face of w*, of the linearised stationarity condition of J^R(w) = J_tau(w) + (tau/2) w'Sigma w, that is v'(C delta + Sigma w*) = 0 for every tangent v. J_tau is expanded to first order at the clean minimiser w*, using its Hessian tau C there, the ridge is differentiated exactly (tau Sigma (w* + delta)), and the product Sigma delta is set aside (L20.35). It is a stationary point of the first-order model of J^R on the face, not shown to be a minimiser of J^R. J^R is the logarithm of the expected noisy sum, not the expectation of the noisy objective (docstring: the difference is Jensen's slack). The script never says J^R is convex, never says a minimiser exists, and does not bound the distance from the first-order shift to a true minimiser of J^R.

## 3. Hypotheses

Used in the script's steps but not printed in the box and not implied by it:

1. A5 (L20.17, L20.50, L20.51). Only the equal-noise reading. The equation Sigma = sigma² I is in the box's words, so only sigma > 0 is extra; it matters for the sign statement about ‖w‖² and nothing else.
2. A margin hypothesis for "zero exponents stay zero" (L20.62, L20.63): the positive margin mg = g - |e|, that is, the clean off-support gap exceeds the size of its first-order change. The box prints "noise small" and R3; R3's text says "a small perturbation ... leaves them at zero", but that is a posited consequence, not a quantity the script computes. The script treats it as a given positive symbol; e (a change of the gap that must include the change of lambda) is never defined from Sigma, C, w*.
3. The shift is tangent: sum_j delta_j = 0 and delta supported on T (L20.40, L20.46, L20.58, L20.61 substitute these in as assumptions). These are the assumptions that the true minimiser of J^R is on the same simplex and in the same face. They come from the constraint plus item 2. The simplex axiom A2 is not in the ledger of L20 and is not in the box's list; it enters through R3 ("minimizer on the simplex") and L3's dsimplex.
4. The Taylor step. The gradient of J_tau at w* + delta along a tangent v equals tau v'C delta to first order, with the remainder dropped. No step states it. It uses the existence of a smooth J_tau in a neighbourhood and a remainder of order ‖delta‖²; "noise small, result first order" in the box covers its status but not its content.
5. Symmetry of X = P C P (and of P). I18 as quoted requires a real symmetric X. C is shown symmetric (L20.31); L20.57 gives what is needed to derive symmetry of P, and thus of PCP, but no step states it, and the `I18.entails` call has no symmetry premise. The premise "delta in the range of X" is supplied as `range_ok = mc.is_positive` (L20.16, L20.43, L20.60), a proxy for "X is positive definite on the tangent space, so its range is the tangent space".
6. nd > 0 ("when the shift is not zero", L20.48), as noted at clause 10.
7. Identification of symbols with their meaning in L20.44 to L20.49: A_sym with delta'C delta, B_form with delta.y, nd with ‖delta‖, ny with ‖y‖ (ny is a bare real symbol, not declared as a norm). `VV` is defined and not used. The chain proves the inequalities for those symbols.

Printed in the box and not used by any step:
- "noise small": no step quantifies it. It names the term dropped in L20.35.
- Nothing else. A1 is used (L20.3, L20.20), A3 (L20.1, L20.20), A9 (L20.4 to L20.8), I9 (L20.13, L20.15, L20.47), I18 (L20.16, L20.43, L20.60), R3 (L20.11, L20.12, L20.14, L20.43, L20.52, L20.62).

## 4. Wording I would change

1. "Gaussian noise of covariance Sigma on the conditioned metrics" to "Gaussian noise of covariance Sigma on the log-metrics u = log m, the same Sigma in every row and mean zero". In the script the noise is additive on u, not on the metric m.
2. "the expected noisy objective" (in the box and sketch) to "the log of the expected noisy exponential, J^R(w) = (1/tau) log E (1/n) Sum_i exp(tau w.(u_i + eps_i))". As written it reads as the expectation of the noisy objective, which the script says it is not.
3. "moves the exponents to first order by" to "moves the exponents, to first order, from the clean fit w* to w* + Delta w, where Delta w solves the linearised stationarity condition P C P Delta w = -P Sigma w* and is given by".
4. "C is the softmax-weighted covariance of the log-metrics at the fit" to "... at the clean fit w*".
5. "The shift is at most ‖P Sigma w‖/m" to "The first-order shift, when it is not zero, is at most ‖P Sigma w*‖/m in the Euclidean norm". Optionally add "m > 0 being any constant with v'Cv >= m‖v‖² on that space (R3), the smallest eigenvalue giving the sharpest bound".
6. "and lowers w^T Sigma w, in the equal case ‖w‖²" to "and lowers w^T Sigma w to first order: Delta w^T Sigma w* <= -m ‖Delta w‖² <= 0, so ‖w‖² falls to first order in the equal case". The exact change contains the second-order term Delta w^T Sigma Delta w, which the script leaves out.
7. "so zero exponents stay zero" to "the shift vanishes where w does, and the zero exponents stay zero as long as the first-order change of their gradient gap stays below the clean gap (the margin of R3)".
8. "It is the shift of the minimizer of the expected noisy objective" to "It is the first-order shift of the stationary point of J^R on the face". Alternatively keep "minimizer" and add the missing step that J^R is convex (J_tau by L4, the ridge by A9's PSD Sigma). Not in the script as it stands.
9. Hypotheses line: add A5 for the equal case, or say "A5 for the equal case".
10. Sketch: "its Hessian is tau C ... and its gradient on the face vanishes" refers grammatically to the expected noisy objective. Say "J_tau has Hessian tau C (Theorem 3) and gradient vanishing on the face (Theorem 2); the ridge has gradient tau Sigma w, and setting the sum to zero on the face, with tau v'Sigma delta set aside, gives the closed form".

## 5. Where I was unsure

- Whether the first-order condition "for every tangent v" is meant to be a derived step. I read it as the one place where the script relies on the comment text rather than an assertion: L20.35 and L20.36 are algebra; L20.38 and L20.39 turn the condition at v = Pq into Pq = 0, and L20.44 tests it at v = delta. The condition itself is posited from the Taylor expansion described in the docstring.
- The face-case size bound and fall (L20.61): the script checks only delta.y_T = delta'Sigma w* and then says L20.44 to L20.49 hold "with m the smallest eigenvalue of C on that space". I read this as sound by the symbolic generality of those steps (they use only tangency, the Rayleigh bound and Cauchy-Schwarz), but they are not re-run for P_T.
- Whether R3 is meant to supply "delta lies in the range of X" for the face case (L20.60), where the kernel of X contains all non-tangent directions. I read it as: delta tangent and X positive definite on the tangent space gives delta in range. The script carries it as the boolean `range_ok`.
- What "e" in L20.62 stands for (I read it as the first-order change of g_j - lambda, including the change of lambda) and whether `b_dn = |e|` is meant to be proved from the earlier steps; the comment says it is defined that way.
- L20.9: the L3 quote is cut off in the entry ("and the"), so I could not tell whether the imported "balanced KKT G_j = lambda" is stated for all j or only on the support; the script treats it as all-j in the sum-zero section (L20.33) and as G_j = lambda + (1 - chi_j) a_j in the face section (L20.52).
- Whether "Proposition 2 is the equal case of L18" (sketch) is correct; L18's statement is not in the material.
