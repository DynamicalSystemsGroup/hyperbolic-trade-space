## 1. What the Corollary 1 box asserts, in box order

1. Gaussian noise with covariance Sigma on the conditioned metrics adds the ridge (tau/2) w^T Sigma w to the objective J_tau.
2. To first order the exponent vector w moves by Delta w = -(PCP)^+ P Sigma w.
3. When Sigma = sigma^2 I, this is -sigma^2 (PCP)^+ P w.
4. C is the softmax-weighted covariance of the log-metrics at the fit. P is the orthogonal projector onto vectors that sum to zero and vanish wherever w does. When all exponents are positive, P = I - 11^T/d.
5. Zero exponents stay zero (follows from P's range).
6. The shift has norm at most ||P Sigma w|| / m, with m the smallest eigenvalue of C on the range of P.
7. The shift lowers w^T Sigma w (in the equal case, ||w||^2).
8. The result is the shift of the minimizer of the expected noisy objective, not of any single noisy fit.
9. The result says nothing about whether the rates (the fitted exponents) are right.

## 2. What the box leaves unclaimed

Stated: nothing about a single noisy fit, nothing about correctness of the rates, nothing beyond first order. Inferred:
- No bound on the second-order remainder and no size for "small".
- No statement about sampling error, finite-sample behaviour or consistency (Proposition 2's hypotheses say "no consistency claim"; here the box only says "not of one noisy fit").
- No recipe for estimating Sigma, and nothing on what to do when noise is not Gaussian or not independent of the design.
- No claim that the fit is a minimizer in the first place; the proof sketch uses a vanishing gradient "on the face", which is imported from Theorem 2.
- "Lowers" is a first-order statement; strictness is not claimed (it would fail if P Sigma w = 0).

## 3. How someone with a data table and a fitted model would use it

1. Take the fitted exponents w (a point presumably on a simplex face, with some entries possibly zero) and the fitted J_tau with its tau.
2. Compute the log-metrics for each row of the table, the softmax weights at the fit (weights proportional to exp(tau w . log m_i), presumably), and their weighted covariance C.
3. Build P from the support of w: sums-to-zero projector on the positive entries, zero elsewhere.
4. Choose or estimate Sigma, the noise covariance of the log-metrics; in the equal case choose sigma^2.
5. Compute Delta w = -(PCP)^+ P Sigma w. The bound uses the smallest eigenvalue of C on the range of P.
6. Read Delta w as the correction between the exponents fitted to noisy data (in the expected-objective sense) and the noise-free exponents; the sign of the shift is toward smaller w^T Sigma w, i.e. toward more evenly spread exponents in the equal case.

What the box does not tell them: whether Sigma is noise on metrics or on log-metrics (I assume log-metrics, because C is a covariance of log-metrics and Proposition 2 uses w . epsilon); what "J_tau" is exactly (I assume a (1/tau) log-mean-exp of tau w . log m with data-dependent normalisation) and what tau is; whether w sums to one (needed for P to keep the shifted w feasible; the box does not say); whether to use the noisy fit's w or a de-noised w for the correction (w appears on both sides in spirit); whether the noise is on all metrics or only some; how large noise may be.

## 4. Are the printed hypotheses sufficient?

Roughly, yes for a first-order stationarity argument, with several steps I could not close from the material:
- Closed: if the expected noisy objective is J_tau + (tau/2) w^T Sigma w, with Hessian tau C and gradient tau Sigma w at the fit from the ridge (with J_tau's own gradient zero on the face), then Newton-style first order gives tau C Delta w = -tau Sigma w on the face, so Delta w = -(PCP)^+ P Sigma w; tau cancels. The norm bound follows from ||(PCP)^+|| = 1/m. The decrease of w^T Sigma w follows because w^T Sigma Delta w = -(P Sigma w)^T (PCP)^+ (P Sigma w) <= 0 for PSD C.
- Not closed: (a) the identity "expectation of noisy objective = J_tau + ridge" holds exactly if the expectation is taken inside the logarithm (log of an expected exponential, as in Proposition 2). The expectation of the log-mean-exp itself would not give it, by Jensen. The box says "minimizer of the expected noisy objective", which could be read either way. (b) The bound requires m > 0, i.e. C positive definite on the range of P. That is not printed in the hypotheses as far as I can read them (A9 or I9 may cover it, but I cannot see them). (c) Whether the fit is a stationary point of J_tau on the face (Theorem 2, cited in the sketch, not in the hypotheses line). (d) Whether the minimizer is unique and the face constraint is the only one (the sum-to-one constraint, and nonnegativity, are what P encodes; the box does not state them). (e) First-order error is not controlled, so "noise small" is not quantified.
