## 1. The thread, box to box

**Definition 1 (Three objects).** It names three things that the word "frontier" can mean: the fitted reference surface (V = 1, finite tau), the supporting envelope (the tau = infinity fit) and the Pareto boundary. It hangs from the "Words" paragraph before it. It already uses "V" and "posynomial equals one", which are not defined until Definitions 2 and 3.

**Definition 2 (Invariant form).** V(m) = e^(-beta) times the product of m_j^(w_j), with log V = w.u - beta. This is the first formula I could hold. It points forward to Definition 7 for beta, and I carried beta as "an offset" until I got there.

**Definition 3 (Posynomial).** With several terms, V_K is (sum of c_k times monomials)^(1/kappa), and Phi_K is its log. Each term is a piece of the surface, and a design's "cell" is the set of designs whose largest term is that term. It generalises Definition 2 from one term to K terms, and points to Theorem 8.

**Definition 4 (Temperatures and band).** tau controls how sharp the maximum over designs is, and kappa how sharp it is over terms. The "near-frontier band" is |log V| < 1/tau. It hangs from the assumptions A3 and A6 in Section 3, which I met much earlier without knowing what tau and kappa were.

**Definition 5 (The chart).** Z = Bu, with B a (d-1) x d matrix with orthonormal rows and rows summing to zero. (Z, log V) is a reversible rewriting of the metrics. This is the first box that was plain linear algebra for me. It matches assumption A7.

**Definition 6 (The n-lemma).** For each term, take the cell's rows, fit at tau = infinity, and call the exponents above 10^-9 the "contending set". Its size n_k names the n-lemma. It leans on f_k of equation (2), which is on page 9, and on a threshold in Section 5. **The thread frayed here.** I picked it up from Figure 1 and its caption, which say what the result is for: corner = one metric, edge = two metrics, interior = three.

**Definition 7 (Reach and share).** beta_k is the soft maximum of the scores along w_k. "Term share" is the fraction of near-frontier designs in the cell. It closes the loop opened in Definition 2.

**Definition 8 (Elasticity).** Along term k, d log m_j / d log m_i = -w_ki / w_kj. It is stated here and derived in Theorem 5.

**Definition 9 (Ascent direction).** The tangent space {v : w_k.v = 0} and the normal w_k, which is the gradient of log V in u. Theorem 5's sketch is the same computation. The title says "ascent direction", but the body mostly describes the tangent space, and only the normal points uphill.

**Theorem 1 (Homogeneity, L1).** V(km) = kV(m) and V(m/V(m)) = 1. The sketch is one line and I could follow it, except that the sketch says "A1 gives V = exp(w.u)" with no beta, while Definition 2 has e^(-beta).

**Proposition 1 (Uniform shift, L2).** Adding c to every entry of U adds c to J_tau and leaves the minimizing w unchanged. It uses "J_tau", which is first written in equation (1) in Section 5 and not named in Section 4. The "beta moves by c" line took me a moment.

**Theorem 2 (Gradient and the simplex KKT conditions, L3).** At a minimizer w on the simplex, with g the gradient and lambda = w.g, g_j >= lambda everywhere and g_j = lambda where w_j > 0. So U^T p has equal components on the support of w. I could follow this as Lagrange-style stationarity with the constraint w >= 0, sum w = 1. It is the first box whose hypothesis line refers to R1 and an appendix line.

**Theorem 3 (Convexity, L4).** The second derivative along any line is tau times a p-weighted variance, so J_tau is convex. I could follow this. It is the natural second step after Theorem 2.

**Theorem 4 (Log-mean-exp against log-sum-exp, L5).** The two differ by (log N)/tau, and there are squeeze bounds with the mean and the maximum. I could follow it, and it explains the earlier remark about the factor 1/N.

**Proposition 2 (Gaussian cumulant, L6).** (1/tau) log E exp(tau w.eps) = tau sigma^2 ||w||^2 / 2. This is the standard Gaussian moment generating function, so I could follow it. It sets up Corollary 1.

**Corollary 1 (Noise moves the exponents by a ridge, L20).** Noise adds a ridge (tau/2) w^T Sigma w and shifts w by -(PCP)^+ P Sigma w. P is a projection and ^+ is a pseudoinverse. I followed the projection and the shape of the formula. I could not check the formula itself. Proof 7 helped: Hessian tau C (Theorem 3), gradient on the face vanishing (Theorem 2), and Newton-like balancing.

**Theorem 5 (Elasticities and improvement factors, L7).** On {w.u = beta}, d log m_j / d log m_i = -w_i / w_j, and a design with V < 1 reaches V = 1 by improving metric j alone by V^(-1/w_j) or all metrics by 1/V. I could follow the algebra. It cashes Definition 8.

**Theorem 6 (The chart, L8).** Z(u + c1) = Z(u), u = B^T Z + (1/d)(1^T u)1, and (Z, log V) is a bijection. I could follow this. Q = [B; 1^T/sqrt(d)] orthogonal is used with I6.

**Theorem 7 (Pull-back to attributes, L9).** Substituting log m_j = alpha_j log(a_j + s_j) + b_j gives V as a product of powers of the attributes. I could follow the substitution.

**Theorem 8 (The posynomial extension, L10).** Phi_K shifts by c, Phi_1 = w_1.u - beta_1, max_k x_k <= Phi_K <= max_k x_k + (log K)/kappa, and e^(kappa Phi_K) is a posynomial. It cashes Definition 3. The squeeze borrows Theorem 4, with K and kappa in place of N and tau.

**Theorem 9 (A maximizer of V is weakly Pareto-efficient, L11).** A maximizer is "weakly" efficient, and efficient when every w_j > 0. I could follow this in words and with the sketch (contrapositive, a gain is a sum of nonnegative terms).

**Theorem 10 (Soft-weight alternation, L16).** Part (i): the objective is symmetric but not convex. This works as an implication, because a convex symmetric function would have a symmetric minimizer. Part (ii): the soft slack is bounded by a fixed-weight objective. Part (iii): the exponential of that is a "generalized posynomial". I could follow (i) and the statement of (ii) and (iii) but not why they hold. **The thread frayed at (iii).**

**Theorem 11 (Level 1, descent, L15).** One accepted iteration does not raise the mean slack F, because reassigning rows to their best term and refitting each term both lower or keep it. I could follow the idea. It is a standard alternating-minimization argument.

**Lemma 1 (Level 2, term gap, L12).** The term gap G_k = g.w - min_j g_j >= 0, with 0 <= f - f* <= G, and G = 0 exactly at the KKT conditions of Theorem 2. It is a measurable proxy for "how far from stationary". It hangs from Theorems 2 and 3.

**Lemma 2 (Assignment margin and stable radius, L13).** If score changes are below half the margin gamma, each row's maximizing term does not change. I could follow the statement. The length ||(u_i, -1)|| in r was not explained in the box. I guessed that the -1 pairs with beta.

**Proposition 3 (Level 3, conditional local optimality, L14).** Within radius r of Lemma 2, with cells held fixed, F(W') - F(W) >= 0. It combines Lemma 2 (cells stay fixed) with each w_k minimizing f_k.

**Corollary 2 (Approximate certificate, L14).** F(W) minus the fixed-cell optimum is at most the weighted sum of the term gaps G_k. It is Lemma 1 summed with weights n_k/N, and Proof 18 says so.

**Theorem 12 (Soft-fit certificate, L17).** At a KKT point of the soft fit, if H = D - kappa M is positive definite on the tangent space of the active face, the point is a strict local minimizer. I could follow "second-order test on a Hessian". I could not follow "positive-multiplier bounds fixed, the rest free", "critical cone", or "second-order sufficient conditions (I16)". **This is where the thread broke for good.** I took the rest on trust for the statement's shape.

The two Numerical paragraphs and 6.3 are unboxed, and I read them as prose.

## 3. What the layout did

**Helped**
- The colours separate roles at a glance: purple for Theorem and Lemma, blue for Definition and Proposition, orange for Corollary, grey for Proof (Sketch), green for Script. Once I had the key I scanned the pages by colour.
- "Derived. Hypotheses." in bold and italics at the top of every result box. The hypothesis line is the first thing I read, and it told me which standing assumptions each result needs. Where it is glossed (A1 (metrics positive)) I did not have to go back. In Theorem 1 it did that for the first time.
- The sentences between boxes ("The one-term fit has simplex optimality conditions (L3), is convex (L4), ...") gave me the plan for the next two or three boxes. These helped most in 6.1.
- Sketches are one to three lines and sit directly under the box, so I could see the idea without leaving the page.
- Script boxes put a path and an appendix link in a fixed place, so I knew every Derived result has a check behind it.
- Figure 1 with its Shows / Does not show / Good for lines. The three sentences told me what to look for in the triangle, and the legend (term 1: dilemma of 2 and 3) matched the picture. The ring for the planted vector next to each coloured disc was easy to read.
- Blue dotted links for Theorem, Definition, Lemma and Proposition references. The ones I tried (listed below) landed on the right box.
- Equation numbers on the right for (1) and (2), with the formulas centred.

**Got in the way**
- Result numbers are per type (Theorem 1, Proposition 1, Theorem 2 ...), the lemma tags are L1 to L20, and Proofs have their own counter (Proof 7 sits under Corollary 1, Proof 9 under Theorem 6). So Theorem 5 is L7, Proof 8 and Theorem 5. "Lemma 2" is L13, while "L2" is Proposition 1. I had to keep a table in my head. "Box numbers follow the page" explains the order but not the offsets.
- The lettered codes: A and R are explained in Sections 3 and 5, but I-codes (I5, I9, I18 ...) are explained only where they occur and are not links. Hypothesis lines also carry remarks ("The converse is not claimed", "a population identity, with no consistency claim", "noise small, the result first order"). So I could not tell which parts of a line are conditions and which are caveats.
- Hypothesis lines carry the technical meaning, and the gloss differs from box to box. I had to decide whether "I10: first-order conditions" in Proof 3 is a name or a reference.
- Appendix links (Appendix B, L3.9; L17.19 and others) and lemma scripts are outside the pages. I could not follow them, and some statements in boxes lean on them: Theorem 2's "facet-normal reading", Theorem 11's finite stopping, Theorem 12's "critical cone".
- The "Script" box says "verify" in monospace. I did not know whether "verify" is a function name, a command or a status.
- Definitions 1 to 9 are blue boxes, like Propositions. Results are in colours but definitions look like results at first sight.
- Definition 3, Figure 1, Theorem 3, Theorem 6, Theorem 10 and Corollary 2 are cut by a page edge in the pictures, so the title and body were in different pictures.
- Two Numerical paragraphs are in smaller type and unboxed, so they read like footnotes, but they carry the numbers.
- Equation (2) is referred to without a link, and equation (1) the same way.

## 5. The three places I would have stopped first

1. **R1 in Section 5**: "optimal dual weight vector", "balanced point U^T p", "relative interior of a facet of the convex hull of the rows, whose normal is positive". This is convex-analysis vocabulary, and it comes before the model that would give it meaning. Theorem 2 later refers to it.
2. **Definition 6 and the paragraph after Theorem 10**: three different fits of one term (the plain one-term fit on the cell, the refit of record over all rows, and the contending-set fit at tau = infinity), with f_k of an equation that has not appeared yet.
3. **Theorem 12**: KKT point, tangent space of the active face, "positive-multiplier bounds fixed, the rest free", critical cone, second-order sufficient conditions, and H = D - kappa M as a difference of two PSD matrices.
