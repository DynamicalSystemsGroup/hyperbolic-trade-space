## Q5 (a) What the image shows (written before the caption)

A scatter plot titled "total mass and energy balance". Horizontal axis: log of the conditioned total mass (inverse kg; larger is better), about -3 to 2.5. Vertical axis: log of the conditioned energy balance (Wh; larger is better), about -3.5 to 2. Several hundred points sit in a cloud that runs from upper left to lower right. A minority are dark grey ("designs whose other six metrics are near the median, within 0.1 in log V"). The rest are light grey ("the other designs"). One straight black line falls from upper left to lower right ("the surface in this slice"). It cuts through the middle of the cloud, so many points lie on each side. The dark points are scattered through the whole cloud and do not follow the line. The line seems to bound the cloud near the top right but does not clearly bound it.

## Q5 (b) Image against caption

Mostly yes. The axes (the two heaviest metrics, total mass and energy balance, as logs), the single line with a negative slope, and the dark and light points are all as described. What the image does not show clearly:

- "they reach the line and stop there, a few beyond it". The dark points fill the region left of and under the line, and a visible number sit to its right. The claim holds loosely for the upper right, but the dark points do not hug the line. They are spread across the whole cloud, and the cloud is thinner at the far left and far upper-left than the line suggests. The eye does not see an edge.
- The line cuts through the middle of the plotted cloud, with many light points far to its right. A reader who has just read that the surface is fitted to the outer edge sees a line through the middle, not along an edge. The caption's reply (light designs are weighed by the other six metrics) is not visible in the picture.
- The picture gives no sign of the "within 0.1 in log V" criterion beyond the legend. It does not show the other six metrics, and its title does not name the regime or K.
- The slope as an elasticity is read from the line, but no number appears on the figure.

## Q5 (c) The sentence test

Sentence: "With one term, the surface in the plane of total mass and energy balance is a straight line in the log-metrics whose slope is their elasticity, and the designs whose other metrics sit near the median stop at that line."

The figure conveys a little more than the sentence: how fat the cloud is, how far the dark points spread, that some designs do exceed the line, and the rough size of the slope (about -1 in log space). It conveys less than the sentence on the "stop at it" half, because the stopping is not visible by eye.

## Q1 What the figure conveys

At K = 1 the one-term surface appears in the plane of the two heaviest metrics as a straight line whose slope is their elasticity, with the designs plotted around it.

## Q2 Sentence of the story served

Main-results item 7 ("on a cubesat model ... the terms separate contending sets that one term blends"), as its one-term baseline. It also serves item 2 ("its exponents are elasticities"), for the slope. It does not serve any abstract sentence directly except "one term blends the subsystems' trade-offs into a single tension". The paper's text next to it says only "At K = 1 the fit reports one tension at the balanced point (Figure 5)."

## Q3 Verdict

advances

Figure 6 refers back to it ("Dark and light as in Figure 5"), and it is the only picture of the one-term fit that the K = 3 comparison in item 7 rests on. The weakness is in how well it carries its own claim, not in whether the story needs it: the "stop at the line" reading is not visible, and the line through the middle of the cloud sits uneasily with "fitted to the outer edge".
