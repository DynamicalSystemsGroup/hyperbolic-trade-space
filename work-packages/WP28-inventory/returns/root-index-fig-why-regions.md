# root-index-fig-why-regions

## Q5

(a) A triangle whose corners are labelled "favours metric 1" (top), "favours metric 2" (bottom left) and "favours metric 3" (bottom right), so each point inside is presumably a direction or weighting over three metrics. The triangle is split into three coloured regions with curved and straight boundaries: a large orange region on the left and top labelled "all three trade", a blue region on the right labelled "metric 3 trades against metric 1", and a green region along the bottom labelled "metric 3 trades against metric 2". Several hundred grey dots (larger ones and fainter small ones) are scattered over it, densest in a band from the middle-left down toward the bottom centre, crossing all three regions. I read it as: depending on which way you lean among the three metrics, a different set of metrics is in tension, and the dots are designs (or directions) placed in that map. I cannot tell from the image alone what a dot is, what placed it, or why the boundaries are where they are.

(b) Mostly yes. The caption confirms my reading: dots are evaluated designs, placed by which metric they favour, and the regions name the trade-off that binds there, so "what trades against what depends on where a design sits" is shown. The caption adds one thing the image cannot show by itself: that the larger dots are nearest the frontier. The image has no legend for dot size, and at this point on the page "the frontier" has not been introduced, so I took the size difference as a styling choice until the caption told me. What it does not show: where the regions come from (the caption does not say they come from the fitted model, so a reader cannot tell if they are the method's output or a hand-drawn illustration); what "trades" means numerically (no elasticities or exponents); and the "all three trade" label does not say whether all three are traded evenly. The region near the bottom-right and top corners with few or no dots could be read as "where the data do not reach", but neither the figure nor the caption says so.

(c) Sentence: "Which metrics trade against which is not fixed for the whole table; it changes with where a design sits, and the method finds those regions from the evaluated designs." The figure conveys more than that sentence: it shows that one region (all three trade) covers most of the designs and the two pairwise regions are smaller, that the boundaries are curved, and that the designs do not fill the triangle evenly. It does not convey the "the method finds those regions" part; that is my addition, and the figure does not back it.

## Q8

- Home page ("learns the shape of a design trade space ... tells you which metrics contend ... where each design sits"): yes, it previews the kind of answer a user gets: per-region contention with designs placed on it. It does not show elasticity or the gaps.
- Root page opening ("applies a posynomial invariant form ... to learn the shape of a trade space from evaluated designs"): partly; it shows a shape learned from designs, but nothing in it ties the regions to the posynomial form or its terms.
- Its page's opening sentence for the section it sits in, Why ("Trade studies are usually decided from documents and tables, where the detail of the evaluated designs is lost"): partly; it keeps every evaluated design visible as a dot, which answers "detail is lost", but it does not show reasoning, missing data or rerunnability, the three points of the bar the paragraph sets.
