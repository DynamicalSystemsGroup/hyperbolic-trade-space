## Q5 (a) What the image shows (written before the caption)

A triangle whose corners are labelled "favours metric 1" (top), "favours metric 2" (bottom left) and "favours metric 3" (bottom right), so a point inside is a mix of weights on three metrics. The triangle is shaded in three regions: orange (left, large, labelled "all three trade"), blue (right, labelled "metric 3 trades against metric 1") and green (bottom, labelled "metric 3 trades against metric 2"). Grey dots (many, of two sizes) are scattered across it. Black contour lines run through it, and form closed loops around three marked points: a square numbered 3 in the orange region, a blue star numbered 1 near the right edge, and a green diamond numbered 2 near the bottom edge. It reads as a map of which design or trade-off pattern wins, depending on how the three metrics are weighted, with a best point for each region.

## Q5 (b) After the caption

Mostly yes. The caption's three things are all there: regions (the three tints), numbered exponent vectors (the numbered square, star and diamond), and lines of equal V (the black contours closing around each marker). The alt text adds that the tint is "the term that is largest there" and that the dots are designs; the image does not say that. The labels inside the image ("all three trade", "metric 3 trades against metric 1", "metric 3 trades against metric 2") are not in the caption. The image does not show: what the lines are worth (no V values, no scale, no legend); what the two sizes of grey dot mean; the total that the shares are shares of; that it is a synthetic table of three metrics (the page text says so, the figure does not); any design chosen or recommended. The contour lines continue across the region borders, so the figure does not show the contours as belonging to one term only. The picture reads as a three-way win map; the caption names the parts but not the point.
