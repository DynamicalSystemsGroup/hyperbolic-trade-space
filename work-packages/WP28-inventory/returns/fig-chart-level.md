# fig-chart-level

## Q5 (a) What the image shows (written before the caption)

A grey scatter of several hundred dots. The horizontal axis runs from about -4.3 to 3.8 and is labelled as a position along "the surface", the direction of most spread among near-frontier designs (44% of it), with better mass and control effort to the right and worse pointing error. The vertical axis is "log V: level" (0 on the surface, negative inside), running from 0 down to about -1. A black horizontal line at 0 is "the surface", with a thin grey "near-frontier band" just under it. Most dots sit between 0 and -0.3, densest near the line and toward the middle and right. A sparse tail reaches down to about -1.0, and the lowest dots are on the left. Almost no dots lie on or above the line. Dots thin out at the far left and far right.

## Q5 (b) After the caption

Yes, the image matches the caption: level log V against position along the first principal direction of the near-frontier designs, the surface as the flat line at 0, a grey band near it, and the right-hand end labelled better mass and control effort, worse pointing error. It does not show the K = 3 terms (the dots are one grey colour, so nothing says which term scores a design highest). It does not show the loadings or the other directions of position (these are in Appendix D.16). Nor does it show which dots are near-frontier. The band's width is hard to read (it looks like about 0.03 thick) and the caption says it is the K = 1 band, not the K = 3 one. The caption says a design above the line lies beyond the surface. Only a handful of dots visibly do, so "beyond" is nearly empty in the image. The caption's "44%" appears only in the axis label, and the energy-differential name is not on the axes.

## Q5 (c) The sentence test

"Plotted by position along the surface's main direction of spread, nearly every design lies inside the surface, close to it (level 0 to -0.3), with a few beyond it and a thin tail of deep ones, mostly at the low-position end."

The figure conveys a little more than this sentence: the shape of the cloud (densest near the line across the whole range, thinning at both ends, deepest dots on the left), the roughly 700 individual dots, and that the level and the position are separate coordinates. The sentence cannot show the last of these.

## Q1. What the figure conveys

Each design's level relative to the surface can be read apart from its position along the surface's direction of main spread, and in this case the designs lie inside the flat surface and hug it.

## Q2. Sentence of the story served

Abstract: "A reversible chart separates a design's position along the surface from its energy differential." Also main results item 1, "the level from which every design's position and level... are read". Item 1 (and the reversible chart of item 2) states this in words. This figure is the one place in the paper's story that draws the case's designs in those two coordinates.

## Q3. Verdict

advances. The story says the chart splits position from level, and this is the only figure of the case that draws it, so a reader sees what "level" and "position" are for the data. The advance is modest. The sentence of intent carries most of it, and the figure adds the cloud's shape. The caption's "beyond the surface" and the band are barely visible.

## Q4. If "repeats"

Not applicable. The text of Section 9 only names the figure ("shows each design's level read apart from its position along the surface") and does not carry its content. Figure 5 shows designs stopping at the line in a log-metric plane, a different view of the same edge.

## Q9. Read twice and could not place

- "the K = 1 fit's band as at every K": why the K = 1 band is drawn on a K = 3 chart is explained only by a pointer to gap G-9 (Appendix E), which I was not given.
- "energy differential" is in the caption but the axis says "level"; I took them to be the same thing.
- "44% of it": I read it as the share of the spread along this direction, as the caption's pointer to Appendix D.16 suggests, but the figure does not say share of what.
- "to the right, better total mass and control effort; worse pointing error": it is not clear whether the direction is a trade or a cost, and the figure shows no sign of either.
