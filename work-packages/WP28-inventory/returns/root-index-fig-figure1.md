# root-index-fig-figure1

## Q5 (a) What the image shows (written before the caption)

Three panels side by side, one scatter of grey points seen three ways, with a coloured curve made of three pieces (blue, orange, green; "term 1, 2, 3").

- Left, "Metric space: fitted reference surface": metric 1 against metric 2, points piled toward the origin, a curved frontier in three coloured pieces bending around the cloud, labelled V = 1. Dotted curves lie beyond ("V > 1: beyond") and inside. One black dot inside the cloud has an arrow pointing toward the frontier.
- Middle, "Log space: supporting envelope": the same on log axes. The frontier becomes straight segments (a polyline) with a normal arrow w1, w2, w3 on each. The black dot's arrow is labelled "energy differential log V".
- Right, "Chart (Z, log V): terms laid flat": the frontier is a flat line at log V = 0, split into the three coloured terms along Z (position along the frontier). Points hang below it; the black dot has a vertical arrow up to the line, labelled log V.

So it shows: a design's distance to the frontier, measured the same way in three coordinate systems, ending in a flat picture where the frontier is level and the gap is vertical.

## Q5 (b) After the caption

Yes, the image shows what the caption says: the same three coloured terms in each pane, and one black arrow per pane for one design's step to the surface, labelled "energy differential" in the middle pane and "log V" in the right one. The alt text matches too (surface, envelope, chart with terms end to end).

What it does not show: what "energy differential" means or why the step is called that (the caption uses the phrase as if known); that the surface is fitted to this table (no sign of the fit, only the result); what V is (it appears as "V = 1", "V > 1" and "log V" with no definition on the figure or the page); what Z is beyond "position along the frontier"; what the dotted curves are (the left and middle dotted lines are unexplained); and the arrows w1 to w3 (not in the caption). It does not show tensions, the n-lemma, or PCA, which the text just above it is about. The arrow in the left pane points up and right, not perpendicular to the curve, and in the middle pane the arrow label sits over points and is hard to read.

## Q5 (c) The sentence test

"The same table of designs is drawn three ways (as metrics, as logs, and as a chart where the frontier is flat), and in each a design's gap to the fitted frontier is one step, the log of V, measured straight up in the last view."

The figure conveys more than the sentence: how the curved frontier becomes straight pieces and then one flat line, which piece belongs to which term, and that the designs sit under the line at different depths. It conveys less than the sentence in one respect: it does not say what V is, so a first-time reader cannot tell the vertical gap means "how far short of the frontier".

## Q1. What does this figure convey?

That a fitted frontier made of three terms can be redrawn from curved (metric space) to straight (log space) to flat (chart), and that each design's distance to it is the same step in all three.

## Q2. Which sentence does it serve?

Home page: "where each design sits relative to the fitted reference surface"; and "learns the shape of a design trade space from evaluated designs" / "a sum of monomial terms". On the root page the nearest sentence is "to learn the shape of a trade space from evaluated designs" (the opening), not the "What" paragraph it sits under (that one is about tensions, PCA and the estimator interface).

## Q8. Against the three pages, one line each

- Home page: it helps a little with "where each design sits relative to the fitted reference surface", but "energy differential" and V are not defined there, so a user would not know how to read the step.
- Root page: it does not follow from the "What" paragraph (PCA, tensions, the n-lemma, sklearn methods); a reader expecting a picture of tensions or of the estimator's methods gets a geometry of a frontier instead, and the caption uses terms the page has not introduced.
