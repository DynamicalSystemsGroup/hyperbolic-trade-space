## 1. What the site is about

A Python package, `hyperbolic-trade-space`, that takes a table of evaluated designs (each scored on several "metrics", all positive and larger-is-better) and fits a "frontier" model to it: a surface that the designs sit under or just beyond. The model is a sum of one or more "monomial terms" (products of powers of the metrics), and the exponents of each term tell you which metrics trade against each other and how hard. It's framed as an unsupervised, scikit-learn-style estimator living next to PCA ("PCA finds where the data spread, this finds where they stop"). The site has a landing page, a paper, and a docs section with Getting started, a user guide (one page per task), API reference, a cubesat example, theory and glossary. The package is installed from source with uv.

## 5. Terms I had to guess

- "posynomial invariant form", "monomial term": from the docs home I took a monomial term to be a product of powers of the metrics and the model to be a sum of such terms. "Posynomial" itself was not defined where I met it on the landing page.
- "n-lemma / trilemma / 5-lemma": a set of n metrics that trade against each other, named by its size. Guessed from "an n-lemma with n = 3, which the report names a trilemma".
- "V", "log V", "energy differential": V seems to be a design's score relative to the fitted surface (1 on the surface, above 1 beyond it); log V is the signed distance in log units. The "energy" wording I just read as "distance".
- "tau / temperature": "how hard the maximum is"; larger tau means a sharper frontier and a narrower band 1/tau. I took it on faith.
- "mean slack": mean shortfall of the designs below the fitted surface (the Fitting page says this). Fine.
- "assignment", "cell", "seam": with several terms each design is assigned to the term that scores it highest (its cell); a seam is where two terms tie. Guessed from "if no design sits exactly on a seam between terms".
- "term gap G_k", "KKT", "first-order improvement", "stationarity", "restricted Hessian", "eigenvalue", "curvature": optimization vocabulary I don't have. I read max_gap as "how much the solver could still improve each term", and KKT as the name of the stopping tolerance for that.
- "gamma / margin" and "radius / stable radius": the smallest amount by which a design's winning term beats the runner-up, and how far you could perturb the terms before any design switches. The page does explain this, but in a dense sentence.
- "support offsets", "supporting hyperplane", "refit_step support/tangent", "soft fit", "kappa / term temperature": appeared in the Termination page and docstring; I guessed "soft fit" means designs are weighted across terms instead of hard-assigned, and otherwise skipped them.
- "shares": see section 4; two meanings.
- "certified": not a word I'd use for "converged"; I read it as the package's strongest stop status.

## 6. Did the fit converge, and how I know

Taking "the package's example" as the cubesat study on the Examples page, at three terms (the setting the page itself describes with a status), my answer is: yes, the fit converged, by the package's own criteria, to a local optimum.

Evidence from the record I produced (`case.fit_study(study, K=3).estimator.termination_`, primary regime, tau = 113.22):

- `status = 'certified'`, `reason = 'assignment fixed'`. The Termination page says certified means all three of its levels hold: no step made the mean slack worse, no first-order improvement is left, and the assignment of designs to terms is fixed with a positive margin.
- `assignment_changes = 0` and `n_empty = 0`: no design wants to switch terms and no term is empty.
- `max_gap = 4.1e-15`, each per-term gap ~1e-15, all far below `eps_kkt = 1e-8`; `bound = 2.5e-15`, which the docstring says bounds how far this run's objective is from the fixed-assignment optimum.
- `sum_residual`, `floor_residual`, `offset_residual` all 0.0, below `eps_feas = 1e-9`.
- `gamma = 0.00121 > 0` and `radius = 0.000121`: positive margin, so small changes to the terms don't reassign any design. Following the page's "how small is a small margin" rule, gamma (0.0012) is below the band 1/tau (0.0088) but of the same order; the page's own example calls 0.0020 vs 0.02 "level 3 holds", so by that reading this one holds too. I did not compute the median margin for the cubesat fit.
- `objective_change = 2.7e-06` at the last accepted step, `n_iter = 14`.
- `n_distinct_terms = 3` of 3 requested: no repeated term.
- The k_terms script's seed sweep showed the same objective (0.145788) and the same contending sets for all five seeds, which the Fitting page would call stability across restarts.

Caveats the site itself insists on: the Termination page says certified is "a local optimum of the objective, not of other restarts"; that the fit describes the observed designs (read `mean_slack_`, here 0.146, down from 0.243 at one term); and that whether it holds outside the table is not something the certificate says. The script's seed agreement is my only evidence against other local optima.

For the one-term fit (the Getting started first fit, and the cubesat K=1): the record's status is `not_applicable`, with reason "Optimization terminated successfully". So for one term the record does not certify anything; the site's claim that one term is a convex problem whose answer is the minimizer is what I'd rely on, plus the solver's own success message. The gap value (8.7e-06 for the cubesat, 1.3e-05 for the first fit) is above the 1e-8 tolerance but the docstring says gaps are only diagnostics in that case.

The Termination page's own synthetic example also returned `certified` with max_gap 1.8e-15 and gamma 0.0020, matching the page's printed values exactly.

## 7. What I believe the method does and does not do

Does: takes a table of positive, larger-is-better metrics (after I condition my real attributes into that form with `LogAffinePreconditioner`), fits a frontier as one or more power-law terms in log space, reports which metrics contend (the contending set), the exponents (weights summing to one per term) and their ratios as elasticities, assigns each design a signed log-distance to the surface (`score_samples`), gives a coordinate chart (`transform`/`inverse_transform`), and records how the solver stopped with a status and tolerances. With several terms it partitions designs among terms and reports per-term contending sets. It runs as a normal sklearn estimator.

Does not: it does not choose the number of terms for me (the Fitting page says compare by mean slack at fixed temperature and watch resampled exponents); it does not explain the trades physically ("These are observations of the fit, not explanations"); it does not use requirement thresholds in the fit (they're read after); a certified status does not claim a global optimum, nor validity beyond the table; `conditioning_` tells me exponents may be weakly identified but the site gives no threshold; and the one-term fit carries no certificate at all beyond the solver message.
