## 6. What the fit told me
On the 300 synthetic designs with 3 metrics, the fit says all three metrics contend at the edge of the table. All three are in the contending set, which it calls a trilemma. The exponents are about 0.30, 0.37 and 0.33, so no metric dominates and the three are roughly equally weighted. The elasticities are close to -1 between each pair (about -0.8 to -1.24). Giving up roughly one unit, in relative terms, of one contending metric buys roughly one unit of another near the frontier.

The per-design score is mostly negative, meaning most designs sit inside the frontier. The mean is -0.67 and the worst is -8.8. A few designs are slightly positive (the max is +0.07), meaning just beyond the fitted surface. 108 of 300 are above zero. I did not check whether that is expected, for example whether it is the 0.0 entries of the planted frontier points. I did not check the planted answer in `info['exponents']` against the fit either.

## 7. What I believe the method does and does not do
Does:
- It learns, from a table of positive scored designs, a smooth outer boundary of the best combinations.
- It names which metrics are in tension and how steeply.
- It gives each design a signed "how far inside or beyond" number.
- It provides a reversible coordinate transform.
- It fits into sklearn-style code.

Does not (my understanding):
- It does not turn raw attributes into metrics for you. That is a separate conditioning step.
- It is not a prediction or optimization engine for new designs.
- It makes no claims on the page about uncertainty.
- It does not choose a design for you.
- It describes the data you give it, so gaps in the table limit what it can say. The site mentions "where the table has gaps" and Limitations and Termination pages, which I did not read.
