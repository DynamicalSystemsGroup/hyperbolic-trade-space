# Reader P1 on slice A

## 1. What this is about
A new way to analyse a trade study (the table of design options scored against competing goals). It fits a smooth reference surface to the data and uses it to show where the real tensions between goals lie. It is offered as an alternative to PCA, which only explains variance.

## 2. The main result, as I understood it
The method finds, for each set of competing metrics, how much of one you give up per unit of another, expressed in percentages. It also separates where a design sits along the surface from how far it sits above or below it. A cubesat example (power, mass, attitude control) shows it identifies the trade-offs.

## 3. What I would do next
Ask my lead analyst whether it could run on one of our own trade studies and whether the percentage trade-off figures would stand up in a design review. I would not commit budget yet.

## 4. One sentence for a colleague
A statistical tool that turns a trade-study table into a few plainly stated "give up X percent of this for Y percent of that" trade-offs, shown on one example satellite.

## 5. What the method does NOT do
It does not find the Pareto boundary or pick the best design. It does not do the conditioning of raw data into metrics. It does not appear to be validated beyond one example.

## 6. Where I was confused
"A fitted posynomial reference surface models the data underlying the table, not the Pareto boundary, and gives the data a coordinate system; the table and its reading are views." I could not follow this. Also "Modelling a single n-lemma fits a monomial, which transforms into a convex program and is solved directly."

## 7. Terms I guessed at
Posynomial, monomial, convex program, GGP, kernel, energy differential, n-lemma (I guessed several competing goals), elasticity.

## 8. Would I read on
Maybe. The promise of percentage trade-offs I could defend is attractive. But the abstract gives no number, no comparison with current practice and no cost of adoption, so I would skim the introduction and the example before deciding.
