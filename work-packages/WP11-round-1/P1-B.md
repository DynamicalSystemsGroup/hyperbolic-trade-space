1. What this is about.
A way to explain the trade-offs in a trade study, not just which variables vary most. You fit a reference surface to design data, then read off which metrics fight each other and by how much. The demo is a cubesat (power, mass, attitude control).

2. The main results, as I understood them.
The fitted surface is not the Pareto boundary. One trade-off fits as a tidy, solvable problem. Several trade-offs need a harder fit with graded guarantees. On the cubesat, separate facets split contending metric sets that a single facet blends.

3. Claims / does not claim.
Claims: reproducible, explains tensions, gives percent-for-percent exchange rates. Does not claim the surface is the true Pareto boundary, global optimality, or convergence. Search of the design space is marked preliminary.

5. One sentence.
It is a method that turns a pile of design data into a short list of which metrics trade against which, with exchange rates, demonstrated on a cubesat.
