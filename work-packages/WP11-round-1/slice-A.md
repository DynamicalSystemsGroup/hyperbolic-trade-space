# Scaling Decision Analysis with Hyperbolic Trade Space Identification

## Abstract

A trade study must produce an answer and a justification an accountable executive can follow. Principal component analysis (PCA) is trusted as reproducible and interpretable, but explains variance, not trade-offs. We explain tensions. A fitted posynomial reference surface models the data underlying the table, not the Pareto boundary, and gives the data a coordinate system; the table and its reading are views. A reversible chart separates a design’s position along the surface from its energy differential, its level relative to the surface. Each facet reports its contending metrics, an n-lemma (by analogy with dilemma and trilemma), with elasticities: the percentage of one metric given up per percentage gained in another. Modelling a single n-lemma fits a monomial, which transforms into a convex program and is solved directly; when several trade-offs are active at once the model is a posynomial, compatible with generalized geometric programming (GGP), and the method may be generalized to any kernel that keeps that compatibility. Our implementation mirrors the structure of PCA as an unsupervised learning method for feature engineering. On a cubesat model coupling power, mass and attitude control, the fit identifies the trade-offs among the metrics those subsystems set.
