Examples¶
Where the cubesat designs come from¶

The case is a fleet of identical 12U-class cubesats whose power, mass and attitude control are coupled, described by a SysML v2 system model. A configuration swaps the reaction wheel and the star tracker, tunes the panel span, the battery capacity and the controller gains Kp and Kd, and sets the fleet size; the slew is simulated and the model computes the rest. A row is one synthetic design. Two regimes of 3,000 designs differ in the sampled gains. The model and the data are in examples/adcs/:

uv run python examples/adcs/k_terms.py

Thirteen metrics are traded. Pointing error, unit cost and fleet cost are the stakeholders' measures of effectiveness; the other ten are measures of performance. Orientation -1 marks a cost, inverted. With four feasibility flags on settling, momentum, power and depth of discharge, the primary regime has 2,134 feasible designs of 3,000.

What the fit is asked¶

The stakeholders ask for a payload that points and a fleet they can afford. Near the frontier, do their three measures trade against one another and against the subsystems' measures, and does one trade describe the table, or two?

The one-term fit¶

At the fit temperature select_temperature picks for a tenth of designs above V = 1, the one-term trade-off report finds a contending set of seven metrics that trade off, a 7-lemma: unit cost, available power, pointing error, fleet cost, momentum headroom, settling time, depth of discharge.

Choosing K on the case¶

The choosing-K checks on both regimes, each at its K = 1 temperature (recipe).

import numpy as np
from hyperbolic_trade_space import HyperbolicTradeSpace


def fit_k(M, K, tau, seed=0):
    return HyperbolicTradeSpace(
        n_terms=K, temperature=tau, random_state=seed
    ).fit(M)


def sweep(M, tau, Ks):
    """One line per K at one temperature; returns the mean slacks."""
    slack = []
    for K in Ks:
        fit = fit_k(M, K, tau)
        t = fit.termination_
        print(
            f"K={K} slack={fit.mean_slack_:.3f}"
            f" thinnest={fit.term_share_.min():.2f}"
            f" {t.status} radius={t.radius:.0e}"
        )
        slack.append(fit.mean_slack_)
    print("drops", -np.diff(slack).round(3))
    return np.array(slack)


def movement(M, K, tau, draws=2, seed=0):
    """Largest distance to the nearest term of a refit on a resample."""
    ref = fit_k(M, K, tau)
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    moved = np.zeros(K)
    for s in range(draws):
        W = fit_k(M[rng.choice(len(M), len(M))], K, tau, s).exponents_
        d = np.abs(W[:, None] - ref.exponents_).sum(axis=2)
        moved = np.maximum(moved, d.min(axis=0))
    return moved.max()


studies = {r: case.load_study(r) for r in ("primary", "stock")}
taus = {r: case.fit_study(s, K=1).tau for r, s in studies.items()}
for r, s in studies.items():
    print(r)
    sweep(s.M, taus[r], range(1, 6))


The primary regime drops by 0.078, then 0.023, 0.012 and 0.005: a break at K = 2, where the thinnest term holds 0.28 (0.21 at K = 3). The baseline regime drops by 0.055, 0.031, 0.012 and 0.010, bending with no break, so that mean level cannot choose K; its thinnest term holds 0.35 at K = 2 and 0.13 at K = 3.

for K in (2, 3, 4):
    M, tau = studies["primary"].M, taus["primary"]
    print(
        K, np.round([movement(M, K, tau, seed=s) for s in range(3)], 2)
    )


Over three draws the primary regime's movement is 0.18 to 0.20 at K = 2, 0.15 to 0.29 at K = 3 and 0.14 to 0.95 at K = 4. The ranges overlap, so this check excludes none.

The case is read at K = 2. At the one-term temperature the designs' mean level below the surface falls from 0.122 to 0.043. At the two-term fit's own temperature, status certified, the terms have contending sets of 8 and 7 metrics and shares of 74 and 26 percent of the near-frontier designs. The larger adds peak slew power to the one-term contending set, and the smaller is that set.

A numbered marker is a term's exponent vector, where its V, the invariant value, is largest for a fixed total, not where V = 1. A region is where one term is largest: an analysis of the fit, not a label on a design.

What the fit says¶

In the thirteen-metric fit all three stakeholder measures contend, joined by two power and two control metrics; total mass and control effort do not. Two terms describe the primary regime. The baseline regime's set includes total mass, so the sampled gains decide part of it. Jointly, the six requirements pass about a quarter of the designs.

What the case cannot show¶

The designs are synthetic, the model a demonstrator, and the frontier that of the sampled box.

The synthetic three-metric case¶

The root page's two figures come from one constructed table, so the planted terms are known: three terms in 900 designs of three metrics.

The table. Seed 11. Most designs lie on or below the surface: 70 percent on it, the rest below it by an exponential depth of mean 0.25 in log V, and the surface rows scatter by 0.07 in log V.

    noise=0.07,
