# WP44: the Broadbalk wheat checks (research agent, Sonnet, 2026-11-14), as returned

All fetched 2026-11-14.

1. Title/version. Landing page https://www.era.rothamsted.ac.uk/dataset/rbk1/01-YLD6822 (DOI https://doi.org/10.23637/rbk1-yld6822-01 redirects to it): "Broadbalk Wheat annual grain and straw yields 1968-2022", Version 1.0, Glendining and Poulton (2023), publisher e-RA. The title you gave ("Broadbalk mean long-term winter wheat yields 1968-2022") is not this DOI. The closest sibling is "Broadbalk mean long-term yields of winter wheat 1852-2022", version 3.0, created 2023-05-19, DOI 10.23637/rbk1/meanWWYields1852-2022-03 (https://www.era.rothamsted.ac.uk/dataset/rbk1/03-OAWWYields). It holds multi-year means for six treatments, grain only, in t/ha at 85% dry matter. An older "mean long-term winter wheat yields 1852-2016" page (https://era.rothamsted.ac.uk/dataset/rbk1/01-OAWWYields) has DOI 10.23637/KeyRefOABKyields.

2. Licence, both pages: "This dataset is available under a Creative Commons Attribution Licence (4.0)."

3. Structure, 1968-2022 dataset: annual, per plot (strip x section). Strips 3-22, with strip 1 added in 1968 on Sections 2-7 only. Sections 0-9; 0, 1, 8 and 9 stay continuous wheat; 2-7 rotate wheat with potatoes, beans, oats, forage maize and fallow, and Section 6 reverted to continuous wheat in 1979. Year is the harvest year. Section 0 has had straw incorporated since 1986; Section 8 has never had herbicide. I could not verify the row count or plot count, because the page gives neither.

4. Columns: could not verify. The page lists no column names. It says grain and straw yields are both present, at 85% dry matter. The units and the column list are only in the xlsx (01-BKyld6822.xlsx) or the Frictionless CSV zip, which I did not download. Straw is the combine-cut part only (no stubble, most chaff excluded), and it "will also include weed material". "Straw has been recorded regularly for Section 1 and the first wheat in rotation since 1980, with some other sections in other years" (so straw is incomplete). Fallow years have blank cells. Grain nitrogen, protein and TGW are not stated as columns of this dataset. Siblings:
- Crop nutrient content, wheat 1968-2017, DOI 10.23637/rbk1-BKNUTRW-02 (grain and straw nutrient content, N included): https://era.rothamsted.ac.uk/info/datasets
- Grain quality BKGR_QUALITY, wheat 1974-2018, selected plots: TGW since 1974, Hagberg falling number and hectolitre weight since 1999, and "Wheat variety". The page gives no DOI. Protein is not measured; it is calculated as %N x 5.7. https://era.rothamsted.ac.uk/info/rbk1/grainquality
- Yields and N uptake, Section 1, 2001-2015: 10.23637/rbk1-yldS10115-01.

5. Treatments: the page says fertiliser N, P, K, Na and Mg in combinations plus FYM, and points to "Broadbalk Fertilizer and Manure Treatments 1852-2021" (10.23637/rbk1-FertTreats-02) for details. I could not verify the full list of N rates in this dataset. From the mean-yields pages: FYM 35 t/ha since 1843; PK(Mg)+144 kg N; FYM + 96 kg N from 1968 (144 from 2005); NPK plots up to 288 kg N/ha; Nil. Cultivars: the dataset page lists none. The mean page says "Modern short-strawed, high-yielding cultivars since 1968", with Hereward split 1996-2004 and 2005-2012, Crusoe highest yield in 2014, Zyatt 2022. Old varieties were grown until 1967. A year-by-year cultivar list was not verified.

6. Access: open to anyone but gated by a form. Click "Download Data", give name, organisation, organisation type, country and intended use, then "Agree and Download". Files are xlsx, a Frictionless zip of CSVs, a section-names PDF, and RIS/EndNote citation files. No sizes are shown. Queries go to era@rothamsted.ac.uk.

Story sources:
- Austin et al. 1980, "Genetic improvements in winter wheat yields since 1900 and associated physiological changes", Journal of Agricultural Science 94(3): 675-689. The correct DOI is 10.1017/S0021859600028665, confirmed on the Cambridge page (https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/identifier/S0021859600028665/type/journal_article). Authors: Austin, Bingham, Blackwell, Evans, Ford, Morgan, Taylor. My first guess, S0021859600063048, was a wrong paper and is not used.
- Shearman et al. 2005, "Physiological Processes Associated with Wheat Yield Progress in the UK", Crop Science 45(1): 175-185, DOI 10.2135/cropsci2005.0175a. The DOI redirects to Wiley, which returned 403, so title, journal and year come from search results only (https://agris.fao.org/search/fr/records/65dee3c90f3e94b9e5d2639c). Not fully resolved.
- Austin, Ford, Morgan, Yeoman 1993, "Old and modern wheat cultivars compared on the Broadbalk wheat experiment", European Journal of Agronomy 2(2): 141-147, DOI 10.1016/S1161-0301(14)80144-5, via https://repository.rothamsted.ac.uk/id/eprint/6402. At equal N rates the two cultivars gave similar shoot biomass, and modern Brimstone out-yielded Square Head's Master by about 52% in grain, attributed to its greater harvest index. That fits a fixed-biomass trade-off.
- e-RA's own documentation: the only pattern sentences are about short straw and yield, not a grain-straw trade-off: "Modern short-strawed, high-yielding cultivars since 1968" (https://www.era.rothamsted.ac.uk/dataset/rbk1/03-OAWWYields). I found no e-RA sentence stating that straw yield fell while grain rose. A search summary attributed a Rooney and Leigh 1993 claim of higher harvest index in shorter-strawed varieties since 1969 on Section 1. I did not open that source and could not verify it.
