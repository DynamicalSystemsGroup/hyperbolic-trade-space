# R2

## 1. What the pages are about

The Examples page walks through a cubesat trade study. Eight simulated attributes (settling time, pointing error, control effort, power margin, energy balance, peak slew power, depth of discharge, total mass) are turned into positive, larger-is-better metrics and fitted with `HyperbolicTradeSpace`. The fit is a frontier surface V = 1 in log-metric space, built from one or more terms. The page shows the one-term fit's "tension report" as a heatmap. It reads four threshold requirements after the fit, refits on three design sets, and draws a ternary plot of a synthetic three-metric, three-term fit. It closes by pointing to `examples/adcs/k_terms.py`, which fits one, two and three terms to each of two "regimes" (controller-gain settings).

The Chart page explains the coordinates a fit gives each design: log V, which is the offset from the surface, plus d - 1 position coordinates along it. It covers how to read V (as a factor of improvement), the near-frontier band |log V| < 1/tau, tools that look for holes or white space, and the six plots.

## 2. My answer: K = 1 for reading the tension report, with K = 3 run as a check

I choose **one term (K = 1)** as the fit whose tension report I would read and act on. I would keep the three-term fit as a secondary check that splits the frontier, not as the main report. Here is what that rests on.

- **The one-term report is readable and stable.** Figure 5 (adcs-tensions.png) shows a 5-metric contending set (outlined): total mass w=0.36, energy balance 0.18, pointing error 0.16, depth of discharge 0.16, settling time 0.14. Control effort, power margin and peak slew power are greyed with w=0.00. The report rests on 56 near-frontier designs (my `case.tension_table` run: `n_lemma 5, near-frontier 56`). `case.design_sets_summary()` prints the same five metrics for the primary regime whether the fit uses all designs (1000), the feasible ones (706) or those meeting the requirements (288). The page says this too: "Refitted on three design sets in one conditioning, the one-term contending set barely moves". A reading that survives changes to the design set is one I can carry into a design discussion.
- **More terms fit better, but nothing on the pages tells me when to stop.** At the one-term temperature the mean slack falls 0.243 → 0.132 → 0.088 for K = 1, 2, 3 (page bullet, reproduced by `case.mean_slack_fixed_tau`). The share of designs above V = 1 also rises at that fixed tau: 0.092 → 0.190 → 0.258 (`k_terms.py` DIAGNOSTIC block). So part of that drop comes with a looser fit to the target fraction, not a cleaner one. When tau is re-selected per K, the drop is smaller: 0.243 → 0.182 → 0.146. Neither page gives a criterion such as held-out score, an information criterion or an elbow for choosing K. Without one, I default to the simplest model.
- **The three-term report is thin on one term.** At the re-selected temperature (tau = 113.22), term 1 of K = 3 has only **3** near-frontier designs (`k_terms.py`: `term 1 rows 181 ... near-frontier 3 ... share 0.034483`). The example code's own guard needs ten (`thin_reason(3)` → "too few near-frontier designs for an association reading: 3 of ten"). The page's three-term numbers (contending sets of 5, 4 and 5 metrics; shares 17, 10 and 73 percent) come from the *fixed* one-term temperature. There the same term has 17 near-frontier designs. So the three-term picture depends on which tau you use.
- **What three terms add is still worth a look.** The page says one term "alone contains peak slew power" and another "alone contains control effort". This matches the run: K = 3 term 3 has peak slew power with exponent 0.612 and 73 near-frontier designs, and term 1 has control effort at 0.397. The one-term set is one of the three (term 2 at fixed tau, all five shared). The K = 3 split is identical over seeds 0-4 in the primary regime. I would treat that split as a pointer to two more trade regions to check against the physics. It does not replace the one-term reading.
- **Regime matters more than K.** The stock regime's one-term set is different (control effort, settling time, power margin, pointing error, depth of discharge). The page says "the finding depends on the regime". Picking the regime affects the reading more than picking K.

## 4. Where I was confused, in order

1. "a 5-lemma, found from the data, not chosen beforehand." I could not tell what a "lemma" is here. It reads like "a set of 5 metrics in tension", but the word suggests a proof. Figure 5's title adds "5 metrics in the exact-maximum support", a third name for what I took to be the contending set.
2. "With the fit temperature chosen by select_temperature for a tenth of designs above V = 1". I expected to learn why a tenth is the right target and how much the tension report depends on it. The pages don't say.
3. "At the one-term temperature (case.mean_slack_fixed_tau) the mean slack falls ... status certified." I didn't know what is being certified, or whether mean slack at a fixed tau is a fair way to compare K values. `k_terms.py` shows the share above V = 1 rising with K at that tau, and that confuses the comparison. I expected a sentence saying how to use this comparison to choose K. None was given.
4. "term shares of 17, 10 and 73 percent of the near-frontier designs". In the `k_terms.py` table, "term rows" and "share" are both listed. The first is cell rows, the second the near-frontier fraction. That took a recomputation (27/162 = 0.167) to confirm.
5. "No pair of the eight metrics reaches a rank correlation of 0.99 over the feasible designs". Figure 5 shows power margin vs energy balance at **0.99**, and the caption says the cells are correlations of log-metrics near the frontier. I worked out that the two numbers are over different rows and probably different measures (Pearson vs rank). On first read it looked like a contradiction. It also left me unsure why energy balance is in the set while power margin, correlated with it at 0.99, gets w = 0.00.
6. In Figure 5, the legend says "negative = tension", yet several pairs inside the contending set are near zero (pointing error vs settling time 0.03, pointing error vs depth of discharge 0.00). I couldn't tell what makes these five metrics "trade off" if their pairwise associations are not negative. The tension may be a joint property, not a pairwise one, but the page doesn't say.
7. "Requirements read after the fit ... a price at the one-term frontier, in band widths". I couldn't work out what a price of 7.92 bands means for control effort in the stock regime.
8. The Examples page names the column `objective_, equal here to mean_slack_`, plus gamma, radius, bound and gap G_k. It refers to "the termination page", which I was not given.

## 5. Terms I had to guess

- **Contending set**: the metrics with nonzero exponent in a term, which the frontier trades against each other. The figure calls these "the exact-maximum support". My guess is these are the metrics active in the tau = infinity (hard-max) fit.
- **n-lemma / 5-lemma**: a contending set of n metrics.
- **Term / cell**: one monomial in the posynomial frontier. A cell is the set of designs on which that term scores highest.
- **tau (temperature)**: how hard the soft maximum is. 1/tau sets the near-frontier band.
- **Mean slack / objective_**: the average log-distance of designs below the fitted surface.
- **Status certified, gamma, radius, bound, gap G_k**: some optimality certificate for the multi-term fit. The gap is near 1e-15, so the fit is solved to tolerance.
- **reach beta_k**: possibly how far a term's surface extends, or the term's offset. I can't tell.
- **Regime**: one controller-gain setting, which gives its own dataset.
- **Price, bands**: how much the frontier moves, in units of the band width 1/tau, when a requirement is imposed.

## 6. What I believe the method does and does not do

It does:
- fit a log-convex (posynomial) outer surface to strictly positive, larger-is-better metrics;
- score each design by log V relative to that surface;
- give reversible coordinates (position along the surface plus log V);
- report which metrics the frontier trades against each other (the contending set and its exponents) and how they correlate among near-frontier designs;
- split the frontier into several terms when asked;
- suggest holes or white space in the position coordinates as candidates.

It does not:
- condition attributes into metrics for you (the example uses LogAffinePreconditioner, and the page says this is the analyst's job);
- choose K for you (I found no criterion on either page or in the docstrings I read);
- see structure that is not log-convex (Chart page);
- explain why metrics trade off ("observations of the fit, not explanations");
- say whether a design beyond V = 1 is feasible or wanted;
- use requirements during the fit;
- tell an empty region that is an opportunity from one that cannot be occupied or has not been sampled.

## 7. Open questions and what I would open or run next

- Is there a recommended way to choose K: held-out mean slack, a per-term minimum count of near-frontier designs, or the stability of contending sets? I would look in the user-guide page on multi-term fits and on termination (not given), and in the `HyperbolicTradeSpace` docstring for `n_terms`.
- How sensitive is the one-term contending set to the 0.10 target? I would run `case.tau_sweep(study)` and refit at targets 0.05 and 0.2.
- Why does power margin drop out while energy balance stays in, when the two correlate at 0.99 near the frontier? I would print `case.association_table` and the exponents at tau = inf.
- I would hold out a fraction of designs and compare mean slack at K = 1, 2, 3 on the held-out rows. That would show whether the extra terms generalise or just absorb more designs.
- For my own table, I would fit K = 1 first, read its tension report, then fit K = 2 and K = 3. I would keep the extra terms only if each has at least ten near-frontier designs at the re-selected tau and its contending set is stable across seeds and design sets.
