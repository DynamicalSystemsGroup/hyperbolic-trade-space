## 1. What the pages are about

The Examples page walks through a cubesat trade study: eight attributes, 706 feasible designs in the primary regime. It fits a one-term and then a three-term "posynomial frontier" model (HyperbolicTradeSpace) and reports a "contending set" of metrics that trade off, with a tension heatmap (Figure 5). It also covers requirements read after the fit, a ternary plot on synthetic data (Figure 6), and how to run examples/adcs/k_terms.py. The Chart page describes the coordinates a fitted model gives each design (log V plus d-1 position coordinates), how to read V, how to find holes, and the plot functions.

## 2. Answer to the task

For the example's one-term fit, the fit did converge, but the status label does not say so. I know it from the numbers in the record, not from the label.

- fit.estimator.termination_ has status 'not_applicable', reason 'Optimization terminated successfully', n_iter=8, max_gap about 8.7e-6.
- 'Optimization terminated successfully' is the solver's own stop message. That is the direct evidence of convergence.
- The attribute's docstring in the checkout says that K = 1 records "not_applicable" with the gap of the fitted objective, read only. So the label is not a verdict.
- The residuals (sum_residual, floor_residual, offset_residual) are all 0.0, so the constraints hold.
- The docstring says "certified" means a fixed assignment, residuals within eps_feas, max_gap <= eps_kkt, no empty term and a positive margin. I could not apply that test to K = 1. Its max_gap of 8.7e-6 is above eps_kkt of 1e-8, which would fail it if it applied.
- For the three-term fit, status is 'certified', n_iter=14, max_gap about 4e-15, and the assignment is fixed. That matches the Examples page, which says "status certified". The two-term fit is also certified (n_iter 6, max_gap about 3e-15).

So: the one-term fit converged according to the solver message and the zero residuals. The gap is small but not within eps_kkt, and no certification applies at K = 1.

Passages relied on: the Examples line "status certified", the last paragraph ("A fit's record is fit.estimator.termination_, read on the termination page"), and the Termination docstring in the checkout (solvers.py) and the termination_ entry in the estimator docstring.

4. max_gap = 8.7e-6 against eps_kkt = 1e-8: the gap is about 870 times the tolerance, yet the solver says it terminated successfully. I cannot tell from the pages whether 8.7e-6 counts as converged. The docstring says only "read only".
5. gamma and radius are nan at K = 1, so the stability margin that K > 1 gets is absent.

## 5. Terms I guessed

- Certified: the several-term alternation reached a fixed assignment with stationary terms and a positive margin, not a global optimum. The docstring's "conditional local optimality" supports this.
- Gap (G_k): a first-order (KKT-style) stationarity residual per term. A guess from "eps_kkt".
- Bound: an upper bound on how far the objective is from the best objective for this assignment. It is not a bound against all assignments.
- Margin (gamma) and radius: how far the data could shift before rows change term.
- SLSQP: a constrained nonlinear optimizer.
- Soft fit and term_temperature: terms weighted rather than hard-assigned. Not needed for this task.

## 6. What the method does and does not do

It fits a log-convex (posynomial) frontier surface to strictly positive, larger-is-better metrics. It gives each design a V (a factor of improvement over the surface) and a reversible coordinate chart. It reports contending sets and exponents per term. It does not explain the physical reasons, treat hole candidates as verdicts, or see structure that is not log-convex. For several terms it certifies a local optimum of a fixed assignment, not the global best. For one term it reports the solver's stop and its gap but certifies nothing.
