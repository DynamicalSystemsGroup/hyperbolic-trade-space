## Figure 5 (request 36): the K = 1 plane of available power and unit cost

**Recipe (written first).** Question: does the one-term surface, drawn as a line in the plane of the two heaviest
metrics, bound the designs it is drawn for, and what is its slope. Intended takeaway: the line is the upper edge of
the designs and its slope is the elasticity, the ratio of the two exponents. Sentence of test 2: "In the plane of
available power and unit cost the K = 1 surface is a line whose slope is minus the ratio of the two exponents, and
the designs lie below it, about a tenth beyond."

**The readers on the figure before** (`figure-loop/fig5_before.png`, head c42d860):

- F12 (20): "most dark points sit visibly below the line, some by about one log unit on the vertical axis, and the
  caption's 'the line is the surface to within 0.1 in log V' is hard to square with that spread."
- G21 (43): "In the plotted coordinates they scatter about a full log unit around it."
- G24 (34): "the line looks like a regression trend through the cloud's upper-middle, not like an edge. The caption's
  'stop there' did not match what I saw."
- F05 (6): "Dividing by the unit-cost exponent 0.26, 0.1 in log V should be about 0.4 in the vertical axis."
- F13 (10): "I could not tell how the slice's slope relates to the exponent ratio 0.20/0.26."
- F02 (10): "The dark/light selection ... is complicated and I had to read it three times."

Nine cells read the figure; none read an edge from it. The cause is in the drawing: the line is the surface only for
designs whose other eleven metrics sit at the slice's median, so the drawing shows those designs in dark (a
selection within 0.1 in log V of the median) and leaves the rest light; a design's distance below the line is its
level over the unit-cost exponent (0.1 in log V is 0.4 on the axis), which the picture does not say and a caption
cannot fix, since the dark designs are not on the line by construction.

**Disposition: redraw** (a caption was the smaller change and was tried first in the nine readers' own words: three
rounds of captions for this figure, `WP11 CR-33` and wave 2's request, left the claim "they reach the line and stop
there" unreadable from the image). What changes: each design is moved vertically by what its other eleven metrics add
(`planes.adjusted_plane`), so the line is the surface for every design, a design above it lies beyond the surface,
and its distance below is log V over the vertical exponent. One grey, one line, no selection, no 0.1. What it
shows that the dark/light drawing hid: the cloud's upper edge runs along the line on the right and falls away from
it on the left (about 0.5 on the axis, 0.13 in log V). The test `test_the_adjusted_plane_puts_every_design_at_its_
level_from_the_surface` pins the identity (distance times exponent equals log V) and the tenth above.

**The five tests.** (2) Sentence test: the sentence above carries the slope, and the picture carries what the
sentence cannot, the shape of the edge: kept. (3) Ink: the frame is the data (2,134 designs and the line; the legend
two entries in the lower left over empty frame). (4) Legible at the column width (8 by 4.4 inches, 9 point type).
(5) Shows, does not show, good for: on the page beside the figure (the three lines of `fig-planes-k1-lines`).

**Verdict: passes tests 2 to 5 as judged here; test 1 on the redraw pending.**

## Figure 4's lower panel (request 56): the K = 1 exponent of each metric against the temperature

**Recipe (written first).** Question: which metrics carry the K = 1 exponent at each temperature of the sweep, and how
far does each move with it. Intended takeaway: eight metrics carry an exponent, five none; six move by at most 0.03
across the sweep, unit cost rises by 0.05 and total mass leaves the support as the temperature passes the fit
temperature, which sits in that transition. Sentence of test 2: "Five metrics carry no exponent at any temperature;
of the other eight, six move by at most 0.03 across the sweep, unit cost rises by 0.05 and total mass falls from
0.06 to zero between tau = 25 and 71, where the fit temperature 42 sits."

**The readers on the heat map** (`figure-loop/fig4_before.png`):

- G20 (27): "The cells at tau 3 to 25 are all near-black for the top rows, so I could not read how fast exponents change
  with tau."
- F21 (31): "a nonlinear colour scale (0.001, 0.1, 0.2, 0.28), which is hard to read; the numbers in the outlined column
  helped."
- F05 (4): "the exponent columns from tau = 3 to tau = infinity look almost identical."
- F21 (32): "the figure shows the exponents barely move between tau = 3 and tau = 1000, which was reassuring."
- G22 (6.8, triage): the exponents sum to 1.01 with the simplex unstated.

Three readers took "nothing moves" from the heat map; the data (`figure-loop/` table below) move: unit cost 0.225 to
0.274, total mass 0.064 to 0 (at 25 it is 0.049, at the fit temperature 0.021, from 71 on exactly 0). The colour scale
(linear, light grey to black, topping at the largest exponent) hid both. This is a failure of the figure to
convey what it shows, not a failure of a caption.

**Disposition: redraw** the lower panel as one line per metric (`case.tau_sweep_lines_figure`; the top panels are
unchanged; the heat map function stays for the model-view diagnostic pair). Rank is the vertical order, direct
labels name the lines, rings mark the fit temperature, the exponents sum to one (said in the axis label). The five
metrics that are at most 0.001 throughout are not drawn and the caption says so. The values at the fit temperature are
in Appendix D.3 (the heat map printed them in the outlined column; the caption's "Shows" line now says where they
are).

**The five tests.** (2) Sentence test: the sentence carries the finding; the picture is what shows that the fit
temperature sits where total mass leaves the support: kept. (3) Ink: eight lines in a 0 to 0.28 frame, labels in the
right margin; the vertical line and the rings are one colour. (4) 8 by 7 inches, 9 point type. (5) The three lines of
`fig-tau-sweep-lines` rewritten.

**Verdict: passes tests 2 to 5 as judged here; test 1 on the redraw pending.**

## Test 1, the cold read of the redraws (run by the assistant at 40a93f2; one fresh reader, Haiku, the two images alone)

Figure 4, in the reader's words: "how each metric's K = 1 exponent changes with fit temperature tau ... unit cost
rises from about 0.226 (tau 3) to about 0.257 at 42 and about 0.275 at 71, then stays flat ... Total mass falls from
about 0.05 at 25 to about 0.02 at 42 and reaches 0 at 71. Rings mark tau = 42 on each line." Against the sentence:
"consistent on the 0.1 crossing at 42, n_eff of 74, the eight non-zero metrics, the direction of unit cost and total
mass, and the position of 42." Not read: the five zero metrics ("do not appear anywhere"; the caption says they are
not drawn). Unclear: "the bottom x-axis is categorical, so spacing is by index, not by value ... slopes look different
from the values"; "the rings have no legend and depend on the title"; "the pointing and fleet rings overlap". The
reader's numbers differ from the sentence's by one hundredth in two places (the rise 0.04 from tau 25, 0.05 from tau
3; total mass 0.05 at 25), which is the reading precision of a line chart, and the figure "also shows that available
power and momentum headroom barely move, which the sentence does not say".

Figure 5, in the reader's words: "log conditioned available power (x) against log inverse unit cost, adjusted for the
other eleven metrics (y) ... a slope of roughly -0.77 by the axis endpoints. Points form a band that lies below the
line. The band is tight in the middle and widens at both ends ... the gap widening with power, which the sentence does
not say." Against the sentence: "the line shape and the 'below' placement are shown. The slope value, the ratio and
'about a tenth' are not shown." The reader saw "no point clearly above the line"; the drawing puts the designs with
V > 1 above it (the test `test_the_adjusted_plane_puts_every_design_at_its_level_from_the_surface` pins the tenth),
and at the figure's scale they sit within the grey band's upper fringe, so the tenth is in the caption and not
legible from the picture. Unclear: "the y-axis is inverse unit cost, while the sentence says 'unit cost'"; "the
y-label wraps to three crowded lines".

Verdict of test 1: both redraws are read as their sentences say (the edge and the slope; the rise and the drop and
where the fit temperature sits). Two notes go to the next unit as wording, not redraws: Figure 4's x-axis is a
category axis of the sweep's values and the caption should say so (the sweep is geometric); Figure 5's y-label can
lose a line ("inverse unit cost, adjusted for the other eleven metrics"). Nothing here returns the unit.
