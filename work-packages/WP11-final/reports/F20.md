## 1. What this is about

A method for reading a table of evaluated designs as a trade study. The table's metrics (larger is better) are log-transformed, and a posynomial is fitted to the outer edge of the data. The fit is a soft maximum over a simplex-constrained geometric mean, so it is one-sided and sits on the data's edge rather than through its middle. Each monomial term of the fit is read as a contending set of metrics with elasticities (an "n-lemma"). Each design gets a position along the surface and a level relative to it. The worked example is a cubesat model (power, mass, attitude control), with 3,000 random designs per regime, of which 2,134 are kept as feasible in the primary regime.

## 2. The main results, as I now understand them

- The fitted surface is the level set where the invariant value V is 1. It is not the Pareto boundary.
- With one term the fit is a convex programme in the weights. Its exponents are elasticities, and the log-chart is reversible.
- A maximiser of V with positive exponents is weakly Pareto-efficient. The converse is not claimed.
- With several terms the fit has no global guarantee. It has a descent result, a numerical stationarity measure (the term gap), and local optimality only where a margin is positive. A second-order certificate covers the soft fit.
- On the cubesat case, one term (K = 1) puts the trade on available power against unit cost. At K = 2 the fit splits into two terms, one led by peak slew power and pointing error, the other by settling time and unit cost. The K = 2 choice comes from an in-sample rule about the mean slack.
- Table 2 prices six decision rules. The paper says it does not answer the sponsor-versus-operator question.

I checked two readings by hand against the figures. The K = 1 line in Figure 5 has slope of about -0.78, which matches -w(power)/w(cost) = -0.20/0.26 from Figure 4. The K = 2 elasticity of -0.9 in the text matches -0.22/0.25 from Figure 7. Table 2's fleet costs equal fleet count times unit cost to within a few dollars of rounding.

## 3. What I would do next

- Test the K = 2 split out of sample. Hold out part of the 2,134 designs and see whether the two contending sets and the term shares replicate. As written, the K choice and the terms are both read from the same designs.
- Make the fleet handling explicit. Section 8 calls fleet size "no part and not drawn", but Table 2 treats it as a design variable with values from 1 to 25, and fleet cost is exactly fleet times unit cost. I would either sample fleet size as a variable and drop the product, or state that fleet cost is a post-hoc multiplier and remove it from the fit.
- Price the cheapest-unit row properly. It needs 25 satellites, and the paper says the launch price is flat. I cannot tell whether that means per unit or per launch. A 25-unit fleet is where that matters most.
- Replace the requirement thresholds. Section 8 says they are "read from the data's pass fractions, not a mission's". That makes the requirements partly a product of the data, so the feasibility screen cannot say much about a real programme. I would set them from a mission or a stakeholder document.
- Replace the illustrative hardware values with a datasheet set, as the paper itself lists as future work.
- Test the planted-table result on a case where the true answer is known from practice, such as a past programme with recorded decisions.
- Test the regressor on more than two distinct proposals. The text says three proposals, but the Discussion says two of them are one configuration.

## 4. What I would tell a colleague

It is a useful way to read a trade table. It shows which metrics trade against which, with exponents, and it puts every design on a common scale. The chart and the energy-differential reading are easy to use. Trust the algebra more than the case. The cubesat numbers are toy values that the paper labels as illustrative. The decision in Table 2 is a set of rules applied to in-sample fits, not a recommendation. The paper is clear that the method prices a question and does not answer it.

## 5. What the method does not do, and what the paper does not claim

The method does not give uncertainty on the exponents. The paper says so. It does not give a causal reading, and it does not check designs outside the table. It cannot choose K by out-of-sample error, and K is left to the analyst. It cannot say anything about schedule, risk, or the value of a larger fleet. It is limited to continuous variables within a category. It depends on the conditioning, which the paper says defines the question.

The paper does not claim priority or novelty. It does not claim to replace PCA or Pareto analysis. It does not claim the surface is the Pareto boundary (Q4 is open). It does not claim the fit holds outside the table, or that the hardware numbers are realistic. It does not claim the stock and primary regimes are the same problem.

2. Abstract and Section 1: "an n-lemma (by analogy with dilemma and trilemma)." "Lemma" is also used for the numbered results in Section 6 (Lemma 1, Lemma 2). "7-lemma" in Section 9 means a contending set of seven metrics. I read the coinage as a term for a set, not a result, and it confused me until Definition 6.

5. Section 1, "convex in the logarithmic variables": the decision variables are the weights w, and the convexity (Theorem 3) is in w. "Logarithmic variables" here reads as the log-metrics u, which is a different thing.

## 7. Terms whose meaning I had to guess

- Energy differential: log V, the level of a design relative to the surface. Guess: confirmed by Definition 2.
- Reach (beta_k): the soft-maximum score of the data in direction w_k, the offset. Guess: confirmed by Definition 7.
- n-lemma: the contending set of a term, named by its size. Guess: confirmed by Definition 6, though I read "lemma" as a result until then.
- Band: the near-frontier set |log V| < 1/tau. Guess: confirmed by Definition 4. "Band" is also used once for the regressor's own error (0.0935) and once for proposals (0.094), and I took those to be different things.
- Participation threshold: an exponent level that counts as "in" a term. The paper gives 10 to the minus 3 for participation and 10 to the minus 9 for contending-set support, and I guessed these are different thresholds for different purposes.
- Term share: fraction of near-frontier designs in a term's cell. Guess: confirmed by Definition 7.
- Cell: the designs whose largest term is that term. Guess: confirmed.
- Executives: the stakeholders (operator and sponsor) in Section 9. Guess: the paper's decision-maker, which the abstract's use does not match.
- Thin term: a term with a small share. Guess only.
- Tension: a trade-off between contending metrics. Guess from the figure captions.
- MoE and MoP: stakeholder-facing and subsystem-facing measures. Guess confirmed by the Section 8 text.
- Gap G-9 and the other gap codes: references to the ledger in Appendix E. I did not open that appendix and guessed they are open items.

## 8. The questions I am left with

1. Is fleet size a design variable, or a post-hoc multiplier on unit cost? (section 6, item 22)
2. Which participation threshold governs the counts reported in Figure 4 and the text?
3. Why does the text say the two stakeholders are the operator and the sponsor, and what does the word "executive" mean in the abstract's sense?
4. How many metrics are non-contending at K = 1: two (text) or five (Figure 4)?
5. How does the K = 2 split hold out of sample? The K choice and the terms both come from the same 2,134 designs.
6. Where does the 0.56 M figure come from? Which tau gives the 0.0935 regressor band?
7. Does "a flat launch price" mean per satellite or per launch, and what does that do to the 25-satellite row?
8. Are the requirement thresholds, which come from the data's pass fractions, adequate for a feasibility screen?
9. Which of the 37 near-seam designs fall into which term, and does that change the term-share numbers?
10. The sponsor-alone and operator-alone designs are above the K = 2 band. Is the K = 1 band used for the shortlist deliberate, and does the reader need to know?
11. Did the 4 planted-table failures and the 20-table test use the same generator as the case?

## 9. Whether I would go on to read the appendices

Yes, but selectively, and I could not open them here. I would read Appendix E first, because it is the gaps ledger the main text relies on for the 37-design seam issue, the K-band mismatch (gap G-9) and the four open questions. Next I would read Appendix D.15 (the rule for K) and D.19 (the shortlist rules and the "balanced" rule), because the decision in Table 2 depends on them. The main text says each proof has a script, and my concern is what the case shows, not whether the algebra holds, which I checked at two points.
