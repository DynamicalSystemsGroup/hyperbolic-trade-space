## 1. What this is about

A method and a worked example for turning a table of evaluated designs into something an accountable executive can follow. You have a table: rows are designs (configurations), columns are conditioned outcomes (metrics, strictly positive, bigger is better). The method fits a one-sided "reference surface" to the outer edge of that table. The surface is a weighted geometric mean of the metrics (the "invariant value" V, scaled so V = 1 on the surface). The weights are fitted, they sum to one, and they are read as trade-off exponents. A design's position along the surface and its level relative to the surface (log V, called the "energy differential") form a reversible chart. With one term (K = 1) the fit is a convex geometric program. With several terms (K > 1) it is a posynomial fitted by an alternating scheme, with local certificates only. Each term reports which metrics "contend" on it (the "n-lemma") and the percentage given up in one per percentage gained in another (elasticity).

The case is a 12U cubesat model (reaction wheel class, star-tracker grade, panel span, battery, two controller gains, fleet size) evaluated into 13 metrics. The paper reads K = 1 as a 7-way tension and K = 2 as two terms. It then reads six requirements after the fit and produces a shortlist of 28 designs and a closing question for the sponsor and operator. The paper is explicit that the method is not new in its parts, does not replace PCA or Pareto analysis, and that K > 1 convergence, consistency and the link to the true Pareto boundary are open.

## 2. The main results, as I now understand them

- The surface is a soft support of the data: about 10 percent of designs sit beyond it by convention. It is not the Pareto boundary and not a regression through the middle. Exponents are fitted; V doubles when every metric doubles (Theorem 1).
- One term: the fit is convex in the exponents, so every minimizer is global, with uniqueness not claimed. Exponents are elasticities, and the chart (position, log V) is an exact bijection with the log-metrics (Theorems 2 to 6). Conditioning that is log-affine pulls back exactly to the raw attributes (Theorem 7).
- A maximizer of V is weakly Pareto-efficient, and efficient if all exponents are positive. The converse is not claimed (Theorem 9).
- Several terms: the objective is not convex (an instance shows it). The scheme gives descent, a numerical stationarity gap, local optimality where an assignment margin is positive, and a second-order certificate for the soft variant. None of it is global (Theorems 10 to 12, Lemmas 1 and 2, Proposition 3). Four questions (global convergence, consistency, identifiability, recovery of the Pareto boundary rather than the sample envelope) are stated open.
- Case numbers, primary regime (aggressive gains): 2,134 of 3,000 random designs are feasible. Nondominated sets are 236 on four chosen metrics and 2,134 (all) on thirteen. K = 1 fit temperature tau = 41.7, band 0.024, effective sample about 74 designs. The 7-metric contending set is unit cost, available power, pointing error, fleet cost, momentum headroom, settling time and depth of discharge. K = 2 splits into term 1 (8 metrics, led by peak slew power 0.43 and pointing error 0.21, share 0.74) and term 2 (7 metrics, led by unit cost 0.25 and available power 0.22, share 0.26). K = 2 is picked from the drop in mean slack (0.08), a thinnest-term share, and resampling movement. The K = 2 elasticity between unit cost and available power on term 2 is about -0.9.
- Requirements are read after the fit (D.17). The six requirements (peak slew power, pointing error, control effort, total mass, unit cost, fleet cost) cut the 2,134 feasible designs to 548, of which 28 also lie in the near-frontier band of both fits. A subset of 5 of the 28 are nondominated on pointing error and cost. The paper says the method prices the closing question (is a fine star tracker worth about 0.56 M USD of a 6 M USD limit) and does not answer it.
- Propose, predict, check: 3 proposals (2 distinct), each predicted to reach the frontier and each did, but by spending the battery down to 22 Wh. All 3 fail the depth-of-discharge requirement (0.39). Presented as illustration, not a rate.
- PCA run on the near-frontier chart is offered as a composable downstream step ("a reading and not a result").

## 4. What I would tell a colleague

- It is a way of reading a trade table as a fitted model of its upper edge instead of as a ranking. The headline value is the named structure it gives (which metrics trade against which, at what rate, and how far inside the edge a design sits), together with local certificates and a story for how an analyst or auditor can check each step.
- The cubesat is a demonstration and says so. Hardware values are illustrative, there is no schedule, no qualification, no thermal or comms model, no mass or power growth, and the cost is a lower bound that excludes payload, comms, operations, environmental test and broker fees. Read it as a pipeline test, not as a design answer. The sponsor and operator question is posed in dollars, but the dollar side rests on invented curves.
- The method depends on how you condition and scale the metrics ("These choices define the question"). A stable exponent answers the question you posed with your scaling. It is not a discovery independent of it.
- The surface is not the frontier of what can be built. It is the edge of the sample you drew. Whether the real attainable edge is further out is explicitly open (Q4).
- The paper is unusually frank about what is proved (SymPy scripts, "business casual") versus observed versus open. It also discloses the use of an AI coding assistant.

## 5. What the method does not do, and what the paper does not claim

Method, from my reading:
- It does not select a design. It produces a model, a chart and exponents, and a shortlist only after the analyst applies requirements and rules (D.19). The paper says the method "does not tell the schedule or the risk, what to buy beyond the modelled parts, or what a larger fleet is worth."
- It does not estimate sampling error on exponents, does not give a causal exchange rate (a change in a variable need not reproduce the elasticity), and does not represent non-log-convex structure (dents and holes).
- It does not find the Pareto boundary. A maximizer of V is efficient, but the converse is not claimed.
- It does not choose K or the conditioning for you, and thin terms (few frontier rows) are not identified from the frontier rows alone (G-7).
- It does not predict (no `predict`). Prediction is a supervised regressor composed with the chart.
- It does not handle discrete design variables except as "a finite comparison" or a within-category continuous assumption.

Paper, explicitly not claimed: novelty of any component; priority; global convergence; statistical consistency; that tensions are orthogonal or that successive extraction works; that the fit holds outside the table; that hypotheses suit a given table (human validation); that the cubesat numbers are flight-like; a rate for propose-predict-check.

## 7. Terms whose meaning I guessed

- "energy differential" / "level": log V, a dimensionless offset from the surface. I took it as a weighted geometric-mean margin, with positive meaning beyond the surface. The paper says it is "neither a physical energy nor a Euclidean distance".
- "n-lemma", "contending set": the set of metrics with non-negligible exponent on a term. I guessed the support of an exact LP (a vertex-like set) at tau = infinity, which Definition 6 confirms.
- "reach" (beta): how far the data extend along a term's direction. I read it as the offset of the surface (the log-mean-exp of the scores).
- "support": two senses. "Support weights" (softmax weights over designs) and "support of the exponent vector" (nonzero entries). I guessed from context.
- "cell", "term share": the designs whose largest term is a given term; share is the fraction of near-frontier designs in it.
- "participation threshold" (1e-3): the exponent size at which a metric "participates", separate from "numerically zero" (1e-12).
- "balanced point": the dual weighted average of log-metrics at the optimum (R1). I do not know how a facet of the convex hull of rows is checked in practice.
- "one axis": two metrics with |Spearman| at least 0.99, summed together.
- "stable radius", "margin", "term gap": local-optimality bookkeeping. I read margin as the smallest winner-minus-runner-up score gap, radius as how far parameters can move before assignments change, term gap as a first-order stationarity measure.
- "novelty" V - 1: the relative improvement a design would need to reach the surface.
- "hole": a region of the chart with no designs.
- "near-frontier": within 1/tau of V = 1.
- "slack" / "mean slack": the surface minus the design's level, averaged; the K-selection objective.
- "ridge" as in "noise moves the exponents by a ridge": a Tikhonov-like penalty term in the objective. I did not read the proof.
- "MoE" / "MoP": measure of effectiveness (stakeholder-facing) and measure of performance (subsystem-facing); the paper defines them, and I knew them.

## 9. Questions I am left with

- How are the 3,000 designs drawn: uniformly in the box on each variable, on a log scale, or otherwise, and how does that choice move where the "edge" is? The paper says only "drawn at random" and that the method assumes nothing about it.
- With six design variables (and nine discrete wheel and tracker combinations) mapping into 13 metrics, the data live on a low-dimensional manifold in 13 dimensions. How much of the 7-lemma and the two-term split is a property of that low-dimensional structure and how much of a real tension?
- What does the surface say about the physics beyond what a table of the model's formulas would show? In particular, are the term cells largely the discrete hardware swaps (the paper says term 1's leaders are "stepped by the swaps of the tracker grade and the wheel class")?
- What do the exponents look like in raw units (the pull-back weights w_j alpha_j), and are the elasticities in raw units reported anywhere?
- Which fleet sizes below 4 are excluded from the shortlist, and does the unpriced launch-slot minimum change that?
- How sensitive is the closing 0.56 M USD question to the invented fleet-pricing curve and the unsourced 100 k USD assembly line?
- What is the regressor's band 1/tau = 0.0935 in the propose-predict-check, and how was the regressor trained?
- Which "five metrics" does the Discussion mean?
- Is the tau and band mismatch between Appendix E and D.2 a stale ledger or a different dataset?
- Why does D.17's table report "63.7 %" in the main text where it gives 65.6 % for the baseline regime?
