## 1. What it is about

A method for turning a table of designs (one row per design, several metrics per row, all strictly positive and larger-is-better) into a fitted "reference surface" in log space. The surface is a posynomial level set, V = 1, where V is a weighted geometric mean of the metrics scaled by a constant. For one term (K = 1) the surface is a hyperplane in log-metric coordinates and the fit chooses the weights w by minimising a log-mean-exp (soft maximum) of the scores w.u_i over the simplex. The paper then gives a chart (position along the surface plus a level offset), elasticities along the surface, an extension to K > 1 terms through a soft maximum over terms, an alternating algorithm for K > 1, and a set of guarantees graded as derived, numerical, or open.

## 2. The main results, as I now understand them

- The invariant value V(m) = exp(w.u - beta) is homogeneous of degree one in the metrics, and beta is a log-mean-exp, so the offset is a property of the data's extent along w (Definition 2, Theorem 1, Proposition 1).
- The one-term fit J_tau(w) is convex in w (Theorem 3). At tau = infinity it is a linear program; at finite tau it is a geometric program in x = exp(w). The KKT conditions on the simplex are Theorem 2.
- The chart (Z, log V) is a bijection with the log-metrics (Theorem 6), and under log-affine conditioning the pull-back to attribute units is exact (Theorem 7). Along the surface the elasticities are -w_i/w_j and the improvement factors are V^(-1/w_j) or 1/V (Theorem 5).
- For K > 1 the model is the posynomial extension Phi_K, with max_k x_k <= Phi_K <= max_k x_k + (log K)/kappa (Theorem 8). A maximiser of V over a set is weakly Pareto-efficient, and efficient when every weight is positive (Theorem 9). I checked the direction of the dominance argument in Theorem 9 and the bounds in Theorems 4 and 8; they hold as stated.
- For the alternation: each accepted step does not raise the mean slack (Theorem 11); the term gap bounds the gap to the term's minimum (Lemma 1); within a stable radius the assignment is fixed and the fit is a local minimiser of the slack, conditional on exact term minimisers (Lemma 2, Proposition 3); a soft fit is a strict local minimiser under a second-order test (Theorem 12).
- Noise: the Gaussian cumulant (Proposition 2) and a first-order shift of the exponents by a ridge-type term (Corollary 1), explicitly not a claim about the rates.
- Open (6.3): convergence of the alternation, statistical consistency, identifiability of active metrics, and when the fitted surface recovers the Pareto boundary rather than the supporting envelope.

## 4. What I would say to a colleague

The construction is clean where it is a one-term problem: the fit is a convex programme, the offset is a log-mean-exp, the chart is an orthogonal change of coordinates, and the elasticities are read off the hyperplane. The several-term part is honest about its limits: the alternation is non-convex, the paper says so, and the guarantees are local and conditional. The weak point is the wording around certificates. Several things called "certified" are tolerance checks, and the theorem that would make them certificates needs exact first-order conditions. Read the 6.2 and 6.3 sections with that in mind, not as a global result.

## 5. What I believe the method does not do, and what the paper does not claim

From the text itself:
- It does not establish a global minimiser for K > 1; Q1 (global convergence) is open and "not pursued".
- It does not establish stationarity of a returned several-term fit or convergence of the alternation beyond finite termination under unstated strictness (see point 6, Theorem 11).
- It does not establish statistical consistency under noise or refitting (Q2), identifiability of the active metrics under finite biased data (Q3), or that the fitted surface is the Pareto boundary rather than the supporting envelope (Q4).
- It does not claim that the cells, the contending sets or the term shares are stable under noise; the text says the contending sets hold under rounding-level jitter only.
- It does not claim that the surface's level is a property of the attainable set; Definition 1 says the Pareto boundary is that property and the fitted surface is a different object.
- It does not claim that tau = 10 percent is optimal; it calls it a convention.
- It claims weak efficiency for maximisers, not the converse.

## 6. Points where I was confused, stopped, or met something imprecise (in reading order)

1. Definition 1, "The fitted reference surface is V = 1 at finite tau, where the posynomial equals one". V is not a posynomial at K = 1 (it is a monomial), and the sentence should say V equals one.
2. Definition 2, "the reach beta (Definition 7)". beta is written without an index here, but Definition 7 defines beta_k for each direction w_k. At K = 1 the two coincide; the text should say so.
3. Definition 2, "A difference of levels is the same along every path between two designs." This holds for any function of the design, so it adds no mathematical content, and the analogy with a potential is doing the work. I would want the sentence to say only that log V is a scalar function of the design.
4. Definition 3, "c_k = e^(-kappa beta_k) is the 1/kappa power of a posynomial". This is garbled. What is meant is that V_K is the 1/kappa power of the posynomial sum over terms. As written it states that a constant is a power of a posynomial.
5. Definition 3, "V_K ... is the 1/kappa power of a posynomial" together with the package name "posynomial". At finite kappa, V_K is not a posynomial; the posynomial is e^(kappa Phi_K) (Theorem 8). The title and the definition use the same word for two objects.
6. Definition 3, "The package's mean (kappa = infinity: the maximum) subtracts the constant (log K)/kappa ... and changes neither exponents nor minimizers." "Mean" here means a soft maximum, and in Section 5 "log-mean-exp" is a different object. I did not find the sign convention stated; the bounds in Theorem 8 suggest it is the soft maximum minus (log K)/kappa, which lies between max - (log K)/kappa and max. The sentence should give that formula.
7. Definition 4 and the band: I checked that inside |log V| < 1/tau a design weighs within a factor e of a design on the surface. Consistent.
8. Definition 6, "the support (exponents above 10^-9)". Section 5 gives 10^-3 as the participation threshold and 10^-12 as numerically zero, and Definition 6 also says a member below the participation threshold "contends in the limit". The gap between 10^-12 and 10^-9, and the relation between the three thresholds, is never stated.
9. Definition 6, "the n-lemma". The name is used for a set of contending terms, named by its size. The word "lemma" in a mathematical text means a result, so the reader is first told this is a definition and then has to remember it is a count. Equation (2) is also cited here before it appears (Section 6.2).
10. Figure 1 caption and legend (checked as a screenshot). The three term vectors are on the simplex and sum to one, term 2 is interior with all weights positive, term 1 has w = (0, 0.35, 0.65) and lies on the edge between metrics 2 and 3, term 3 has w = (0.70, 0.30, 0) and lies on the edge between metrics 1 and 2, consistent with the caption. The caption says the figure is for "the case's association maps", which is never defined in the text I read. The figure says nothing about designs, which the caption also says.
11. Definition 7, "The reach beta_k is how far the data extend in direction w_k." beta_k is a level, the soft maximum of the scores; "how far" does not match a log-level. The term share is "the fraction of near-frontier designs in the cell", which depends on the band; the band is defined in Definition 4 only through |log V|.
12. Definition 8, the elasticity d log m_j / d log m_i = -w_ki / w_kj. This is only a derivative along the surface and only for K = 1. Theorem 5 states that hypothesis, Definition 8 does not.
13. Definition 9 says the normal w_k is "the ascent direction, ... the gradient of log V in u". Correct as a gradient, but the tangent space {v: w_k . v = 0} is not the sum-zero tangent space of the chart; the chart uses both constraints. Minor.
14. Section 5, R1. The "balanced point U^T p" uses p, the "optimal dual weight vector", which is not defined here, and "a facet ... whose normal is positive" is not explained; I could not tell which normal is meant. Theorem 2 later says it uses "R1's relation at the fit temperature" in the hypothesis "for the facet-normal reading, w_j > 0 for every j there", pointing to an appendix. R1 is therefore a condition used in a theorem whose listed hypotheses do not include R1.
15. Section 5, R2. Uses k(i), defined only in Section 6.2 as the argmax label. A forward reference inside a condition.
16. Section 5, R3. "At the clean fit" is not defined. "The softmax-weighted covariance C" is not defined in Section 5; I took it from Theorem 3 and Corollary 1 to be the p-weighted covariance of the log-metrics. I could not tell what "clean" means.
17. Section 5, "At K = 1 the frontier is the unit level set of V." Definition 1 says the word frontier names three different objects. The sentence does not say which of the three it means. I read it as the fitted reference surface.
18. Equation (1) and "the factor 1/N makes it a property of the shape of the data, not the number of designs (Theorem 4)." The 1/N makes the log-mean-exp unchanged under replication of designs. "Shape of the data" does not say that; the sentence should give the replication property.
19. Section 5, "tau puts a target fraction of designs above V = 1: 10 percent here". The paper gives no map from tau to a fraction, so the reader cannot check that 10 percent is what tau = 10 percent produces, or reproduce the choice. The claim is about a convention and should be stated as one with its formula.
20. Section 5, "Conditioning (stage 2) ... a scaler equalizes log-spread so one tau serves every metric." "Scaler" is not defined, and the reason one tau serves every metric is not given. "Rescaling one metric changes which term is tangent" is unclear: no term is tangent to anything in the text before this.
21. Section 5, "A row with a zero metric is left out of the fit, and N counts the other rows." This is a data-handling step that changes the population of the fit and does not appear in the assumptions, which require strictly positive metrics. The consequence for the claims is not stated.
22. Section 5, "The estimator ... prediction composes a supervised regressor with the chart." No regressor is named and no claim is attached; I read this as scope rather than a result.
23. Section 5, Simplifying assumption (ii), "kinks on the ridges". "Ridge" is used here for the locus where the maximum switches, and in Corollary 1 for the regularising term from noise. The two collide.
24. Section 5, Simplifying assumption (iv), "the case needs three terms". Section 6.2 gives "At K = 2 the margin ...", and Theorem 12's paragraph says the case is certified "at K = 2 and 3". The text does not say how K is chosen, so I cannot tell which K is the case's.
25. Section 5, Simplifying assumption (vi), "A log-convex envelope: Phi_K is convex in u; no K represents non-log-convex structure." The second clause is a limitation stated as an assumption. "Envelope" is also the word Definition 1 uses for the tau = infinity fit, a different object.
26. Section 6 introduction, "Derived means proved from its hypotheses by a plain-SymPy script the tests run". A symbolic check of a specific instance is not a proof of the general statement for arbitrary d. The sentence must say which: the script proves the statement for all d, or it checks instances.
27. Theorem 1, proof sketch: "A1 gives V = exp(w.u)". Definition 2 gives V = exp(w.u - beta). The sketch drops the constant.
28. Theorem 2, hypotheses: "for the facet-normal reading, w_j > 0 for every j there (R1's relation at the fit temperature, Appendix B, L3.9)". A hypothesis deferred to an appendix pointer, in phrasing I could not parse on first reading. The paragraph after the theorem is clear that R1 is a statement about the exact-maximum geometry and does not imply that the finite-tau minimiser is interior. I appreciated that caveat.
29. Theorem 5, hypotheses list A1, A2, w_j > 0. The statement "the fitted reference surface {V = 1} is the hyperplane {w.u = beta}" is only true for K = 1, which is not in the hypotheses.
30. Theorem 6, Q = [B; 1^T/d]. I read the last row as 1^T divided by the square root of d, which is what orthogonality needs. The rendering shows d only; check whether the root was lost in rendering.
31. Theorem 9, hypotheses list only A2. The statement needs V and log V, which need A1 (positivity). Minor.
32. Corollary 1, ridge and power of tau. The statement says the noise "adds the ridge tau^2/2 w^T Sigma w" to J_tau. Proposition 2 gives (1/tau) log E exp(tau w.eps) = tau sigma^2 ||w||^2/2, which is first power of tau. The closed-form shift in Corollary 1 has no tau in it, which is consistent with the first power and not with a tau-squared ridge. I read the exponent as tau^2; if the rendering misread it, the sentence needs checking, and if not, the ridge is off by a factor of tau.
33. Corollary 1, hypotheses cite A5 and A9 together. A5 gives isotropic noise, independent N(0, sigma^2) in log space; A9 gives a general covariance Sigma. Both cannot be the operative noise model, so the hypotheses are either inconsistent or redundant. "Noise small" is also not quantified.
34. Corollary 1, proof sketch: "The expected noisy objective is J_tau plus the ridge". For independent noise on N designs, the expectation of a log-mean-exp is not the log-mean-exp plus a cumulant term; Proposition 2 is for one scalar. The first-order approximation should be stated as the claim.
35. Section 6.2, "Refitting a term on its own rows alone ... is ill-posed (observed on planted data, Appendix D.13)". Ill-posedness is a mathematical property, and the sentence presents it as an observation. Either the property is proved (and then it belongs to a theorem) or it is an observed symptom (and then it should not say "ill-posed").
36. Section 6.2, "On centred log-metrics, as the case's conditioning makes them". The conditioning described in Section 5 does not centre anything; it applies log-affine maps with shifts and scalers. The sentence assumes a step not yet described.
37. Section 6.2, the mean slack F(W) = -(1/N) sum Phi_K(u_i), and equation (2). I could follow the algebra for the sharp labels. Equation (2) with the cell means is consistent with that; the reader needs the sentence "the labels are the argmax of s_ik" to be stated before equation (2).
38. Theorem 10, part (i). The argument is: a convex symmetric function has a symmetric minimiser, and at a particular symmetric point the fit's objective is higher than at a distinct-term point, so the objective is not convex. One symmetric point being beaten does not rule out a different symmetric point being a minimiser. The statement needs "every symmetric point", and "symmetric point" needs a definition.
39. Theorem 10, part (ii). "The soft model's slack is bounded above by the fixed-weight objective". The fixed-weight objective is not written, and the entropy term that makes the bound work is absent from the text. I could not check the bound.
40. Theorem 11. One accepted iteration "does not raise" F, and the conclusion is finite termination. Non-increase alone does not give finite termination: the iteration could cycle among equal values. The theorem needs the strict decrease that the appendix's unique minimisers and tie rule are meant to supply, stated in the theorem.
41. Lemma 1, titled "Level 2, numerical stationarity: the term gap", but labelled Derived. The inequality is derived; the word "numerical" in the title refers to its use. The mixed label is confusing.
42. Lemma 2. I checked the logic of 2 delta < gamma and the radius r = gamma / (2 max_i ||(u_i, -1)||). Consistent.
43. Proposition 3. The local optimality is conditional on exact minimisers of each f_k. The solver does not produce exact minimisers; the paper says so for the stopping declaration, which is the place the reader needs it.
44. Corollary 2 and the stopping declaration. The paragraph says "Corollary 2 bounds the remaining improvement". Corollary 2 bounds F(W) minus the least slack with the cells held fixed. Changing the cells could improve F further, and the corollary says nothing about that. The paragraph overstates the corollary.
45. Theorem 12. The sign in H = D - kappa M is not reconstructable from the text: the derivative of Phi_K gives a covariance with positive sign, and I could not see where the minus comes from. "The designs' ambiguity between terms" is a gloss, not a definition of M. "Positive-multiplier bounds" is not defined.
46. Theorem 12 requires first-order conditions "exactly, not within a tolerance". The stopping declaration applies it to soft fits that meet the conditions only within tolerance. The hypothesis and its use disagree.
47. Stopping declaration. "Certified" is used for a tolerance-based status. Elsewhere the paper says a tolerance-based status is "measured, not proved". The word "certified" for a tolerance check invites the wrong reading. "Stationary that ... the curvature is not positive" is unclear.
48. "On the primary regime the fits of record are sharp; the solver declares the status certified at K = 2 and 3, with the largest term gap below 1e-12." "Primary regime" is not defined. A term gap below 1e-12 is a numerical threshold; under the paper's own taxonomy it is numerical, not certified.
49. "At K = 2 the margin is 0.0003768 and the stable radius 2.552e-05, against a band 1/tau of 0.0088 ... a margin below the band puts at least one design within the band of a seam". The band is defined in Definition 4 as |log V| < 1/tau, a level band around the surface. The seam is a boundary between cells, and the margin is a difference of term scores. The paragraph compares a score difference with a band in log V and calls the result "within the band of a seam", which needs a definition of the band for seams. The count "37 of the 2,134" has no criterion in the text. The arithmetic is consistent: 0.0088 divided by half the margin is about 47, which matches the text.
50. "For the two-term split: the local optimality certified covers changes of the term scores under half the margin". This is Lemma 2's 2 delta < gamma; the sentence is correct but "certified" is the same overloaded word as in point 47.
51. Section 6.3, Q1 "global convergence of the alternation, not pursued". "Open" and "not pursued" are different statuses; the reader cannot tell whether Q1 is a planned item or dropped.

## 7. Terms whose meaning I had to guess

- reach (beta_k): the offset, a log-level of the data's extent in direction w_k. (Definition 7)
- term share: fraction of near-frontier designs whose cell is term k.
- energy differential: log V, a level relative to the surface, not an energy.
- chart: the pair (Z, log V), with Z the position along the surface.
- n-lemma: the contending set of a term, named by its size; corner, edge, interior correspond to 1, 2, 3 metrics.
- contending set: the support of the exact-maximum one-term fit to the term's cell.
- supporting envelope: the tau = infinity fit.
- Pareto boundary: the nondominated set of the attainable set, a property of the system, not of the table.
- mean slack F(W): the average over designs of (surface level minus design level), which is -Phi_K averaged.
- band: |log V| < 1/tau. For seams I guessed its meaning, which the text does not define.
- seam: a boundary where two term scores tie.
- cell: the designs whose largest term is k.
- sharp / soft weights: sharp is one-hot at the argmax, soft is softmax at kappa.
- fit temperature tau and term temperature kappa: the sharpness of the soft maximum over designs and over terms.
- generalized posynomial: a sum, product, or maximum of posynomials, in the sense of Boyd et al.; I did not check this against the cited source.
- participation threshold: 10^-3.
- clean fit, softmax-weighted covariance, positive-multiplier bounds, balanced point, dual weight vector: guessed as noted in part 6.
- scaler: a per-metric scaling step, as in scikit-learn.
- mean (in "the package's mean"): a soft maximum at finite kappa.
- hypothesis codes (A, R, F, I, and I-numbers such as I14): I took them as labels for assumptions, conditions, fallbacks, and inherited results, resolved only in the appendices.
- primary regime: the main configuration of the case, undefined.
- the case: the cubesat study, which I took from the pointer in Section 3.
- ill-posed: as used in 6.2, undefined as a property.
- certified / stationary (status words): tolerance-based labels, as noted in part 6.

## 8. The questions that remain for me

1. Is the ridge in Corollary 1 tau-squared or tau, and does the closed form agree with it?
2. Which noise model is in force, A5 (isotropic) or A9 (general covariance)?
3. What are the thresholds, and how do 10^-3, 10^-9 and 10^-12 relate?
4. Which K is the case's K, and why does the simplifying assumptions text say it needs three terms when the results are given at K = 2?
5. How does tau = 10 percent map to the 10 percent of designs above the surface?

## 9. Whether I would proceed to the appendices, and why

Yes, but a targeted reading, not the whole of them. The body defers most of its hypotheses and several of its numbers to appendices: the hypothesis codes (I14, I15, I16, L-numbers such as L3.9, L5.25, L15.17 to L15.22, L17.19), the conditioning rule (C.4), the numerical records (D.5, D.8, D.11, D.13), and the symbol register (A). I would read Appendix A first, because several terms above have no definition in the body. Then Appendix E, the gap ledger, because the body says it records the open items and the bridging assumptions between the map and the territory, and I want to see whether it lists the points in part 6. Then the specific L-entries behind Theorems 10 to 12 and Corollary 1 for the power of tau and the noise model. I would not read the case study or the supporting material beyond those pointers, because the paper's own taxonomy puts the case in the numerical category and the claims I am worried about are in the derived and conditional ones.
