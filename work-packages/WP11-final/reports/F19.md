# F19
Head: c23cf60

## 1. What this is about

A method for trade studies: a table of design alternatives scored on several metrics, from which an accountable person has to pick and justify a choice. The paper proposes fitting a posynomial "reference surface" to the data, not to the Pareto boundary, and then reading the table and a chart off that fitted surface. Each monomial term in the fit is said to name the metrics that contend with one another (an "n-lemma") and to report the trade-off between them as elasticities. The abstract also places the method next to PCA, and gives a cubesat example (power, mass, attitude control).

## 2. The main results, as I understand them

- A fitted posynomial surface, fitted to the outer edge of the data and allowed to be exceeded by a few designs. The abstract calls this a "soft support" and contrasts it with regression.
- A chart that separates two quantities for each design: its position along the surface, and its "energy differential", which is its level relative to the surface, named by analogy with a potential.
- Monomial terms with elasticities as the trade-off readout, one n-lemma per active trade-off.
- A monomial fit is a convex program solved directly (through geometric programming). A posynomial with several active trade-offs is handled by generalized geometric programming (GGP).
- A claim that the approach is unsupervised feature engineering and composes with PCA, because PCA can be run on the chart.
- A cubesat demonstration in which one term blends subsystem trade-offs into a single tension and two terms separate the contending sets.

The abstract states these as results. It gives no theorem, no error analysis and no comparison numbers, so I take them as claims for the body of the paper to support.

## 3. What I would do next with it

I would go to the fitting procedure and the cubesat section, in that order. I would want the loss or constraint used for the outer-edge fit, the way the "soft" tolerance is chosen, and the same data run through a standard frontier estimator (DEA envelope, stochastic frontier, or a log-linear envelope) to see what is different.

## 4. What I would tell a colleague

On the abstract alone: the monomial core looks like a log-linear frontier fit with a smooth envelope, and the trade-off readout looks like the elasticities that production-frontier work has reported for a long time. The geometric-programming step for a monomial is a known result. What the abstract adds beyond those is the framing (n-lemma, the two-view chart, the composition with PCA) and the soft outer-edge fit. Whether the soft fit is substance depends on what it does with noise and dominated points, which the abstract does not say. I would not yet call the difference substance or vocabulary.

## 5. What the method does not do, and what the paper does not claim

From the abstract: it does not claim superiority over DEA, stochastic frontiers or regression, and gives no comparison. It does not claim any statistical property (consistency, a confidence region, a sampling distribution for the fit or for the elasticities). The GGP extension is stated conditionally ("any kernel that keeps that compatibility"), so the general case is not claimed as done. The paper does not claim that PCA is replaced; it claims the two compose. It does not state how many designs may exceed the surface, and does not say how a dominated or inefficient design is treated differently from a noisy one.

## 6. Where I was confused, stopped or found something imprecise (in the order I met them)

- "Principal component analysis (PCA) is trusted as reproducible and interpretable, and explains variance. We explain tensions." I could not see how "explain tensions" follows from the PCA sentence before it. It reads as a jump, and I did not know what "tensions" refers to until the cubesat sentence later.
- "A fitted posynomial reference surface models the data underlying the table, not the Pareto boundary, and gives the data a coordinate system." I expected a frontier. I could not tell what the "coordinate system" coordinates, or whether it is the surface's own parametrisation or a re-expression of the design variables.
- "It is fitted to the outer edge of the data, not through its middle: a soft support that a few designs may exceed, not a regression." Two things. "A few" is not quantified, and a surface that designs may exceed is no longer an envelope in the usual sense, so I could not tell what it is bounding. I also wanted to know how "soft" is measured, since the sentence contrasts with regression without saying which loss it uses.
- "A reversible chart separates a design's position along the surface from its energy differential: its level relative to the surface, by analogy with a potential, not a physical energy." "Reversible" was undefined to me. I did not know whether it means the chart can be inverted back to the metrics or means something about the two axes. The double analogy (potential, then "not a physical energy") told me the quantity is named for its form and not defined in the abstract.
- "Each monomial term (a product of powers of the metrics) reports its contending metrics, an n-lemma (by analogy with dilemma and trilemma), with elasticities". "Contending metrics" is not defined here. I could guess it means the metrics with non-zero exponents, but that is a guess, and I stopped on the coined word "n-lemma" as well.
- "the percentage of one metric given up per percentage gained in another." This is the standard trade-off elasticity. I could not tell whether it is evaluated at the fitted surface or at the data, and for a monomial it is constant, so I wondered why it is reported per term and not per design.
- "Modelling a single n-lemma fits a monomial, which transforms into a convex program and is solved directly". The sentence does not say what is fitted (least squares on logs, a maximum-likelihood form, or a geometric program with a stated objective). "Transforms into a convex program" is the step I most needed to see written down.
- "when several trade-offs are active at once the model is a posynomial". I stopped on "active". I could not tell whether activity is decided from the data, from a constraint at the optimum, or from a threshold.
- "any kernel that keeps that compatibility generalizes the method." "Kernel" could mean a kernel in the machine-learning sense or a kernel in the sense of a monomial-sum structure. "Compatibility" is not defined in the abstract. I could not work out what property is preserved.
- "Like PCA, our method is unsupervised feature engineering; the two answer different questions and compose, as when PCA is run on the chart." I could not tell what PCA is run on: the chart's coordinates, the design table, or the energy differential. "Feature engineering" as a framing for a surface fit was the point where I expected the claim to become concrete, and it did not.
- "one term blends the subsystems' trade-offs into a single tension; two terms separate the contending sets." Here "term" appears to mean monomial term, but I was unsure, given the earlier sense of a term as a summand of the posynomial. I could not tell from the sentence what makes one term "blend" and two terms "separate".

## 7. Terms whose meaning I had to guess, with my guesses

- Soft support: a smooth upper envelope that a small share of points may lie above. My guess, not confirmed.
- Energy differential: a signed distance or log-distance from a design to the fitted surface. My guess, by the analogy the abstract gives.
- Reversible chart: a chart from which the metrics can be recovered, or a chart that can be read in either direction. Low confidence.
- n-lemma: a named trade-off among n metrics, identified with the active monomial. Guess.
- Contending metrics: metrics with non-zero exponents in a term. Guess.
- Kernel (in "any kernel that keeps that compatibility"): a transformation of the metrics that preserves the posynomial form. Low confidence.
- Compatibility: preservation of the form needed for GGP. Low confidence.
- Coordinate system (as given to the data): the surface's own parametrisation of the designs. Low confidence.

## 8. Questions I am left with

- How is the outer-edge fit computed, with what loss, and how is the softness set?
- How many designs may exceed the surface, and is that fraction a parameter or an output?
- Against the same data, how does the fitted surface compare with a DEA envelope, a stochastic frontier, and a log-linear envelope? Is the monomial case the same estimator as one of these under another name?
- How does the method treat noise, and does it change when the data are rescaled?
- What does the energy differential equal in numbers, and is it defined on the design or on the surface?
- Are the elasticities uncertain? The abstract gives no interval or variability.
- What does PCA run on the chart actually produce, and is the composition tested anywhere?
- Is the cubesat demonstration a check against a known answer, or a worked example?

## 9. Whether I would read further, and why

Yes, but with a specific question. The abstract makes a clear set of claims (fit to the edge, two-view chart, convex monomial core, GGP for posynomials, composition with PCA) and a relatively clear method. What it does not show is whether the soft outer-edge fit does anything a standard frontier estimator does not, and I cannot judge that from the abstract. I would read the fitting section and the cubesat section first, looking for the loss, the tolerance and any comparison with a frontier estimator on the same data. If those are absent, I would take the framing as the contribution, and I would say so.
