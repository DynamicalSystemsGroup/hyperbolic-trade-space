## 1. What it is about

The paper proposes a method, hyperbolic trade space (HTS), for explaining trade-offs in a dataset of designs and their measured performance on real-valued attributes. The stated aim is a trade study whose answer and justification an accountable decision-maker can follow. HTS is presented as an unsupervised method, in the same family as PCA: where PCA fits a subspace and projects the data onto it, HTS fits a surface one dimension below the data and places each design on it, with a level above or below the surface. The worked example is a cubesat design problem with competing performance and cost objectives, described by a SysML v2 model and scored by a simulation on thirteen metrics.

## 2. The main results, as I now understand them

- Conditioning: attributes are turned into metrics that are strictly positive and follow "all else being equal, more is better". The abstract says this conditioning is also applied so the solvers behave in practice.
- Assumption: no strong assumption about the sampling distribution, and no assumption that the design space is well behaved. The one assumption named is that the frontier is log-convex.
- Family: hyperbolic trade spaces are defined by level sets of a posynomial in the metrics. The monomial case is a convex optimisation problem; the posynomial case is compatible with generalised geometric programming.
- Energy: fitting the surface amounts to solving for "energy" level sets, and the fit is said to describe the trade-off space.
- Dominance: Pareto improvements increase energy, and a design that maximises energy is weakly Pareto-efficient.
- n-lemmas: a monomial in n metrics is called an n-lemma. High-dimensional n-lemmas are said to be detected in the worked example and used to inform a design decision.
- White space: empty regions inside an occupied level set may be under-explored parts of the design space.
- Traceability: a point in the trade space pulls back to the design that produced it. A point with no data is a conjecture, to be settled by building and measuring.
- Software: the proofs are scripted in SymPy, and HTS is a scikit-learn estimator.

I take the main claims to be (a) a usable unsupervised fit that yields a trade-off description, (b) an interpretable unit of trade-off (the n-lemma), and (c) a worked cubesat example in which these were used for a design decision.

## 4. What I would say about it to a colleague

It is a method for finding and describing trade-offs across many designs and many metrics, closer in spirit to PCA than to an optimiser. Its fit is a surface with designs placed on it by their level, and each trade-off is a monomial that can be named. The author says the proofs are scripted, and the code is an estimator you can drop into a pipeline. Whether it is useful depends on the method section, which I have not seen.

## 5. What the method does not do, and what the paper does not claim

From the abstract alone:
- It does not, as far as the abstract says, assume a sampling distribution or a well-behaved design space. It does assume log-convex frontiers, and it needs conditioning first.
- It does not claim formal verification. The abstract lists formal verification and a move to Rust and theorem provers as future work, and says the current proofs are SymPy scripts.
- It does not claim that a fitted surface is a validated model of the designs. The abstract says a point without data is a conjecture, so the fit's predictions are presented as hypotheses until built and measured.
- It does not say how the worked example's decision was validated or whether stakeholders agreed with it.
- It does not claim that searching the design space by sampling white space works; the abstract lists this as a future thrust.

5. "Trade-offs trace piecewise hyperbolic frontiers: in order to get more of one objective, we must tolerate less of another." The frontier is called piecewise hyperbolic, but nothing in the abstract gives the shape or the pieces.
6. "When supplied with a dataset we first condition the attributes into metrics that are strictly positive and 'all else being equal, more is better.'" I could not work out what conditioning consists of, so I could not say how an attribute becomes a metric.

8. "To discover trade-offs among many designs and many metrics, we define the family of hyperbolic trade spaces by the level sets of a posynomial function of the metrics." "Posynomial" is not defined. I could not say what the function is, or what its level sets are.
9. "The monomial case is a convex optimization problem, and the posynomial case is compatible with generalized geometric programming." I could not connect this to the log-convexity assumption. The abstract does not say whether the convex problem is the fit, the search, or the trade-off description.
10. "HTS fits a surface one dimension below the data and places each design by its position on it and its level above or below it, losing nothing." I stopped at "losing nothing". ## 7. Terms whose meaning I had to guess

- trade study: a structured comparison of design alternatives against objectives, leading to a recorded decision (guess from usage).
- trade space: the set of designs with their metric values, viewed as a space (guess).
- hyperbolic / hyperbolic frontier: a frontier with a hyperbolic shape, probably a curve like x times y equal to a constant (guess; not stated).
- frontier: the boundary of achievable trade-offs (guess).
- log-convex: convex after taking logarithms of the metrics (guess, standard usage).
- posynomial: a sum of monomials with positive coefficients in the metrics (guess, standard usage in geometric programming).
- monomial: a single product of metric powers with a positive coefficient (guess).
- geometric programming: optimisation in the form posynomial objective with monomial constraints, convertible to convex form by logarithms (guess).
- level set: the set of designs where a function takes a fixed value (standard).
- energy: the value of the posynomial function at a design, or some level of it (guess; not stated).
- Pareto-efficient, weakly Pareto-efficient: no design improves all metrics, with the weak form allowing ties (standard guess).
- white space: unsampled regions of the design space (guess).
- n-lemma: a named monomial in n metrics, after dilemma and trilemma (guess from the abstract's wording).
- conditioning: the transformation of attributes into positive, larger-is-better metrics, plus any numerical preparation for the solver (guess; the abstract uses it in two senses).
- attribute versus metric: attribute is the raw measured quantity; metric is the conditioned one (guess).
- pulls back: maps a point back to its generating design (guess).
- conjecture: an untested statement about a design (standard).
- SysML v2: a systems-modelling language (standard).
