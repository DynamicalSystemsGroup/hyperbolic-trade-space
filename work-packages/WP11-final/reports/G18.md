## 1. What it is about

A method, "hyperbolic trade space" (HTS), for reading a trade study from a table of evaluated designs. Each design has metrics (positive, larger is better). The method fits a posynomial "reference surface" to the data: a level set of a weighted geometric mean V of the metrics, with exponents on the simplex. Each design is then placed by its position along the surface and its level (log V) relative to it. With one term the fit is a geometric program convex in the exponents; several terms are fitted by an alternation that the paper says has only partial guarantees. The worked example is a simulated cubesat design problem (2,134 feasible designs, 13 metrics), with a sampled random design table, and a decision table of fleet configurations.

## 2. The main results, as I understand them

- One term (K = 1): the soft-maximum fit is convex in the exponents, so every minimizer is global; uniqueness is not claimed (Theorems 2 and 3). Elasticities are read from ratios of exponents, and the chart (position, level) is a bijection with the metrics (Theorems 5 and 6). Log-affine conditioning is exactly invertible (Theorem 7).
- A maximizer of V is weakly Pareto-efficient, and efficient when all exponents are positive (Theorem 9). The converse is not claimed.
- Several terms (K > 1): the fit is not convex in the terms (Theorem 10, shown by an instance). The alternation has three levels of guarantee: descent with exact term minimizers (Theorem 11), a numerical term gap (Lemma 1), and local optimality with cells held fixed under an assignment margin (Lemma 2, Proposition 3, Corollary 2). A second-order test certifies a strict local minimizer for a soft fit (Theorem 12).
- Noise on the log-metrics adds a ridge to the expected objective, to first order, and shifts the exponents by a closed form (Corollary 1). I checked the algebra: the log of an expectation of exp(tau w.(u+eps)) gives J_tau(w) + (tau/2) w' Sigma w, and the first-order shift is consistent with that ridge.
- On the cubesat case, the paper reports a seven-metric one-term contending set, two terms at K = 2, and a decision table. These are reported as observations, not proofs.

## 4. What I would say to a colleague

The convex core is standard and correctly stated: log-mean-exp of linear functions of the exponents is convex, the simplex KKT conditions are correctly derived, and the Frank-Wolfe style term gap bounds the first-order suboptimality correctly. The novel part is the combination with a soft fit and an alternation, and there the paper is careful about what it does not prove. The result to be careful with is the decision on the cubesat case, which is a human reading of numerical output.

## 5. What the method does not do, and what the paper does not claim

The paper does not claim priority, does not replace PCA or Pareto analysis, and does not claim global convergence of the alternation (Q1), statistical consistency under noise (Q2), identifiability of active metrics under finite biased samples (Q3), or that the fitted surface recovers the Pareto boundary and not the sample's supporting envelope (Q4). It states that the lemma scripts are not formal verification, that the fit describes the table and not designs outside it, and that interpretation is human validation. It does not say how to pick K beyond the package's checks. The exponents are not causal, and the fit holds only on a log-convex surface.

## 6. Points of confusion, stopping and imprecision, in the order I met them

1. Abstract: "HTS fits a surface one dimension below the data ... losing nothing." I expected the "losing nothing" claim to be qualified. The chart is a bijection for K = 1 (Theorem 6); for K > 1 the level is recovered only through Phi_K. The sentence reads as a general claim.
2. Abstract: "a monomial term in n metrics characterizes an n-lemma." Definition 6 says the n-lemma size is the contending set size, which is read from the exact-maximum fit under a 1e-9 threshold. The abstract's "n" reads as a property of the term, but it depends on a threshold and on which fit is used.
3. Abstract and Section 2: "the frontier is log-convex." The word "frontier" is used for the fitted surface here, but Appendix A says "frontier" is "not a defined object", and Definition 1 names three objects that carry the word. I could not always tell whether the Pareto boundary, the supporting envelope or the fitted surface was meant.
4. Definition 3: "the formula is the sum. The package's mean (kappa = infinity: the maximum) subtracts the constant (log K)/kappa." The definition is a sum, but the package aggregates by a mean. At finite kappa this changes the level offset. The sentence says it changes neither exponents nor minimizers, but I could not tell which aggregator the stated levels use.
5. Definition 4: the band is stated as "the closed band |log V| <= 1/tau" in the bound, and "the band here is the open one, as the code counts it". I was not sure which set the rest of the paper uses.
6. Section 5, conditioning: "the package tolerates a zero metric by leaving that row out of the fit, without a warning." This conflicts with A1 (metrics strictly positive) and with the clear rejection of negative values in the same paragraph. Appendix E, G-5, also says a row with a zero metric "carries no information about the other exponents" and then lists "the row still constrains the other metrics" as unproved. These do not agree.
7. Section 6, the status words: "Derived means proved from its hypotheses by a plain-SymPy script." Section 13 says "The derivations are machine-checked," and Section 10 says "machine verification shows that a formula follows from its hypotheses." Appendix B says the scripts are "a substitute for pencil-and-paper derivation, not formal verification." The word "proved" is used for steps that are posited axioms (160 of 505 steps are "by declared axiom" in Appendix B) and that rest on F hypotheses, so the term is doing more than the checker shows.
8. Section 6, hypothesis codes: F is described as "a fallback hypothesis a script declares because a step resisted symbolic simplification, such as that a maximum over finitely many terms is attained." I did not reach the list of F hypotheses, so I cannot tell how many results depend on F rather than A, R or I.
9. Theorem 2 remark: "for the facet-normal reading, w_j > 0 for every j at the fit temperature, taken on its own: R1 is a tau = infinity condition and does not give it." I checked this against Appendix B L3.9, which says the same. It is clearly stated, though it sits oddly with the phrase "the balanced point ... in the relative interior of a facet" in R1.
10. Theorem 5: "w_j = 0.02 at V = 0.9 gives about 190." Checked: 0.9^(-50) is about 194, so the figure is right.
11. Theorem 11: "the sharp alternation then stops after finitely many iterations." The finiteness argument uses the hypothesis of unique term minimizers, which the text says Theorem 3 does not supply. Appendix E, G-2, lists the positive definiteness of the restricted Hessian on the sample as not provable. So the finite-termination claim depends on a hypothesis that the paper itself says it cannot verify for a data set. The main text states this as a hypothesis, but the abstract-level reading ("the alternation stops") is stronger than the hypothesis.
12. Proposition 3: local optimality "with the cells held at those of W" and "every w_k minimizes f_k over Delta." The text says these are exact minimizers the solver approximates, so the claim is conditional. The conditional form is clear.
13. Theorem 12 (soft-fit certificate): "H = D - kappa M ... Both D and M are positive semidefinite." Whether this sign gives a strict local minimizer depends on the sign convention for F = -mean Phi_K. I did not verify this in Appendix B L17.
14. Table 2 caption: "the levels, all inside the near-frontier band, do not rank them." The band at K = 2 is 0.0240 (stated, 1/tau at K = 1). The baseline row has level -0.330, which is far outside it. The caption's "all" is false for the table as printed, unless the baseline row is excluded from the sentence, which the table does not say.
15. Section 9, K = 1: "total mass also carries 0.02 and is not in the contending set." This is explained by Definition 6 (contending set is the exact-maximum support, participation is at the fit temperature), but a reader must hold three thresholds apart to follow it.
16. Section 9, decision: "the fleet of 5 medium-wheel, fine-tracker satellites ... is the one whose larger shortfall on the two measures, each scaled from its best to its worst of the 28, is smallest, among the 5 that no other beats on both." The rule is clear, but it needs the 28-design set and its min and max, which I did not check.
17. Figure 1 caption: "the point drawn, from the fit temperature, may lie a little inside that face." A point drawn inside a face named by a different (exact-maximum) support is an honest caveat, but I could not see the figure to check how far inside.
18. Section 6.2: "On the cubesat case ... 37 of the 2,134 [designs]" are within the band of a seam. This is a numerical count, correctly marked as observed.
19. Section 2, HoroPCA: "In a prospectively specified test ... recovered planted exponents only where they were the direction of largest spread." The term "prospectively specified" is not explained in the main text; I did not read Appendix H to see what was specified in advance.

## 7. Terms whose meaning I had to guess

- "Frontier": my guess was the fitted surface, but the paper uses it for at least three objects; Appendix A says it is not a defined object.
- "n-lemma": my guess was the number of metrics with nonzero exponent in one term; the text defines it as the size of the contending set (exponents above 1e-9 in the exact-maximum fit to the term's cell).
- "Contending set": the exact-maximum support of a term; I took the threshold 1e-9 from Definition 6.
- "Reach": my guess was the soft maximum of the scores along the direction; Definition 7 confirms.
- "Term share": fraction of near-frontier designs whose cell is that term.
- "Cell": the rows whose maximizing term is k (Appendix A), not a cluster.
- "Energy differential" and "level offset": log V; the paper says it is neither a physical energy nor a distance.
- "Improvement factor": V^(-1/w_j), the single-metric scaling that brings a design to V = 1.
- "Chart": the reversible map from metrics to (Z, log V).
- "Assignment margin" and "stable radius": the minimum gap between best and second-best term scores, and gamma divided by twice the maximum norm of (u_i, -1). I checked the Cauchy-Schwarz step.
- "Mean slack": minus the mean of Phi_K over the rows.
- "Certificate": for a sharp fit, the three levels; for a soft fit, the second-order test.
- "Ridge": the term (tau/2) w' Sigma w that noise induces.
- "Active face": the face of the simplex spanned by positive exponents.
- "Participation threshold": 1e-3 at the fit temperature; "numerically zero": 1e-12.
- "Generalized posynomial" and "generalized geometric program": the paper's phrasing matches Boyd et al. (2007), section 5.
