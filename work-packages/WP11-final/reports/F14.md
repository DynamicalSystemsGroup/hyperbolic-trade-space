## 1. What this is about

A method for analysing trade studies. Candidate designs are evaluated, and the outcomes are conditioned into strictly positive larger-is-better "metrics" m, one row per design. The paper takes logs, u = log m, and fits a "frontier" to the cloud of log-metric rows. For one term the frontier is a hyperplane w·u = β with w on the probability simplex. The invariant value is V = e^{-β} ∏ m_j^{w_j}, a weighted geometric mean scaled so that V = 1 on the fitted surface. The weights come from minimising a smooth maximum of the scores w·u_i over the simplex. The smooth maximum is the log-mean-exp J_τ with a fit temperature τ. At τ = ∞ it is the plain maximum, and the fit is a linear program. For several terms the model is a log-sum-exp (a posynomial raised to the power 1/κ) of such affine pieces, each piece with its own simplex weights and offset. The fitted weights read as elasticities (trade rates between metrics along a piece). The set of metrics with positive weight in a piece is its "contending set" (the "n-lemma"). Section 6 collects the mathematical facts about this: homogeneity, convexity of the one-term fit, the log-mean-exp versus log-sum-exp identity, noise as a ridge, exact pull-back to attributes, Pareto efficiency of maximisers, and a descent and local-optimality account of the alternating multi-term fit.

## 2. The main results, as I now understand them

- Theorem 1: V is homogeneous of degree one in m and equals 1 after dividing m by V(m). Proposition 1: adding a constant c to every entry of U adds c to β and leaves the minimisers alone.
- Theorem 2: first-order conditions for the simplex problem. ∇J_τ = Uᵀp with p = softmax(τUw), and Uᵀp is constant on the support of w, and not smaller off it.
- Theorem 3: J_τ is convex, with curvature τ·Var_p(v·u). It is not claimed strictly convex.
- Theorem 4: LSE = LME + (log N)/τ. mean ≤ LME ≤ max, and LME ≥ max − (log N)/τ.
- Proposition 2 and Corollary 1: for Gaussian log-space noise, the soft maximum acquires the ridge (τ/2) wᵀΣw. To first order the exponents move by −(PCP)⁺PΣw, where C is the softmax-weighted covariance at the noise-free fit and P projects onto sum-zero vectors vanishing off the support. Zero exponents stay zero.
- Theorem 5: along the surface dlog m_j/dlog m_i = −w_i/w_j. A design with V < 1 reaches V = 1 by improving metric j alone by V^{-1/w_j}, or all metrics by 1/V.
- Theorem 6: the chart (Z = Bu, log V) is a bijective rewriting of u, with B an orthonormal basis of the sum-zero space.
- Theorem 7: under log-affine conditioning, V in attribute units is e^{w·b−β} ∏ (a_j+s_j)^{w_j α_j}.
- Theorem 8: Φ_K = (1/κ) log Σ_k e^{κ(w_k·u−β_k)} satisfies max_k x_k ≤ Φ_K ≤ max_k x_k + (log K)/κ and is shift-equivariant. Its exponential is a posynomial in m.
- Theorem 9: a maximiser of V is weakly Pareto-efficient, and efficient if the relevant weights are all positive.
- Multi-term fit (6.2):
  - The objective F (mean slack) is not convex for K ≥ 2 (Theorem 10 i).
  - A soft-weight alternation majorises it (Theorem 10 ii and iii).
  - The sharp-weight alternation does not increase F (Theorem 11).
  - A term gap bounds each term's suboptimality (Lemma 1).
  - A positive assignment margin γ gives a stable radius (Lemma 2).
  - Given exact term minimisers, W is a local minimiser of F within that radius (Proposition 3).
  - There is an approximate certificate bound (Corollary 2).
  - There is a second-order certificate for the soft fit (Theorem 12).
- Numerical statement for the case: at K = 2 the margin is 3.77e-4 and the stable radius 2.55e-5, against a band 1/τ = 0.0088. So 37 of 2,134 designs are within the band of a seam, and which term they fall to "is not a finding".
- Open (6.3): global convergence of the alternation, statistical consistency, identifiability of active metrics, and conditions under which the fitted surface recovers the Pareto boundary rather than the sample's supporting envelope.

## 4. What I would tell a colleague

It is a log-space "soft envelope" fit. The one-term case is clean convex analysis on a simplex. The several-term case is a heuristic alternation, and the paper is honest that nothing global is proved. The one-term results are ordinary and checkable. The K ≥ 2 certificates are local and conditional, and in the case study the certified radius is about 47 times smaller than the band, so they say little about the designs near seams. The hypotheses and the symbol overloading need care when reading.

## 5. What the method does not do, and what the paper does not claim

- It does not claim global optimality or convergence of the multi-term fit. Q1 to Q4 are open, and Theorem 11 says stationarity of the stop is "not claimed".
- It does not claim consistency under noise. Corollary 1 is about the shift of the minimiser of an "expected noisy objective", "not of one noisy fit".
- It does not claim that the fitted surface is the Pareto boundary. That is Q4, and Theorem 9 gives only one direction, with "the converse is not claimed".
- It does not claim strict convexity or unique minimisers.
- It does not claim the conditioning is context-free: "a stable fitted direction answers that question and is not a context-free discovery".
- It cannot represent non-log-convex structure (simplifying assumption vi).
- It does not predict. There is no predict method.
- It does not claim that a design's term assignment near a seam is meaningful.

## 7. Terms whose meaning I had to guess

- "Reach": how far the data extend along w_k. I took it as the offset β_k. Offset versus reach appear for the same thing.
- "Energy differential": log V, relative to the surface (and s_ik at a term).
- "n-lemma": the contending set of a term, and in the figure the face containing the exponent vector (see 6.6).
- "Slack": how far the surface lies above a design in log scale, β − w·u (stated in 6.2).
- "Term gap": the quantity G_k that bounds suboptimality.
- "Stable radius": the parameter radius under which each row's maximising term cannot change.
- "Fixed-weight objective" (Theorem 10): the objective with the soft assignment weights frozen, possibly with an entropy term.
- "Active face" and "critical cone" (Theorem 12): the face of the simplex product defined by the active positivity constraints with positive multipliers, and the standard critical cone.
- "Supporting envelope": the τ = ∞ fit, a supporting hyperplane to the convex hull of the rows in log space.
- "Ascent direction": w_k as the gradient of log V in u.
- "Generalized posynomial": from geometric programming (compositions of posynomials with max and powers).
- "Participation threshold": the cut below which an exponent carries no elasticity at the fit temperature.
- "Accepted iteration": one that leaves no cell empty (stated).
- "I-n", "F-n" and "L-n" codes: inherited results, fallbacks and lemma scripts.
