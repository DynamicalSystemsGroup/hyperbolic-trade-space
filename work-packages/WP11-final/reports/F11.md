## 1. What this is about

A method for turning a table of designs and their metrics (all strictly positive, larger is better) into one smooth "level" per design. The level is measured against a fitted reference surface, V = 1, where V is a weighted geometric mean of the metrics. The exponents of that mean are the elasticities of the trade-off. The worked example is a cubesat model with thirteen traded metrics. The paper places itself beside PCA and Pareto analysis, says it claims nothing as novel, and says it does not replace either. One term gives a monomial fit, which is a geometric program. Several terms give a soft-max posynomial, with weaker guarantees.

## 2. The main results as I now understand them

- V(m) = e^(-beta) times the product of m_j^(w_j), with w on the simplex. It is homogeneous of degree one, and log V = w.u - beta in log coordinates (Theorem 1, Definition 2). I checked the homogeneity identities by hand; they hold.
- For one term, minimising the soft-max objective is convex in log coordinates (Theorems 2 and 3), so any minimiser is global. Uniqueness is not claimed. I checked the term-gap bound in Lemma 1 by hand; it is right.
- On the surface, the exponents are elasticities: d log m_j / d log m_i = -w_i / w_j (Theorem 5). A design below the surface reaches it by improving metric j alone by V^(-1/w_j). I checked both statements.
- The chart Z = Bu, with log V, is an orthogonal, scale-free, invertible rewriting (Theorem 6). Z has d-1 coordinates. It is a change of basis, not a reduction of dimension, so it is not PCA. PCA enters only as a reading of the spread among near-frontier designs (Figure 6, Appendix D.16).
- A maximiser of V is weakly Pareto-efficient, and efficient when every exponent is positive (Theorem 9). I checked this; it holds.
- Several terms: soft-weight alternation with a descent claim (Theorem 11), three levels of guarantee (Lemma 1 numerical stationarity, Proposition 3 conditional local optimality, Corollary 2 approximate certificate), and a second-order certificate for the soft fit (Theorem 12).
- Noise on the log-metrics adds a ridge and moves the exponents by a first-order formula (Corollary 1).
- On the cubesat model, the two-term fit separates contending sets that one term blends (Figure 8).

## 3. What I would do next with it

I would treat it as a transformer in a scikit-learn pipeline. The input is a design-by-metric table of strictly positive values, conditioned by me first, with signs flipped where a metric is a cost. fit(K=1) would return w and beta. transform would return the chart coordinates Z and log V. I would read log V as a level, not a ranking, since the paper says so in the Table 2 caption. Before trusting any exponent I would bootstrap or add noise, because Corollary 1 says exponents move with noise through a pseudoinverse. I would run PCA on the same near-frontier designs and compare its axes with the exponents. I would check collinear metric pairs (Figure 7) before reading elasticities off them.

## 4. What I would tell a colleague

It fits a geometric-mean level set to your metrics. The exponents are elasticities. For one term the fit is convex, so the minimum is global. The level says how far a design sits from the fitted surface, not how good it is. It is not PCA and does not replace Pareto analysis; PCA only reads the spread among the near-frontier designs.

## 5. What the method does not do, and what the paper does not claim

- The reference surface is not the Pareto boundary (Definition 1). The level is not a ranking.
- It does not say schedule, risk, what to buy beyond the modelled parts, or what a larger fleet is worth (Table 2 caption).
- The paper claims nothing as novel and does not replace PCA or Pareto analysis (main results).
- Several terms: no global guarantee and no uniqueness. Stationarity of the alternation's stop is not claimed (Theorem 11).
- Questions Q1 to Q4 are open (Section 6.3). The monotonicity of the search is open (Section 7).
- Corollary 1 does not say whether the rates are right. Corollary 2 is for the returned run, not across restarts. Theorem 3 gives convexity, not strict convexity.

(d) Figure 4, caption: "primary regime (aggressive controller gains)". "Regime" is not defined in what I was given. The fit temperature is defined as the temperature where the fraction above V = 1 is within 0.01 of a target of 0.1. That is a selection rule, not a derivation, and the caption says so only by a parenthesis ("a convention, Section 5"). The effective sample size at that point is 74. The caption ends with a stray word, "enlarge".

(e) Figure 5, y-axis: "unit cost (inverse USD; larger is better)". The metric is named as a cost but is inverse USD, so I had to reread. I checked the slope. The line runs from about 2.2 at x = -2.3 to about -1.4 at x = 2.3, a slope of about -0.8. Figure 4 at tau = 42 gives -w_power / w_unitcost = -0.20 / 0.26, about -0.77. (f) Figure 6, caption: "each design's level log V, the energy differential". "Energy differential" is not defined; the same phrase appears in Definition 5. The caption says the surface is drawn as the flat line log V = 0 "whatever the position". That flatness follows from the chart's construction, so I did not read it as evidence. (g) Figure 7: within term 1, power margin, energy balance and available power correlate at 0.97 to 1.00. ## 7. Terms whose meaning I had to guess

- "n-lemma" and its names: the size of a contending set, with 2 a "dilemma", 3 a "trilemma" and 1 a corner where one metric "binds alone". My guess is that a corner names a one-element set.
- "Primary regime": a subset of the sampled designs. I guessed it is the data sampled with the aggressive gains.
- "Energy differential": my guess is log V as a level offset from the surface, as Definition 5 says.
- "Executives' measures": stakeholder-level measures, by analogy with the MoE labels.
- "Derived": proved from the listed hypotheses.
- "Triple": a three-metric subset.
- "Reach" and "term share": a soft maximum along a direction, and the fraction of frontier designs in a cell.
- "Participation threshold": an exponent of 0.001, below which a metric counts as unused.
- "Fit temperature" tau: the soft-max temperature of the fit.
- "Contending set": the support of the exact-maximum fit within a cell.

## 8. The questions I am left with

- What is the "slack" whose soft maximum is minimised, and what sign convention does it use? The factor-e claim for the band only follows if the weight is exp(tau log V).
- How does the fit separate near-collinear metrics such as power margin, energy balance and available power? Are the exponents on them reported with any uncertainty?
- Why is 0.1 the target for the fraction above V = 1, and what in the data sets it? How sensitive is the chosen tau = 42 to that choice?
- What are Q1 to Q4?
- How many designs are in the primary regime, and how is that regime tied to the aggressive gains?
- Does the cubesat case's count of ten of thirteen include settling time?
- Once the metrics are standardised, does the chart reproduce PCA's axes? The paper only says the two compose as a reading.
