## 1. What this is about

A table of evaluated designs is turned into a fitted "reference surface". Each design is a vector of metrics (strictly positive, larger is better). The fit finds weights on the simplex so that a soft maximum of the weighted log-metrics is as small as possible; the minimum sets the level of the surface. Each monomial term of the fit is then read as a named set of metrics that trade against each other (the paper calls the name an "n-lemma", by analogy with dilemma and trilemma) and each exponent gives an elasticity, the percentage of one metric given up per percentage gained in another. Sections 3 to 6 contain the definitions, the reversible chart, the one-term fit, the several-term alternation, the lemma and theorem statements with their checks, and four open questions.

## 2. The main results, as I now understand them

- The invariant value V(m) = e^(-beta) times the product of m_j^(w_j) is a weighted geometric mean scaled so that V = 1 on the fitted surface. Its logarithm is w.u minus beta. Scaling all metrics by k multiplies V by k (Theorem 1). I checked the factor form of the elasticity claim in Theorem 5 and it holds: a design with V < 1 reaches the surface by multiplying metric j by V^(-1/w_j) alone, or every metric by 1/V.
- One term: beta is the minimum over w of a log-mean-exp soft maximum of the scores w.u_i. This is convex in w (Theorem 3), so every minimizer is global; the paper does not claim uniqueness. Exponents are elasticities (Theorem 5). The chart (Z, log V) is a reversible rewriting (Theorem 6) and the pull-back to attribute units is exact (Theorem 7). A maximizer of V is weakly Pareto-efficient (Theorem 9). The log-mean-exp and log-sum-exp differ by log N over tau (Theorem 4), which is why the 1/N factor is there.
- Several terms: the surface is a posynomial (a soft maximum over terms, Theorem 8). The fit is not convex. It is solved by alternation: label each design by its best term, refit each term, repeat. Three levels of guarantee are stated. Level 1 is descent, with finite stopping claimed only under an assumption about unique minimizers (Theorem 11). Level 2 is a numerical stationarity measure, the term gap (Lemma 1). Level 3 is local minimality of the mean slack within a stable radius, conditional on exact minimizers and a positive assignment margin (Lemma 2, Proposition 3, Corollary 2). For a soft fit a second-order test certifies a strict local minimizer (Theorem 12).
- Noise: Gaussian noise in log space adds a ridge to the objective and moves the exponents to first order (Corollary 1). This is a statement about the expected objective, not about one fit.
- The paper labels each result Derived (checked by a SymPy script named in a green box), Numerical (measured, not proved) or Open.

Checks I could do by hand: the bands 1/tau = 0.0240 at tau = 41.7 and 1/tau = 0.0088 at tau = 113 match the stated values; 0.0088 divided by half the stated margin 0.0003768 is about 47, as the paper says; the Gaussian cumulant in Proposition 2 is right.

## 4. What I would tell a colleague

It is a way to put one surface under a table of evaluated designs, so that each design has a position along the surface and a level above or below it. The trade-offs are the exponents, read as elasticities, and a term is named by the metrics that contend in it. The one-term case is a convex problem with global minimizers and the algebra is checked by script. The several-term case is not convex and comes with only local, partly conditional guarantees, which the paper labels honestly. The method says what trades against what; it does not say which trade to choose.

## 5. What the method does not do, and what the paper does not claim

- It gives no global optimum for several terms and no convergence of the alternation (Open Q1, "not pursued").
- It makes no statistical claim: no consistency under noise (Q2), no identifiability of the active metrics under finite biased samples (Q3), and no proof that the fitted surface recovers the Pareto boundary rather than the supporting envelope of the sample (Q4).
- Exponents describe the sample. The paper says they are not causal, and that designed samples such as grids are not independent observations, so the exponents are not estimates with a sampling error.
- The conditioning choice is said to define the question, and the level of the surface depends on the 10 percent target fraction, which is a convention.
- The soft-fit certificate (Theorem 12) assumes first-order conditions hold exactly, not within a tolerance. The paper says numerical stationarity is measured, not proved.
- Whether a table meets the hypotheses is a human judgment, which the paper says it does not decide.

## 7. Terms whose meaning I had to guess, and my guesses

- n-lemma: a named set of metrics that contend within one term; the number is the set size.
- contending set: the metrics with a nonzero exponent in a term's exact-maximum fit.
- elasticity: the ratio of exponents as defined in Definition 8.
- reach: how far the data extend along a term's direction (a soft maximum of scores).
- energy differential: the log level of a design relative to the surface.
- chart: the pair (position along the surface, level), built from an orthonormal basis of sum-zero vectors.
- posynomial, generalized posynomial, geometric program: sums of monomials and their powers and maxima; minimization of a log-sum-exp-type convex function. I did not check the exact definitions.
- frontier: ambiguous, as noted at 6(b) and 6(i).
- support: ambiguous, as noted at 6(aa).
- sharp and soft: kappa or tau infinite versus finite.
- the package: the software implementation.
- the case: the cubesat example of Section 8, inferred from the forward reference.
- scaler: a per-metric scaling for equal log-spread.
- ridge: a quadratic penalty added to the objective.
- balanced point: the vector U^T p.
- accepted iteration: one that leaves no cell empty.
- tie rule: a fixed rule for ties in the argmax.
- certified: at 6(ii), a tolerance-based status, or a test that holds.

## 8. The questions I am left with

- What is the membership threshold? Definition 6 says 10^-9, Section 5 says 10^-3 for participation and 10^-12 for numerically zero.
- Why does exponentiating the weights make the one-term program a geometric program?
- Why does Section 5 say the case needs three terms when the fits of record are at K = 2 and 3?
- In Section 5, which of the three objects is the frontier?
- Where is R1 used, if anywhere, after Theorem 2?
- Why does ambiguity between terms subtract curvature in Theorem 12?
- What exactly does the alternation converge to, if it stops at all, and does a stop at a tolerance give anything beyond a measured status?
- Does the level of the surface change when the 10 percent target changes? The paper says the contending sets do not change.
