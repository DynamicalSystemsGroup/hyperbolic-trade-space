## 1. What it is about

A method and Python package (an scikit-learn-style estimator) for reading the trade-offs in a table of evaluated designs. You give it a table: one row per design, columns are outcomes that you have turned into strictly positive "larger is better" numbers (metrics). It fits a surface that sits on the upper edge of the data in log space: the set where a weighted geometric mean of the metrics (the "invariant value" V) equals one. The weights are the exponents, non-negative, summing to one. Which metrics get non-zero exponent says which metrics "contend" on that piece of the edge (the "n-lemma"), and the ratios of exponents are the rates at which one trades against another. Each design is then re-expressed as a position along the surface plus a level above or below it (log V). With several terms (K > 1) the surface is a max over several such pieces. The worked example is a cubesat design with 13 metrics, 3,000 sampled designs per regime, ending in a shortlist of 28 designs and a choice between a few fleets.

For my side (the evaluation): the method treats the evaluation as a black box (simulator, physical test, human assessment) that maps a configuration to attributes. It does not model or run my system.

## 2. The main results, as I now understand them

- With one term, the fit is: minimise a log-mean-exp (soft maximum, with N-normalisation) of w·u over the simplex. It is convex in w, so any minimiser is global, but uniqueness is not claimed. At infinite temperature it is a linear program.
- V is homogeneous of degree one (doubling every metric doubles V). Exponents are elasticities (dlog m_j / dlog m_i = -w_i/w_j along the surface), and a design with V < 1 can reach the surface by improving one metric by V^(-1/w_j) or all by 1/V.
- The chart (Z, log V) is a bijective rewriting of the log-metrics, so nothing is lost; conditioning that is log-affine is exactly invertible, so results pull back to attribute units.
- A maximiser of V over any set of designs is weakly Pareto-efficient (efficient if all exponents are positive). The converse (that the fit reaches the Pareto boundary) is not claimed.
- Several terms: the fit is not convex. They give descent (finite stop), a term gap bounding distance to a per-term optimum, and local optimality with the cell assignment held fixed when the margin is positive; a second-order certificate for the soft variant. None of this is global.
- Noise on log-metrics acts as a ridge that shifts exponents to first order (population-level statement, not a per-fit statement).
- On the case: dominance (nondominated set) shortlists 236 of 2,134 on four attributes and all 2,134 on thirteen; the K = 1 fit gives a 7-metric contending set (K = 2 gives two terms with 8 and 7); a planted-answer test recovers all three terms on 16 of 20 tables, failing when a term has few frontier rows; three "propose, predict, check" proposals landed where predicted but did so by violating depth-of-discharge feasibility.
- Three kinds of claim are held apart: the formal results (scripted algebra, not formal verification), the numbers observed on this table, and anything about designs outside the table or about a decision, which the paper says needs its own validation.

## 5. What I believe the method does not do, and what the paper does not claim

Method does not: model or run the evaluation; do the conditioning; choose K (the analyst does, with checks that "none decides alone"); tell you a design is attainable (V > 1 is "a candidate to test"); give causal exchange rates ("not a causal exchange that changing a variable would reproduce"); replace PCA or Pareto analysis; guarantee that the fitted surface is the Pareto boundary rather than the sample's supporting envelope; predict (no predict method; one composes a regressor with the chart); handle non-log-convex structure (dents, holes); handle discrete or categorical variables inside the theory (variables are treated as continuous within a category); search the design space (Section 7 is explicitly future work with no results).

Paper does not claim: novelty (it claims no priority); global convergence of the several-term alternation (Q1); statistical consistency (Q2); identifiability of the active metrics under finite biased samples (Q3); recovery of the Pareto boundary (Q4); uniqueness of the one-term minimiser; that the hypotheses hold for a given table; stability of the exponents under resampling at K = 1 ("not assessed"); that the cubesat numbers are real (hardware values illustrative, costs lower bounds); that the shortlist or the proposed fleets are the answer ("The method prices the question and does not answer it"). "Formally verified" is said only of the requirement checks in the system model, not of the lemma scripts.

## 7. Terms whose meaning I had to guess, with my guess

- "hyperbolic" (title): the family of level sets m1^w1 m2^w2 = const (rectangular hyperbola in two metrics); I guessed it is only about shape in the metric plane, not hyperbolic geometry.
- "n-lemma": the set of n metrics that carry non-zero exponent on one term, so that improving one costs another (dilemma, trilemma).
- "energy differential": log V; a level relative to the surface, not an energy.
- "contending set": the support of the tau = infinity fit to a term's cell; the metrics that actually trade on that term.
- "invariant value": V; constant on the surface and degree-one homogeneous (I read "invariant" two ways, see 6.10).
- "reach" (beta): the soft-max of the scores w.u over the rows; the offset that sets the level of the surface.
- "tangent" (in "which term is tangent"): the term whose plane touches the edge of the data at a point.
- "cell": the set of designs assigned to a term; sharp assignment by argmax.
- "term share": fraction of near-frontier designs in a term's cell.
- "near-frontier band": designs with |log V| < 1/tau.
- "mean slack": average distance, in log V, by which the surface lies above a design (negative beyond it); the fit's objective.
- "margin" (gamma) and "stable radius": the smallest gap between best and second-best term across designs; the size of parameter moves that do not change any assignment.
- "term gap": the Frank-Wolfe style gap, a computable bound on how far a term's objective is from its minimum.
- "sharp / soft": kappa = infinity (max over terms) versus finite kappa (smoothed max); likewise tau.
- "certified", "stationary", "stationary without margin": solver status levels; certified is local with cells fixed.
- "chart", "position along the surface": Z = Bu, the coordinates orthogonal to the all-ones direction.
- "Helmert basis": a particular orthonormal basis of the sum-zero space.
- "standard scaler", "robust scaler": scikit-learn rescalers (mean/std; median/IQR).
- "conditioning" (two uses): (a) my map from attributes to metrics; (b) "conditioning_ of the exponents", the smallest curvature eigenvalue at the fit. The paper keeps them apart by an underscore and context but I mixed them at first.
- "participation threshold" (10^-3): below it an exponent is not read as an elasticity.
- "supporting envelope": tau = infinity fit, the LP bound no row exceeds.
- "gap G-n", "Ln", "I n", "R n", "A n", "M n": ledger and lemma codes; I guessed they are cross-reference keys for items in appendices.
- "GGP / posynomial / generalized posynomial": posynomial = sum of positive monomials; generalized = allows max and powers; the point is the model stays inside a convex-after-log class.
- "MoE / MoP": measure of effectiveness (stakeholder-read) versus measure of performance (subsystem-read).
- "log-spread k": the scaling constant in Appendix C.4 that gives std of log m = 1; I guessed it from the column values.
- "regime": a choice of sampling range for the design variables (aggressive versus stock gains).
- "propose, predict, check": regress configuration to chart coordinates, propose a design near a hole, measure it.
- "white space": a region of the chart inside an occupied level set where there are no designs.

## 9. The questions that remain for me

- What exactly must my evaluation guarantee for the method to run? The text says positive, oriented, conditionable; I do not know what the package does with a row that has one non-positive attribute (zero: silently dropped; negative: rejected), and which of those I should handle first.
- How many rows do I need? The case has 2,134 feasible designs, an effective sample of about 74 at the fit temperature; planted terms with 5 to 11 frontier rows failed. I could not find a rule of thumb for the number of rows relative to d and K.
- How do I choose the conditioning so the answer is about the thing I care about, and how do I know if two different conditioning choices disagree? The paper reports that a robust scaler gives an 8-metric set with the largest exponent on total mass, against 7 with the standard scaler.
- What does the exponent uncertainty look like? K = 1 stability under resampling "is not assessed"; Q2 and Q3 are open. Appendix E G-12 says a metric's presence "belongs to the sample, the conditioning and the noise together". How would I report an exponent to a decision-maker?
- If my evaluation is noisy (a test rig), how large can the noise be before the contending set changes? The paper gives "changes most at 10 percent noise" but I did not see the table.
- With discrete configuration variables (a swap of a part), what is the elasticity telling me when the trade is stepped? The paper says it reads across alternatives.
- How does the package behave when my evaluation outputs include categorical or ordinal assessments (human assessment)? The paper says the evaluation can be a human assessment but gives no example of how to condition it.
- How do I get from a point of the chart with no data back to a configuration? The abstract says a point "pulls back to the design that produced it"; for a point without data it is "a conjecture proposed by a rule". In Section 9 that check was done on three proposals that all violated feasibility.
- What does the "levels" column in Table 2 let me conclude, given that it does not rank?
- Does K > 1 change what I must deliver? I believe not, but I did not see the K > 1 requirements on the table (number of rows per term).
