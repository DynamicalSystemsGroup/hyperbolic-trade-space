1. What this is about

The paper, "Scaling Decision Analysis with Hyperbolic Trade Space Identification" by Michael Zargham, proposes a way to support a trade study: the comparison of candidate designs that an accountable executive has to justify. It fits a posynomial "reference surface" to the data behind the trade table, uses that surface as a coordinate system, and offers a chart that separates where a design sits on the surface from how far it sits from the surface. It also names and quantifies the trade-offs (the "n-lemma" and its elasticities) that are active among the metrics. A cubesat model that couples power, mass and attitude control is the worked example.

2. The main results, as I now understand them

- A posynomial surface, fitted to the outer edge of the data as a soft support, not a regression through the middle, gives the trade data a coordinate system. The table and its reading are said to be views of this model.
- A reversible chart splits a design's position along the surface from its "energy differential", its level relative to the surface.
- Each monomial term reports the metrics it contends, and elasticities give the percentage of one metric traded per percentage gained in another.
- A single active trade-off gives a monomial, which becomes a convex program and is solved directly. Several active trade-offs give a posynomial, which is compatible with generalized geometric programming.
- The method is unsupervised, and the abstract says it composes with PCA rather than competing with it.
- On the cubesat, one term blends the subsystems' trade-offs into one tension, and two terms separate the two contending sets.

The abstract gives no numbers from the cubesat, so I do not know the size of any trade-off it finds.

It is a way to fit a smooth boundary to the outcomes of past or modelled designs, and to read the trade-offs from the fitted model rather than from a table of Pareto points. It claims to be as interpretable as PCA, and the author says the two can be run together. The worked example is a small spacecraft model, not a flight programme.
