## 1. What it is about

A way to analyse a table of N designs scored on d strictly positive, larger-is-better "metrics". The method fits a posynomial-type surface to the outer edge of the log-metrics, then reads trade-offs off it. With one term, V(m) = e^(-beta) * prod m_j^(w_j), exponents w on the simplex, and the surface is {V = 1}, i.e. the hyperplane w.u = beta in log space. The hyperplane is chosen by minimising a log-mean-exp (entropic soft maximum) of the scores w.u_i over the simplex, equation (1). With several terms the model is a log-sum-exp (or max, at kappa = infinity) of such affine functions, fitted by an alternation between assigning designs to terms and refitting. Each term's exponents are read as elasticities, and the set of metrics with nonzero exponent is called an n-lemma. A reversible "chart" (Z, log V) splits a design's position along the surface from its level relative to it. It is pitched as unsupervised feature engineering that complements PCA and Pareto filtering, demonstrated on a 13-metric cubesat model.

## 2. The main results, as I now understand them

Proved (labelled Derived, with hypotheses):
- V is homogeneous of degree one; log V is shift-equivariant; the chart is a bijection (Theorems 1, 6; Proposition 1).
- The one-term objective J_tau(w) is convex on the simplex (its second derivative along any line is tau times a softmax-weighted variance), so every minimiser is global, with no uniqueness claim (Theorems 2, 3). This is the standard fact that log-sum-exp of linear functions is convex; I accept it.
- Log-mean-exp = log-sum-exp - (log N)/tau, and it is sandwiched between the mean and the max (Theorem 4).
- A maximiser of V over a finite set is weakly Pareto-efficient, and efficient if all exponents are positive (Theorem 9). The converse is explicitly not claimed.
- For K terms the log of the model is within (log K)/kappa of the largest term (Theorem 8).
- The several-term objective F is NOT convex (Theorem 10 (i), by a symmetry argument and a counter-instance). The alternation has three guarantee levels, none global: monotone descent of the sharp alternation (Theorem 11), a term-gap stationarity measure (Lemma 1), local optimality when an assignment margin is positive (Lemma 2, Proposition 3), and a certificate of how far the objective can improve with the cells held fixed (Corollary 2). A second-order sufficient condition certifies a strict local minimiser of the soft fit (Theorem 12).
- Noise to first order shifts the exponents by a ridge-type correction (Corollary 1).

Not established, stated as open: global convergence of the alternation, consistency under noise, identifiability, conditions for the surface to recover the Pareto boundary rather than the sample's supporting envelope (Q1 to Q4), and monotonicity of the search of Section 7.

Observed only (labelled Numerical): whether a given fit is stationary within tolerance, and everything about the cubesat case.

## 4. What I would say to a colleague

It is a geometric-programming-flavoured, soft-maximum "support function" fit, with a careful ledger of which statements are theorems and which are observations. The one-term part is clean convex optimisation and the several-term part is honestly presented as a nonconvex alternation with local guarantees only. The interpretive layer (n-lemmas, elasticities, energy differential) is what the engineering side adds, and the author says explicitly that those readings are human judgement. Read the guarantees in Section 6.2 carefully; they are narrower than the abstract's phrasing suggests.

## 5. What the method does not do, and what the paper does not claim

Does not: find the Pareto boundary (it fits a soft support of the sample, and Q4 is open); give a globally optimal several-term fit; give statistical consistency or any sampling-error estimate for exponents; remain unchanged under rescaling of a metric (the paper says the fitted direction answers a conditioned question); represent non-log-convex structure; predict (no `predict`); provide a convergent search (Section 7 is future work with no results); handle discrete variables or kernels (nothing built). The paper says it claims no novelty and no replacement of PCA or Pareto analysis, and that elasticities are not causal.

## 7. Terms I had to guess

- "invariant value" / "invariant form": I guess it means V is unchanged along level sets (the trade-off), and by homogeneity transforms predictably under scaling; two senses.
- "energy differential": log V; I guess a signed level relative to the surface, with the potential analogy only for path-independence.
- "reach" (beta): I guess the offset in V = 1, same as the soft-maximum value at the minimiser.
- "balanced point" (R1): I guess U^T p with p the dual (design-weight) vector, i.e. a point in the convex hull of rows where the data meet the supporting facet.
- "n-lemma": a set of n metrics that contend; guess is exactly the support size, found at tau = infinity.
- "contending set" vs "elasticity at the fit temperature": I guess the former is an LP support and the latter are the finite-tau exponents, and they can differ.
- "term share": fraction of near-frontier designs in a term's cell.
- "cell": rows for which a term is the largest scorer.
- "slack" (mean slack F): beta_{k(i)} - w_{k(i)}.u_i, a measure of how far the model is above design i, though not nonnegative.
- "fit temperature" tau and "term temperature" kappa: inverse sharpness parameters; guess that large means sharper.
- "supporting envelope": the tau = infinity fit; I guess for K = 1 a supporting hyperplane to the convex hull of the rows.
- "participation threshold" 10^-3 and "numerically zero" 10^-12 and the 10^-9 support threshold: three different cut-offs; I guess each used for a different reading.
- "balanced", "tangent space" and "ascent direction" (Definition 9): the hyperplane's tangent space and the gradient of log V, which for K = 1 is just the constant vector w.
- "I-codes" (I4, I8, I11, I14...): inherited results from the literature; "F-codes": fallbacks.
- "holes" in the chart and "novelty": regions of the chart not covered by designs.
- "stationary" vs "certified" (soft fits) as a declaration status.
- "refit of record": the all-rows refit of f_k used in the actual code.

## 8. Questions that remain

1. At tau = infinity with K = 1, is the fit exactly "min over the simplex of the max score", and is its solution, when non-unique, handled by a rule? How does the contending set (support above 10^-9) depend on that rule?
2. Why is the minimiser of the one-term problem expected to have positive normal (R1) for a table of positive metrics? What happens when a metric has smaller extent than all the others (the minimiser can concentrate on one metric)?
3. In Theorem 11, how often are the term minimisers unique? If the paper itself says uniqueness is not supplied, what is the status of the finite-termination statement in practice, and does the implementation use a tie rule that makes it moot?
4. Does the soft alternation (Theorem 10) descend monotonically on the soft objective? Part (ii) is a majorisation inequality; the text does not state the conclusion.
5. How is the step from the numerical iterate to the exact-minimiser hypotheses of Proposition 3 and Theorem 12 made? What tolerance of "first-order conditions hold exactly" is implied by "certified"?
6. Is the mean slack F bounded below, and does the alternation converge to anything (Q1)? Is the sharp objective F also non-convex in the sense that distinct-term local minima exist in the case?
7. How sensitive are the elasticities to the conditioning (log-spread equalisation), given that rescaling a metric changes which term is tangent? Is any statement invariant under admissible conditioning?
8. What does Corollary 1's "ridge" imply for the choice of tau in the presence of noise (the paper says it does not say whether the rates are right)?
9. How does the Pareto-efficiency of a maximiser of V relate to the fitted surface's closeness to the true Pareto boundary (Q4)? In particular the log-convex part: which attainable sets satisfy the hypothesis?
10. Is the 10 percent target fraction and the dependence of the contending sets on it ("halving or doubling leaves them unchanged") a result on this case only?
11. The Figures 4 to 8 and the tables: I could not read them, so the numerical evidence for the case is outside my reading.

## 9. Whether I would proceed to the appendices

Yes, but selectively. The one-term mathematics (L3 to L5, L7) I am inclined to accept as standard convex analysis; the places I would check are the ones where the main text's hypotheses are either unreadable to me or stronger than the conclusion the main text draws: Theorem 11 (finite termination without uniqueness), Theorem 10 (what is held fixed in (iii), and the status of soft descent), Proposition 3 and Theorem 12 (passing from exact to numerical iterates), the definition of R1 and of the balanced point, the K > 1 clause of Theorem 6, and the L16 counter-instance for non-convexity. The reason is that the main text is careful about labelling Derived, Numerical and Open, which makes the appendix the place where the labelled claims can be tested, and a few statements (the certificate language, finite termination) are those I could not close from the main text alone.
