## 1. What it is about

The paper proposes a method, hyperbolic trade space (HTS), for reading a trade study from a table of designs. Each design has configuration variables, is evaluated into attributes, and the attributes are conditioned into positive metrics where larger is better. The method fits a posynomial "reference surface" to those metrics: a weighted geometric mean (the invariant value V) that equals one on the surface. Each design then gets a position along the surface and a level (log V) above or below it. Each fitted term is read as an "n-lemma": the set of metrics that contend, with elasticities from the exponents. The worked example is a simulated cubesat with 13 attributes, 3,000 random designs per regime, 2,134 feasible in the primary regime. The paper says the method is implemented as a scikit-learn transformer and that the proofs are scripted in SymPy.

## 2. The main results, as I now understand them

- With one term (K = 1) the fit is a geometric program in the exponents, convex in the weights. Its exponents are elasticities, and the chart is a reversible rewrite of the log-metrics (Theorems 2, 3, 5, 6).
- A maximizer of V over the designs is weakly Pareto-efficient (Theorem 9). Efficiency of a maximizer is proved; the converse is not.
- The several-term fit is not convex (Theorem 10). The paper gives three levels of guarantee: descent, a numerical stationarity measure (term gap), and local optimality with cells held fixed, plus a second-order certificate for a soft fit (Theorem 12). None of these is global.
- On the cubesat table K = 1 gives a seven-metric contending set. K = 2 is chosen by "the package's checks" and gives two terms, with 0.74 and 0.26 of near-frontier designs. The paper says the K choice is a modelling choice, like the number of retained components in PCA.
- The requirements are applied after the fit. Of 2,134 feasible designs, 28 pass all six requirements and sit in the near-frontier band; the paper then leaves the trade-off decision to the executives and says the method "prices the question".

## 3. What I would do with it next

I would fit it to a table from my own pipeline, starting with K = 1 and the default sharp fit. I would check the contending sets under resampling of the rows, which the paper says it does not do at K = 1. I would look at PCA on the near-frontier chart as a downstream reading, which the paper uses in Figure 6, and I would want to know whether the fit's positions agree with PCA scores on the same table. I would also try the regressor-based proposal step on a small table of my own.

## 4. What I would say to a colleague

It is a constrained, one-sided fit of a log-linear surface to a table of designs, in the family of frontier estimation and geometric programming. It returns exponents that say which metrics trade against which at a stated temperature, and a level per design. It is conditional on a conditioning choice and on K, and it is not a Pareto front and not a causal model. The guarantees are local, the proofs are for the mathematics not the floating point, and the paper is careful about which results are proved, which are observed, and which are open.

## 5. What I believe the method does not do, and what the paper does not claim

From the text, the method does not:
- give a global optimum of the several-term fit (Q1 is open) or a stopping guarantee beyond a pigeonhole count on partitions;
- give statistical consistency, identifiability under biased observations, or sampling error on the exponents (Q2, Q3, Q4 are open; the paper says exponents "are not estimates with a sampling error");
- recover the Pareto boundary rather than the supporting envelope of the sample (Q4);
- say whether the hypotheses suit a particular table, which the paper assigns to human validation;
- give causal statements about the designs;
- replace PCA or Pareto analysis; the paper says it "claims nothing as novel";
- handle discrete design variables within a category, or a convergence theorem for the outer search loop (Section 7 is "preliminary", with no results).

The paper explicitly does not claim that its proposals reliably cross V = 1, and it says the three proposals "illustrate and do not estimate a rate".

## 6. Points of confusion, stops and imprecisions, in the order I met them

- Abstract: "a monomial term in n metrics characterizes an n-lemma". I read n as the number of metrics. Definition 6 then says the contending set's size "names it", and the results talk about a "7-lemma" in a 13-metric table. So n is the size of the contending set, not the metric count. I had to work this out from Definition 6.
- Abstract and Section 1: "Like PCA, HTS is an unsupervised learning method". Later, Section 2 says "This work is not a PCA of any kind." The analogy is in setting, not method. I expected to read it as PCA-like throughout.
- Section 1, "Main results": "The one-term fit ... is convex in w, so every minimizer is global, though uniqueness is not claimed". Then Theorem 3: "J_tau is convex, not claimed strictly." I was not sure whether "global" was claimed for the fit or only for the convex objective. I took the convex reading.
- Definition 1 and Section 5. Definition 1 says the fitted surface is "V = 1 at finite tau ... some designs lie beyond it", while the supporting envelope is the tau = infinity fit "which none of the rows of the design table exceeds". Section 5 then says the fit "puts a target fraction of designs above V = 1: about a tenth". I had to keep three objects apart; the envelope/surface distinction did not stick on first reading.
- Definition 2 and the name "hyperbolic". The title's "hyperbolic" is explained as "the rectangular hyperbola m1 m2 = const" when w1 = w2. For unequal weights the level sets are generalized hyperbolas. I expected the name to be a precise object, and it is a special case.
- Definition 3: "Term aggregation: the formula is the sum. The package's mean (kappa = infinity: the maximum) subtracts the constant..." I read "the formula is the sum" and then "kappa = infinity is the maximum" as two different aggregators. The sentences do not say which is the fit of record until the last one ("The fits of record ... are sharp (kappa = infinity)").
- Definition 4 and Lemma text: the band is written "|log V| < 1/tau" in Definition 4, with the note that "the band here is the open one, as the code counts it", while the same passage says the bound in Appendix B is on "the closed band |log V| <= 1/tau". I could not settle from the text whether the open or closed band is meant in the claims.
- Definition 6: three thresholds, "10^-12 numerically zero", "above 10^-9 ... in the contending set", "above 10^-3 ... participates". Section 5 then says "An exponent above the participation threshold (10^-3) participates (Definition 6 sets the three thresholds side by side)". The three thresholds are a convention, the paper says so; I expected a single rule and had to hold three in mind.
- Figure 1 (synthetic). The caption and the legend agree (term 1 dilemma of 2 and 3, w = (0, 0.35, 0.65)). The caption says "the point drawn ... may lie a little inside that face". Term 1's exponent on metric 1 is exactly zero, so the point sits on the edge. This was consistent, but I had to read the caption twice to see why a zero exponent is still a dilemma of 2 and 3.
- Section 3, "Stage 1 evaluates, and the evaluation is a black box to the method". Good. Then "Map and territory" with "All models are wrong, some are useful". Clear.
- Section 3, "Assumptions" A1 to A10. There are ten assumptions plus R1 to R3, with A9 and A10 both about noise and A5 separately about noise. The paper's note that "A10 is not used: it only maps an attribute's error to this noise" helped. Keeping the letter codes straight took most of the reading effort.
- Section 3, "The package tolerates a zero metric by leaving that row out of the fit, without a warning, and N counts the other rows." I expected either a warning or an error. A silent drop of a row, with N adjusted, is a choice that matters for a sklearn user and I would want to see it stated in the estimator docs.
- Section 4, Definition 5 and the chart. "Z = B u ... (Z, log V) is the chart" and then "log V is the energy differential (level offset)". As a sklearn user I expected transform to return the chart, and I did; the naming "energy differential" for log V was new. The sign convention (negative inside, positive beyond) is stated in Definition 2, not at the first use of score_samples.
- Section 3, "The estimator": "score_samples returns the energy differential." The sklearn convention is that score_samples is higher-is-better. Here a design inside the surface has negative log V, so the sign convention is the reverse of what I would assume from the name. I had to infer this from "negative inside, positive beyond".
- Section 5, "Theorem 2 ... the facet-normal reading" and R1 are stated as conditions for a reading, not for the theorem. I stopped at R1, read that "R1 is a tau = infinity condition and does not give [the interior case]", and had to go back and forth to see which results need it.

## 7. Terms whose meaning I had to guess, with my guess

- n-lemma: the set of metrics that contend in one term; n is that set's size (the paper's Definition 6 confirms this). My first guess, number of metrics, was wrong.
- contending set: the metrics whose exponent in the exact-maximum fit is above 10^-9. Guessed from the definition, a support set.
- invariant value V: the weighted geometric mean scaled to one on the surface. Guessed from Definition 2.
- energy differential (log V): a design's level relative to the surface. Guessed as a signed offset; negative inside.
- level and position: the two coordinates of a design in the chart, Z and log V. Guessed from Definition 5.
- reach beta: the soft maximum of the scores along a direction. Guessed as the scale at which the surface sits.
- term share: fraction of near-frontier designs in a term's cell. Clear from Definition 7.
- cell: the set of designs whose largest term is that term. Guessed.
- term temperature kappa and fit temperature tau: a softness of the maximum over terms and over designs. I guessed from Definition 4; "infinity" is the sharp, exact case.
- near-frontier band: |log V| < 1/tau. Clear once Definition 4 was read.
- effective sample size: the inverse sum of squares of the support weights. Guessed from the text; it is 73.6 designs at the fit temperature.
- scaler: a per-metric rescaling so that one tau serves every metric. The paper explains it in parentheses.
- stable radius and assignment margin (gamma, r): the margin between a design's best term and its second best; the radius bounds how far term parameters can move without changing the labels. Guessed from Lemma 2.
- term gap and Frank-Wolfe gap: a measure of stationarity; I guessed "distance from the optimum over the simplex".
- Helmert basis: an orthonormal basis of the sum-zero space. I did not know the term; I took it from the parenthesis.
- critical cone and active face: standard optimisation terms I did not recall exactly; I guessed them from context.
- MoE and MoP: measures of effectiveness and performance, as the paper's Figure 3 caption explains.
- Pearson association in Figure 7: standard.
- HoroPCA and horospherical projection: a hyperbolic PCA variant; I took it as a named method from the cited paper.

## 8. The questions that remain for me

- Which of the paper's claims depend on the K = 2 choice, and what are the package's checks that choose it (Appendix D.15)? From the main text I cannot judge that choice.
- What does "machine-checked" cover: SymPy symbolic steps, or something stronger? The paper's own wording on the Lean prover suggests the former.
- Is the open/closed band (Definition 4) a real difference in any claim?
- How is the sharp fit's margin of 0.0003768 compared with the band of 0.0088, and what does that mean for the 37 designs the paper says sit near a seam?
- Does the exponent movement under resampling, which the paper leaves unassessed at K = 1, look like what the K = 2 Appendix D.15 reports?
- For sklearn use: what happens to a row with a zero metric in fit (silently dropped), and what does score_samples return in sign and scale?
- How does one choose the conditioning, which the paper says "defines the question"? A scaler changes the contending sets; I want to know what the default is and why.

## 9. Would I proceed to the appendices, and why

Yes, I would, but I could not, since the supporting material is on a separate page I did not open. My reason is that several claims the main text leans on are either deferred to an appendix or stated as tests the appendix runs: the K choice (D.15), the 60-versus-20 recovery numbers (D.14), the requirement pricing (D.17), and the margin and status numbers (D.8). The main text states the results, but the evidence for the choices that drive the case sits in those appendices. I would also read Appendix B to see how the lemma scripts relate to the proof sketches, since the main text says the sketches are "the idea, the script the check", and I could not judge that relation from the text alone.
