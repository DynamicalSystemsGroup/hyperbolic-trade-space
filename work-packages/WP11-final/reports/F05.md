## 1. What this is about

A way to summarise a large table of candidate engineering designs, each scored on many "larger is better" metrics (13 in the case study, a cubesat with attitude control, power, mass and cost). The method fits a reference surface to the log-metrics. In the one-term case this is a weighted geometric mean V(m) = e^{-β} Π m_j^{w_j}, with w on the simplex and β chosen so that V = 1 on the surface. A design with V > 1 lies beyond the surface and V < 1 lies inside it. The weights w are read as elasticities (how many percent of metric j trade for a percent of metric i). With several terms, the surface is the max (or soft-max) of K such geometric means, a posynomial in the metrics. Each term governs a "cell" of designs, and the set of metrics that carries each term is called its "n-lemma" (1 metric = binds alone, 2 = dilemma, 3 = trilemma, and so on). The paper presents this as a reading of what a sampled design space shows. It explicitly says the surface is not the Pareto boundary, and it says the method is not novel and does not replace PCA or Pareto analysis.

## 2. The main results, as I now understand them

- Definitions: V is homogeneous of degree 1 under a common scaling of the metrics (Thm 1). Log V is linear in the log-metrics u: log V = w·u − β.
- The one-term fit minimises J_τ(w), a log-mean-exp (entropic soft max) over designs of the scores w·u_i. This runs over the simplex.
  - Its gradient is U^T p with p = softmax(τUw), and the simplex KKT conditions are stated (Thm 2).
  - Its Hessian along a direction v is τ Var_p(v·u) ≥ 0, so the problem is convex and every minimiser is global (Thm 3). Uniqueness is explicitly not claimed.
  - β is the log-mean-exp, not log-sum-exp. The two differ by (log N)/τ, and LME sits between the mean and the max (Thm 4).
- Reading the fit:
  - Along a term, exponents give elasticities −w_i/w_j. A design with V < 1 reaches the surface by improving one metric by V^{-1/w_j} or all by 1/V (Thm 5). I checked these two expressions by hand and they are right.
  - The chart (Z, log V), with Z = Bu the scale-free position, is a bijection (Thm 6).
  - Conditioned attributes can be pulled back (Thm 7).
- Several terms:
  - Φ_K is a log-sum-exp-type function of the terms. It lies between the max and the max plus (log K)/κ, and e^{κΦ_K} is a posynomial (Thm 8).
  - A maximiser of V, or of Φ_K at κ = ∞, is weakly Pareto-efficient (Thm 9). It is efficient if every exponent of an active term is positive.
- The multi-term fit is not convex (Thm 10(i) by an instance). Three levels of guarantee are offered.
  - Level 1: the sharp assign-and-refit alternation does not raise the mean slack F. It terminates finitely only if term minimisers are unique and a tie rule is fixed. Stationarity and convergence otherwise are not claimed (Thm 11).
  - Level 2: a computable "term gap" G_k bounds suboptimality of each term fit (Lemma 1), and so gives an approximate certificate for the returned run with cells held fixed (Cor 2).
  - Level 3: if every term is an exact minimiser and the assignment margin γ is positive, the point is a local minimiser of F inside a stable radius r (Lemma 2, Prop 3).
  - For the soft-weight version, the soft slack is majorised by a fixed-weight objective (Thm 10(ii)). That objective is a generalised posynomial (Thm 10(iii)), and a second-order sufficient condition on H = D − κM is offered (Thm 12).
- Noise on the log-metrics acts like a ridge (τ/2) wᵀΣw on the objective and shifts exponents to first order by −(PCP)⁺PΣw. The shift is that of the minimiser of an "expected noisy objective", not of one noisy fit (Cor 1).
- Empirical, flagged as numerical:
  - On the cubesat, K = 1 puts about 0.26 on unit cost, 0.20 on available power, 0.16 on pointing error and 0.15 on fleet cost. Five metrics get exponent 0 at every temperature (Fig 4).
  - K = 2 gives two terms with 8 and 7 contending metrics (Fig 7).
  - The terms separate contending sets that one term blends.
  - Open questions Q1–Q4 and the monotonicity of the "search" are listed as not established.

## 3. What I would do next

- Read the proofs of Thm 3 and Thm 10(i) and the appendix instance behind Thm 10(i).
- Check, on my own, that at τ = ∞ the K = 1 fit is the LP min_w max_i w·u_i over the simplex. By minimax duality that is the point of the convex hull of the (conditioned) log-metrics with the largest minimum coordinate, and w are its dual multipliers. If that is right, the "elasticities" are dual variables of a very specific balance problem, and I want to see whether the paper says so.
- Reproduce Figure 4's n_eff and the fraction curve on a toy GP-type frontier where I know the answer.
- Read how the metrics are conditioned (scaled and centred), since I think the exponents depend on it.
- Test the sharp alternation on synthetic data, including cases with collinear metrics, to see how often the non-uniqueness the paper concedes shows up.

## 4. What I would tell a colleague

It is a log-space "Cobb–Douglas / max-of-affine" envelope fitted by a convex log-mean-exp program. The single-term math is clean and the convexity claim (Hessian = τ·variance) is correct as I verified it. The multi-term fit is k-means-like block coordinate descent. The paper is candid that it proves monotone descent of one specific idealised variant and a local optimality conditional on a margin, and it does not prove convergence or global optimality. The case-study findings are labelled numerical. Treat the weights as properties of this sample and this scaling, not as properties of the system.

## 5. What the method does not do, and what the paper does not claim

- It does not find the Pareto boundary. The paper says so (Def 1, Thm 9 context).
- It does not give a global optimum for K > 1, uniqueness of the one-term minimiser, or convergence of the alternation (stated).
- The surface is a function of a chosen temperature τ, and τ is chosen by a convention (10% of designs above V = 1). So the surface is not a property of the system.
- My reading is that the fitted model class has a convex region in log-metric space (max of affine functions). If so, a log-nonconvex attainable set cannot be represented faithfully. I did not see this stated in the material I had.
- The claims about the cubesat are presented as numerical, and the PCA reading is "a reading and not a result".
- Zero-exponent metrics are invisible to V. In the case study that is 5 of 13 at K = 1, so a maximiser of V is only weakly efficient there.

## 6. Where I was confused, stopped, or found something imprecise (in the order met)

1. Figure 1 caption: "The face that holds the point names the term's n-lemma: a corner, one metric binds alone; an edge, a dilemma…". Definition 6 says instead that the n-lemma is named by "the support (exponents above 10^-9) of the exact-maximum (τ=∞) fit to the term's cell". I expected one definition. The figure uses the exponent vector at the fit temperature, and the definition uses the τ = ∞ fit to the cell. They agree in Fig 7's term 1 (8 positive exponents, "8-lemma"), but the text does not say they coincide in general. "1-lemma" for a single binding metric is also an odd use of the word.
2. Figure 2: the legend says "swap" and "tune" are "each marked once, in the part it belongs to", but the caption says "so are the ranges, which the view does not draw". I could not tell how large or what shape the configuration space is, and hence what the sampled designs cover.
3. Figure 3 box: "SlewExperiment … returns 10 of 13 metrics, computed with no slew: pointing error, total mass, available power. From the measurement: control effort (passed on) and settling time (not returned)." The caption says "the settling time comes from the measured slew directly". I counted 11 arrows leaving the SlewExperiment box, including to unit cost and fleet cost, yet the box says those come from "an analysis each". I could not reconcile "10 returned" with the arrows.
4. Figure 4, bottom panel: the exponent columns from τ = 3 to τ = ∞ look almost identical. I expected a log-mean-exp at small τ to behave like the mean of w·u (linear, so a vertex solution unless the scores are centred) and at large τ like a minimax. They give almost the same weights here, which surprised me. I could not tell from the material whether it is a feature of this data or of the scaling.
5. Figure 4 top and Definition 4: "the fit temperature, the temperature at which the fraction is within 0.01 of the target (a convention)". So the surface is defined by choosing τ to put 10% of designs beyond it. I read this as a quantile choice presented as a fit parameter. The effective sample size there is n_eff = 74, from about 2000 designs. It is not defined in the material I had, and I do not know how 13 exponents (and 26 at K = 2) are identified from 74 effective points.
5b. Figure 4 caption: "primary regime (aggressive controller gains)". "Regime" is not defined here.
6. Figure 5 caption/legend: "They reach the line and stop there" for the dark points. At the left of the plot (log power about −1.5) the dark points sit around 0.7 while the line is near 1.5. The caption says they are within 0.1 in log V of the line. Dividing by the unit-cost exponent 0.26, 0.1 in log V should be about 0.4 in the vertical axis. The slope of the line is about −0.76, which matches 0.20/0.26 from Fig 4 and Thm 5, so that part checks. The "reach and stop" claim holds on the right and not clearly on the left.
7. Figure 6 caption: "The surface is the flat line log V = 0 whatever the position". Given Thm 6 this is true by construction of the chart, so I could not see what the figure shows beyond the spread of designs below 0. "Energy differential" is a term I could not place. The x-axis is a PCA direction of the sample, and the caption says it is "not part of the model".
8. Figure 7: several metric pairs have correlation 1.00 or 0.99 (power margin / energy balance / available power; depth of discharge / battery peak power ratio at 1.00 and 0.97). In log space that is near-exact collinearity. Then the exponent split among them is not identified, and "uniqueness not claimed" (Thm 3) is not a technicality here. Only one of each collinear group gets weight. I did not see this addressed.
9. Figure 7 and its caption: "negative: tension" for the Pearson correlation among near-frontier designs. Selecting designs near a surface w·u = β induces negative correlation among the metrics even for independent metrics (selection on a sum). I could not tell whether the "tension" reading separates that effect.
10. Figure 8 caption: "in A one region trades pointing error against control effort". In Fig 7 control effort has exponent 0.00 in both terms of the 13-metric K = 2 fit. These must be separate fits on three metrics. The material does not say how a metric of zero weight in the full fit can bind in a subset. Panel B's star "1" sits at the corner on the edge of the triangle, where the label says "depth of discharge alone".
11. Table 1 and Table 2 captions: no table body, so I could not check what "how this work differs" or "executives' measures" mean.
12. Main results, first paragraph: "a weighted geometric mean of the metrics that doubles when every metric doubles". True (Thm 1). It is homogeneity only under a common scaling. Proposition 1 shows only a uniform shift. I could not find anything about per-metric rescaling, and my reading is that the exponents depend on it. "Invariant" is therefore narrower than it first sounds.
13. Main results: "The one-term fit is a geometric program, convex in the logarithmic variables". The decision variable is the exponent vector w itself. The objective is log-mean-exp of affine functions of w on the simplex, which is a convex program in w. I would call it a GP only in the sense that w plays the role of the log variable. It is not a geometric program in the metrics m.
14. Definition 3: "Term aggregation: the formula is the sum. The package's mean (κ=∞: the maximum) subtracts the constant (log K)/κ from every level". At κ = ∞ that constant is 0. I could not tell which of sum, mean or max is the model that Theorems 10–12 and the case study use.
15. Theorem 2: "R1" and "R2", "A1…A9", and "I5, I8, I9, I14–I16" are not defined in the material I had. "For the facet-normal reading, w_j>0 for every j there" is not explained.
16. Theorem 9: for a fitted V the statement is that a maximiser of a weighted geometric mean with positive weights is weakly Pareto-efficient. That is a fact about any such V and not about the fit. In the case study 5 of 13 exponents are 0, so the efficient case does not apply.
17. Theorem 10 is a bundle. (i) says a symmetric convex function has a symmetric minimiser, but "in an instance (Appendix B, L16)" the fit's objective is lower at distinct terms than at the symmetric point. A single instance is what shows non-convexity. I could not tell how that instance was established (symbolic or numerical). (ii) is a majorisation bound with equality at the current weights, which is the condition for monotone descent of the soft-weight alternation. The statement never says that. Then the "three levels" (Thm 11, Lemma 1, Prop 3) are for the sharp alternation, and I could not see where the soft-weight alternation sits among them.
18. Theorem 11: "One accepted iteration (one that leaves no cell empty)". I could not tell what happens to an iteration that is not accepted, and whether the algorithm can stall.
19. Lemma 2 and Proposition 3: the stable radius is r = γ/(2·max‖(u_i, −1)‖). With thousands of designs and many near the dashed boundary of Fig 8, I expect γ to be tiny (positive but vanishing). Then "local minimiser within r" would say very little. I could not tell what γ was in the case study.
20. Theorem 12: "the first-order conditions hold exactly at the point, not within a tolerance". An iterative solver never delivers that. H = D − κM is a difference of PSD matrices, so positive definiteness is not automatic. I could not tell how the certificate is used in practice.
21. Corollary 1: the ridge comes from Proposition 2, which is a cumulant, log E exp. The corollary then speaks of "the shift of the minimiser of the expected noisy objective". The log of an expectation is not the expectation of a log. By Jensen they differ, so I could not tell which object is minimised.
22. Definition 6 versus Figure 4: the support threshold is 10^-9 in Def 6, the "participation threshold" is 0.001 in Fig 4, and the text points to "Section 5" and "the glossary" for the latter. A reader of only these pieces has two thresholds with different jobs.

## 7. Terms whose meaning I guessed

- "Conditioned metric": a log-scaled or centred, strictly positive, larger-is-better transform of an attribute (Fig 5 axes, Thm 7). It is probably standardised to a typical size, as Fig 8's legend suggests.
- "Reach" β: the soft or hard maximum of the scores along a direction, so the offset that makes the surface touch the data.
- "Slack": the gap between the surface level and a design's level (a negative log V).
- "Cell": the set of designs for which a term is the largest. It plays the role of a k-means cluster.
- "Near-frontier": designs with |log V| < 1/τ.
- "Share": the fraction of near-frontier designs in a term's cell.
- "Energy differential": log V (stated in Def 5).
- "Contending set": the metrics with nonzero exponent in the exact-maximum fit to a cell.
- "Regime": a setting of the simulation (here, aggressive controller gains).
- "Elasticity": as in Def 8.
- "Level of a design": log V.
- "Support offset": β_k = J_τ(Uw_k).

## 8. Questions I am left with

- Is the K = 1 problem at τ = ∞ literally the max-min point of the convex hull of the log-metrics, so the exponents are LP duals? And how much do they depend on the per-metric scaling that the analyst chooses?
- What restriction does the max-of-affine model class impose on the shape of the attainable set in log space, and what happens for a log-nonconvex frontier?
- How stable are the exponents and the contending sets under resampling, given n_eff = 74 at the fit temperature? The exact-maximum support is a vertex of an LP determined by a few extreme designs.
- Is the near-frontier negative correlation (Fig 7) partly produced by the selection itself?
- With near-collinear metrics, what do the zero versus positive exponents mean?
- What do Q1–Q4 ask?
- What is the monotonicity question about the search in section 7?
- What is the realised γ and r in the case study?
- Which κ and which aggregation (sum, mean, max) underlie the reported K = 2 fit?
- Why does choosing τ by the 10% convention suit decision use, and how sensitive are the reported shortlist and Table 2 to it?

## 9. Would I read the whole paper?

Yes, the theory part. The convex core is correct as stated. The honest labelling of what is and is not proved is unusual. The multi-term guarantees are specific enough that I can check each one. I would go to the appendix lemmas for Thm 10(i), Thm 11 and Thm 12, and to the sections on conditioning and the choice of τ. I would read the case study more cautiously, as a numerical illustration. Two points would decide how far I trust the interpretation: the dependence of the exponents on scaling and temperature, and the collinear metrics.
