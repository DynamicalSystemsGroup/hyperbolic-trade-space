# G07
Head: c23cf60

## 1. What it is about

A way of summarising a set of candidate designs, each scored on several outcome measures, by one fitted "reference surface". The surface is built from a weighted geometric mean of the measures, which the paper calls the invariant value V. Its exponents are read as trade-off rates between measures. The worked example is a cubesat model (power, mass, attitude control) with thirteen traded metrics, and the figures suggest a buyer could use it to see which measures carry the weight in the design space and how much one measure must improve for a design to reach the surface. The paper is explicit that the surface is not the Pareto frontier, is not a ranking, and does not replace PCA or Pareto analysis.

## 2. The main results, as I now understand them

- One-term fit: V = e^(-beta) times the product of m_j^(w_j), with the weights on the simplex. It is fitted as a geometric program, convex in the log variables, so any local minimiser is global. Uniqueness is not claimed.
- Exponents are elasticities. On the surface, the percent change of metric j per percent change of metric i is -w_i/w_j. A design below the surface reaches it either by improving metric j alone by a factor V^(-1/w_j), or every metric by 1/V (Theorem 5).
- A maximiser of V is weakly Pareto-efficient, and efficient when every weight is positive (Theorem 9).
- Several terms (K > 1): the fit is not convex in the terms. The paper gives three levels of guarantee and an approximate certificate, but claims neither stationarity nor convergence of the alternating procedure (Theorem 11, Corollary 2).
- A soft fit has a second-order certificate, but only if the first-order conditions hold exactly (Theorem 12).
- Noise: measurement noise moves the exponents by a ridge-type shift, for the expected objective, not for any one noisy fit (Corollary 1).
- Example, as read from the figures. At the chosen fit temperature tau = 42, the single-term exponents are unit cost 0.26, available power 0.20, pointing error 0.16, fleet cost 0.15, momentum headroom 0.11, settling time 0.07, depth of discharge 0.04 and total mass 0.02; the other six metrics are zero (Figure 4). The two-term fit gives term 1 mainly peak slew power (0.43) and pointing error (0.21), and term 2 mainly unit cost (0.25) and available power (0.22) (Figure 7). In Figure 5 the slope of the line in the available-power and unit-cost plane is about -0.8; the exponents give -0.20/0.26, about -0.77, so the plotted slope agrees with the stated weights.
- Table 2, caption only: the baseline and the designs the rules pick; the cheapest unit needs the largest fleet. The rules and the shortlist are in an appendix I was not given.

## 3. What I would do with it next

I would read the case sections (8 and 9), the discussion, and Table 2 with the rules in the appendix, to see which design the method picks and on what economics. I would check how the weights move with the temperature choice, since Figure 4 shows them changing. I would ask the analysts how fleet cost and unit cost are both counted. This could alter a decision of mine only at the level of how I weigh unit cost against available power in a bus specification. I would not use it for the fleet size or the launch purchase, because the paper says it does not tell the schedule, the risk, what to buy beyond the modelled parts, or what a larger fleet is worth.

## 4. What I would say about it to a colleague

"It fits one weighted geometric mean of our metrics to the sampled designs and reads trade-off rates off the exponents. In the cubesat example, unit cost and available power carry most of the weight and total mass almost none, which I would not accept without a check. It is a reading of sampled designs, not a frontier, and it says nothing about schedule or risk."

## 5. What I believe the method does not do, and what the paper does not claim

- It does not give the Pareto boundary, a ranking, or a schedule or risk answer. The paper says so (Table 2 caption, and "it is not the Pareto boundary" in the main results).
- For several terms it claims no stationarity and no convergence of the alternation, and it claims no uniqueness for one term.
- The certificate for the soft fit assumes exact first-order conditions, not a tolerance, which a numerical optimiser does not return.
- The noise result is first order and about the expected objective. It "does not say whether the rates are right".
- It claims nothing as novel and does not replace PCA or Pareto analysis.
- The proofs and the appendices are withheld from me, so I take the references to them on trust.
- The surface depends on the regime chosen ("primary regime (aggressive controller gains)") and on the temperature convention, which the paper calls a convention.

## 7. Terms whose meaning I had to guess, with my guess

- invariant value V: a weighted geometric mean scaled so that the surface is V = 1.
- fitted reference surface: the level set V = 1 of the fitted form.
- supporting envelope: the tau = infinity fit, an outer bound on the sampled designs.
- attainable set: the realisable designs, which the paper contrasts with the frontier.
- fit temperature tau and term temperature kappa: how sharply the soft maximum is taken over designs and over terms.
- log-mean-exp versus log-sum-exp: the same thing up to a constant that does not move the exponents.
- near-frontier band: designs with |log V| less than 1/tau.
- cell, term share: the designs whose largest term is this one, and the fraction of near-frontier designs in it.
- reach beta_k: how far the data extend along the term's direction.
- contending set and n-lemma: the support of the exact-maximum fit within a cell, and the count of its members.
- support threshold 10^-9 and participation threshold 0.001: the first for support, the second for displaying exponents as nonzero.
- ascent direction and chart: the gradient of log V, and the rewriting of the metrics as position plus level.
- energy differential: my guess is that it is another name for log V.
- conditioned metric: an attribute transformed so that larger is better.
- MoE and MoP: measure of effectiveness and measure of performance, my guess from the stereotypes.
- launch cost (derived): a cost computed from the total mass.
- ridge: a quadratic penalty added to the objective by noise.
- level 1, level 2, level 3, and the stopping declaration: the three guarantee levels in the order of Theorem 11, Lemma 1 and Proposition 3, with the stopping declaration being the point the procedure stops.
- certificate: a sufficient condition for a local minimum.
- trilemma and dilemma: a term whose exponent vector sits in the interior or on an edge of the triangle.
- favours, fixed total, executives' measures, the rules, regime, stable radius, gamma, R1, I6, I14, I15, I16: undefined in what I have. My guesses are the obvious ones: "favours" as the direction a metric improves; "fixed total" as the constraint on the simplex; the rules as the selection procedure in the appendix; regime as the controller-gain setting; gamma as the assignment margin.

## 8. The questions that remain for me

- Why does total mass carry 0.02 weight at the chosen temperature and none in term 1, when launch cost depends on it? Does launch cost depend on mass alone?
- Are power margin, energy balance and available power near-duplicates (correlations 0.97 to 1.00)? If so, how do they split the weight?
- Unit cost and fleet cost both carry weight. How is a fleet of N units counted, and why does the sign of their correlation flip between terms (-0.44 in term 1, 0.56 in term 2)?
- The effective sample size is 74 at tau = 42. How stable are the weights across different samples of designs?
- The temperature convention (fraction above V = 1 near 0.1) moves the weights: unit cost is 0.26 at tau = 42 and total mass goes from 0.02 to zero at infinity. Which temperature do the case's decisions rest on?
- What does "primary regime (aggressive controller gains)" mean for a buyer, and does the purchase decision lie in that regime?
- Which fleet cost does the rule prefer, given that the cheapest unit needs the largest fleet?
- What would a launch purchase need beyond this, given that the paper excludes schedule, risk, parts beyond the model and the value of a larger fleet?
- Does the second-order certificate apply to the numerical output of the software?
