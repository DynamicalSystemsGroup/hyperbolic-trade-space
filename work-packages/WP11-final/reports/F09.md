## 1. What this is about

A method for trade studies: take a table of evaluated designs (configurations, then attributes, then conditioned metrics that are strictly positive and larger-is-better), fit a posynomial "reference surface" to the outer edge of the log-metric data, and read from it which metrics trade against which. Each monomial term is a piece of the surface and is reported with its contending metrics (an "n-lemma") and elasticities. The paper also sets out a chart (a position along the surface plus a level relative to it), a sklearn-style estimator, and a worked cubesat case (3,000 random designs, 2,134 feasible, 13 metrics) with a K = 1 fit, a K = 2 fit, and a short decision reading for the sponsor and operator. It positions the method against Pareto methods, DEA, frontier estimation, PCA, HoroPCA, GP/max-affine fitting and MDAO.

## 2. The main results, as I understand them

- The surface is where the invariant value V (a weighted geometric mean of the metrics, doubling when all metrics double) equals one (Theorem 1). It is not the Pareto boundary.
- The one-term fit is a geometric program, convex in log coordinates, so every minimiser is global; uniqueness is not claimed (Theorems 2, 3).
- With one term, a maximiser of V is weakly Pareto-efficient over the set it is maximised on (Theorem 9); the converse is not claimed.
- The several-term fit is not convex in the terms. It is given three levels of guarantee (descent, numerical stationarity by a term gap, local optimality where an assignment margin is positive) and a stopping declaration. A soft fit has a second-order certificate (Theorem 12).
- On the cubesat case: K = 1 gives a 7-metric contending set; K = 2 separates two terms (8 and 7 metrics), with peak slew power appearing only in one term; the K = 2 margin is small against the band 1/tau, and the paper says so.

## 3. What I would do next with it

I would take a table from one of my own studies (the configurations, the attributes, a conditioning rule I could defend), run the fit at K = 1 first, check the fraction above V = 1 and the effective sample size at the chosen temperature, and then ask whether the contending set is one I can explain to an engineer. I would rerun it when the inputs change, as the paper invites. I would not yet use the requirement-after-fit reading or the "propose, predict, check" loop for a decision.

## 4. What I would tell a colleague

It is a way to get, from a table of evaluated designs, a short list of which metrics trade against which, with numbers for the trade rate, and a clear statement of how far each design sits beyond the fitted edge. It is not a Pareto front and not a PCA. The paper is unusually explicit that interpretation and validation are human work, and that the certificate does not carry over to designs outside the table.

## 5. What the method does not do, and what the paper does not claim

By the paper's own statements: it does not establish global convergence of the alternation (Q1), statistical consistency under noise (Q2), identifiability of active metrics under biased observations (Q3), or that the surface recovers the Pareto boundary rather than the supporting envelope of the sample (Q4). It does not give causal exchange rates; exponents describe the sampled designs. It does not select designs (the executives do), does not price the schedule, risk or payload, does not handle discrete design variables, and the search loop of Section 7 has no results. It says it claims nothing as novel and does not replace PCA or Pareto analysis. It does not claim that a score built on V minus one measures whether anyone wants a design.

## 8. The questions I am left with

- How is a numerically stationary soft fit "certified" when Theorem 12 requires the first-order conditions to hold exactly?
- Is fleet size a design variable of the fit, or an outside choice applied by the decision rules?
- Does the 0.56 M USD figure in the decision paragraph refer to the tracker alone, or to a tracker, wheel and fleet-count change together?
- Where, if anywhere, is the monotonicity of the search stated?
- What is the four-metric chart, and which temperature gives the band 0.0935?
- Are the requirement thresholds independent of the data, given that they are "read from the data's pass fractions"?
- Is the optimum for K = 1 unique in the case, and is that observed or derived?
