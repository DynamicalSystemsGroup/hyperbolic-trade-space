## 1. What it is about

The paper proposes one scalar, which it calls the invariant value V(m) = e^(-beta) times the product of m_j^(w_j): a weighted geometric mean of several larger-is-better metrics, with the exponents w on the simplex and beta a "reach" that sets the level. The set V = 1 is a fitted reference surface. Each design is placed by a chart: its position along that surface, plus its level log V. The one-term model is extended to K terms by a soft maximum (a posynomial-style sum of monomials, taken in the sharp limit for the fits of record). The worked case is a cubesat model with thirteen traded metrics. The paper also gives each term a named trade-off, the "n-lemma", read from the set of metrics that contend for that term's exponents. The fit temperature is set by a convention: about a tenth of the designs are allowed to lie above the surface.

## 2. The main results, as I now understand them

- One term. Minimising the reach over the simplex is a convex problem in w (Theorem 3, convexity of J_tau), so any minimiser is global. Uniqueness is not claimed. The fit is not a regression: Theorem 11 makes the reach beta_k equal to J_tau(w_k), so the fit minimises the soft maximum of the design scores along w.
- Exponents are elasticities (Definition 8, Theorem 5), and a design below the surface can be brought onto it by a factor on one metric or on all of them (Theorem 5).
- The chart (position along the surface, log V) is a bijection with the metrics (Theorem 6). Scaling a design by V leaves it on the surface (Theorem 1).
- A maximiser of V over any set of designs is weakly Pareto-efficient, and efficient when every w_j is positive (Theorem 9).
- K > 1. The soft-weight alternation descends the mean slack (Theorem 11), and finishes after at most K^N + 1 iterations by a pigeonhole argument. Three levels of guarantee follow: a term gap as a numerical stationarity measure (Lemma 1), local optimality with the cells held fixed (Proposition 3), and a second-order certificate for a soft fit (Theorem 12).
- Noise. Gaussian noise on the log-metrics adds a ridge term (tau/2) w' Sigma w to the expected objective and moves the exponents to first order (Corollary 1). The identity is exact, and the paper says the shift is the minimiser's shift, not a single fit's.
- Cubesat. Two terms hold different contending sets, which is observed and not proved (main results; Figure 7 shows contending sets of eight and seven metrics).
- My own derivation, which the paper does not state in the material I have: at tau = infinity and one term, the reach is max_i w.u_i, so the K = 1 exact fit is a linear programme (minimise the largest linear score over the simplex). Its contending set is then the support of an LP solution, which is sparse by construction. If that is right, the n-lemma is a property of which designs are binding at the optimum. I would want to check this before relying on it.

## 4. What I would say to a colleague

It is a log-linear envelope of the designs with a smooth maximum and a convention that about one design in ten sits above it. The efficiency result and the homogeneity results look like facts about weighted geometric means that the paper could cite rather than claim. What is new in the paper's own terms is the n-lemma, a reading of which metrics trade within a cell, and the chart. The paper says it claims nothing as novel, which is to its credit, so the question for the colleague is whether the n-lemma reading survives resampling and a change of convention. That is the part I would test first.

## 5. What the method does not do, and what the paper does not claim

From the material given:
- The fitted surface is not the Pareto boundary (Definition 1; main results).
- No consistency claim (Proposition 2 is "a population identity, with no consistency claim").
- Uniqueness of the minimiser is not claimed (Theorem 2, Theorem 3).
- Stationarity of the stop and convergence otherwise are not claimed (Theorem 11).
- The noise result says nothing about whether the elasticities are right (Corollary 1).
- The PCA reading is "a reading and not a result" (main results; Appendix D.16).
- The method does not tell the schedule, the risk, what to buy beyond the modelled parts, or what a larger fleet is worth (Table 2).
- The cubesat coupling claim is "observed and not proved" (main results).
- Four questions Q1 to Q4 are open (Section 6.3, named only in the main results).
- The outer loop of the search "may move inward on a step" (main results).
- The paper says it "claims nothing as novel and does not replace PCA or Pareto analysis" (main results).

## 7. Terms whose meaning I had to guess

- invariant form and invariant value V: a scale-free name; I took it to mean the surface is unchanged by rescaling the metrics.
- fitted reference surface: the set V = 1 at a finite fit temperature.
- supporting envelope: the tau = infinity fit, from which no design exceeds.
- attainable set and Pareto boundary: the set of realisable designs and its nondominated part, given in Definition 1.
- posynomial: a sum of monomials in the metrics, as in geometric programming.
- fit temperature tau and term temperature kappa: how hard the soft maximum is, for the fit and for the terms.
- reach beta: the soft maximum of the design scores along w.
- near-frontier band: designs with |log V| below 1/tau.
- chart and position Z: the sum-zero coordinates along the surface.
- n-lemma, contending set, cell, dilemma, trilemma: a named trade-off read from the metrics whose exponents are positive in the exact fit; I took dilemma and trilemma to count the metrics.
- term share: the fraction of near-frontier designs in a term's cell.
- elasticity: the ratio of two exponents, the percent change of one metric per percent change of another.
- ascent direction: the normal w of a term, the gradient of log V in the metrics.
- soft maximum and log-mean-exp: a smoothing of the maximum that is at most the maximum and at least the maximum minus log N over tau (Theorem 4).
- participation threshold: 1e-3 at the fit temperature.
- energy differential, level, level offset: log V.
- primary regime: a dataset variant, with aggressive controller gains.
- effective sample size n_eff: I took it to be a participation ratio of the soft weights; the paper does not define it in my material.
- "fixed total" and "stable terms that contend differently": both in Figure 8, undefined.
- "the rules" and "balanced between the terms" (Table 2): the rules are in Appendix D.19, which I cannot see; "balanced" is defined in the caption as the design whose two term levels are closest.
- executives: I took it to mean stakeholders.
