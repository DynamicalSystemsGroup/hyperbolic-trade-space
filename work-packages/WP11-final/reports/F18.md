## 1. What this is about

A method for reading a table of evaluated designs (configurations, each with several performance attributes). The attributes are first conditioned into strictly positive larger-is-better "metrics" m, with u = log m. For one term the method then finds an exponent vector w on the simplex such that the weighted geometric mean V(m) = e^(-β) ∏ m_j^(w_j) equals one on a "fitted reference surface", which is an upper edge of the data in log space. A design's log V is its "level" or "energy differential". The vector w is read as a set of elasticities, and the support of an exact-maximum fit is called the "n-lemma" of the metrics that contend. The chart (Z, log V) is an orthogonal rewrite of the log-metrics into "position along the surface" and "level". With K terms the model is a (soft) maximum of K such affine functions in log space, which makes it a generalised posynomial. The package is a scikit-learn estimator. The worked case is a cubesat model with 13 metrics and about 2,134 feasible designs.

## 2. The main results, as I understand them

In log space, with u = log m, everything is affine or log-sum-exp.

- Homogeneity: V(λm) = λV(m) and V is scale-equivariant. Uniform shifts move β by the shift and leave the minimiser alone (Theorem 1, Proposition 1). These are trivial and correct.
- K = 1 fit: minimise J_τ(w) = (1/τ) log mean_i exp(τ w·u_i) over the simplex. This is log-mean-exp of affine functions of w, so it is convex (Theorem 3: Hessian τ·Var_p(v·u) ≥ 0). Minimisers are global, with no uniqueness claim. The KKT conditions are g_j ≥ λ with complementary slackness (Theorem 2). I checked the algebra: it is correct.
- Log-mean-exp versus log-sum-exp differ by (log N)/τ and the bounds mean ≤ LME ≤ max hold (Theorem 4). Correct.
- Gaussian noise with covariance Σ adds a ridge (τ/2)w'Σw to the log of the expected objective, and shifts the minimiser to first order by -(PCP)^+PΣw (Corollary 1). I re-derived the cumulant identity. The paper says plainly that this is the minimiser of the expectation inside the log, not the expected minimiser of a noisy fit, and that the Jensen gap is measured, not proved (G-3).
- Elasticities are -w_i/w_j along the surface, and the factor to reach V = 1 is V^(-1/w_j) (Theorem 5). Correct for K = 1.
- Chart bijection (Theorem 6), exact pull-back through log-affine conditioning (Theorem 7), the K-term extension within (log K)/κ of the max (Theorem 8), and a V-maximiser is weakly Pareto-efficient (Theorem 9). The last one is weighted-sum scalarisation, true and not deep. The converse is explicitly not claimed.
- K > 1: the objective F(W) = -mean Φ_K is not convex in the terms (Theorem 10 (i), via a symmetry argument: a convex symmetric function is minimised at the all-equal point, and an instance goes below it). The soft alternation is a majorise-minimise step: log Σ e^(κs) ≥ Σα·κs + H(α), so F ≤ G_α - E with equality at the weights' own model, and G_α is a generalised posynomial in e^w. I verified the Gibbs-variational step. This is a sound descent argument for a soft fit.
- Sharp alternation guarantees (three levels, none global): (1) monotone descent and finite termination in at most K^N + 1 iterations by pigeonhole, given unique term minimisers and a fixed tie rule (Theorem 11). (2) A Frank-Wolfe-type term gap bounds f_k(w) - f_k* (Lemma 1). Correct. (3) With positive assignment margin γ, cells cannot change within a radius r = γ/(2 max‖(u_i,-1)‖), so W is a local minimiser of the mean slack (Lemma 2, Proposition 3), plus an approximate certificate with the cells held fixed (Corollary 2).
- Soft fit: the Hessian is D - κM, D = blockdiag((n_k/N) τ Cov_{p_k}(u)) and M the between-term ambiguity. A positive-definite Hessian on the active face's tangent space gives a strict local minimiser (Theorem 12). I checked D - κM by hand from F = -mean Φ_K with β_k = J_τ(w_k). It is consistent.
- Open (declared): global convergence (Q1), consistency (Q2), identifiability (Q3), and when the surface recovers the Pareto boundary (Q4). Search of the design space is future work with no results.
- Numerical on the case: a 7-metric contending set at K = 1, two terms with 8 and 7 metrics at K = 2, certified at K = 2 and 3 with margin 3.8e-4 and stable radius 2.55e-5. 16 of 20 planted tables are recovered. 28 designs remain on the shortlist after the requirements, and a decision between two fleets is priced, not made.

## 4. What I would tell a colleague

It is a careful piece of convex-analysis engineering. The one-term fit is a convex program, a softened LP min_w max_i w·u_i on the simplex, whose duals have a clean geometric meaning, and the guarantees for K = 1 are standard. The multi-term fit is a Magnani-Boyd-style alternation (a max-affine fit), and the paper states honestly that only local statements are made. The statements I could check were true as written. The weakest part of the story is not the mathematics but the gap between the "n-lemma" reading and what the fit identifies. For instance, the contending set is the support of a different (τ = ∞, cell-only, centred) problem from the one whose exponents are reported, and its uniqueness is observed, not proved. The case study is a worked example with a simulated cubesat, and its numbers are labelled illustrative.

## 5. What the method does not do, and what the paper does not claim

Method does not: produce the Pareto boundary (the K = 1 surface sits with about 10 percent of rows above it); give causal exchange rates (stated); identify uniqueness of exponents; give a global optimum for K > 1 (Q1); give any statistical consistency (Q2); handle non-log-convex structure; fit non-log-affine conditioning; provide a second-order certificate for the sharp fits of record, since Theorem 12 is for finite κ; or choose K for you. It is conditional on conditioning (a different scaler changes the contending set).

Paper does not claim: novelty, priority, uniqueness of minimisers, strict convexity, that the sample reaches the attainable set, that ascent in the loop of Section 7 converges (a submartingale conjecture, no conditions derived), that a decision follows from the fit (the "three claims" paragraph), or that the lemma scripts are formal verification (they are described as pencil-and-paper substitutes, and Lean is future work).

## 6. Where I was confused, stopped or found something imprecise (in order met)

1. Abstract and Section 1: "we do not make strong assumptions about the sampling distribution, only that the frontier is log-convex." Section 5(vi) later says "log-convex ... means Φ_K convex in u". The paper uses "log-convex surface" for what I would call a convex region in log coordinates (the set {Φ ≤ 0} is an intersection of halfspaces at κ = ∞). I could work this out, but the word "frontier" is overloaded: Definition 1 gives three objects and then Section 5 (vi) uses "log-convex" for the reference surface only.
2. Section 1, Main results: "The one-term fit is a geometric program in the exponents (x = e^w), convex in w". The decision variables are the exponents w, and a geometric program in x = e^w has posynomial terms with real (possibly negative) exponents τ·u_ij. The sentence is right, but "in the exponents" and "x = e^w" read as contradictory until you see the exponents are the log of the GP variables.
3. Section 1: "Main results ... local optimality with the cells held fixed (Lemma 2, Proposition 3)". Proposition 3 as stated is a true local-minimiser statement of the mean slack in the parameters (within radius r the argmax cells cannot change, so the objective equals the fixed-cell objective). "With the cells held fixed" fits Corollary 2 better. The two readings differ in strength, and the summary sentence blurs them.
4. Definition 2 versus Theorem 1: Theorem 1 defines V = ∏m^w, "the case β = 0 of Definition 2", then states V(m e^(-β)) = e^(-β)V(m). The statement mixes V with and without the e^(-β). Fine once read slowly, but a reader has to track which V.
5. Definition 4: "The near-frontier band is |log V| < 1/τ ... one inside the band weighs within a factor e of the weight a design on the surface would have". True from p_i ∝ e^(τ s_i), but the band as defined is symmetric and centred on log V = 0, while β is the soft maximum, so a large fraction of the weight is above the surface by design. I did not see why the band is a reasonable definition of "near-frontier" beyond the convention (G-9 says it is a convention).
6. Definition 6 (the contending set): the support of the exact-maximum fit to a term's cell, "centred on the cell's mean log-metric". At τ = ∞ with centred rows, min over the simplex of max_i w·ũ_i is a different problem from the term fit f_k over all rows used for the exponents. The text itself says "the last two [thresholds] read different fits". I found this the hardest object to follow: three fits (all rows centred at cell mean; cell rows alone centred; fit-temperature exponents) share the symbols f_k, J, β. Its uniqueness at K = 1 is "checked by a test on both regimes", so it is not a theorem.
7. Theorem 2 hypotheses: "for the facet-normal reading, w_j > 0 for every j at the fit temperature, taken on its own: R1 is a τ = ∞ condition and does not give it". I stopped here. Section 5's R1 says the balanced point lies in the relative interior of a facet, and then "the facet-normal reading of Theorem 2 ... rest on it" while Theorem 2 says R1 does not give what the reading needs. The L3.9 line confirms this. I read it as: R1 is not sufficient and the positivity is a separate hypothesis. The sentence in Section 5 about what R1 buys remained confusing.
8. Corollary 1: "moves the exponents to first order by δ = -(PCP)^+PΣw", "noise small". The proof sketch says "Its Hessian is τC", but the Hessian of the ridge objective J_τ + (τ/2)w'Σw is τ(C + Σ). The first-order formula drops Σδ (the "product of noise and shift set aside"). That is consistent with the L20 statement, but the main text gives the Hessian as τC without saying that Σ is dropped. Also "first order only: no bound on the remainder" (L20).
9. Theorem 10 (i): "lower at a point W with distinct terms than at the all-equal point, the average of W's cyclic shifts, where a convex symmetric F would be at most F(W)". I verified this is a valid disproof of convexity if the instance is correct, but it rests on an instance computed in L16 that I did not see. The text before it says F "is not convex (Theorem 10, part i)". So the non-convexity of the sharp-weight objective is exhibited by one instance, not shown for general data. The paper does not claim otherwise.
10. Theorem 11, finite termination: "the partitions before the stop are pairwise distinct, and there are K^N of them, so at most K^N + 1 iterations". It requires unique exact minimisers at each refit. The paper says Theorem 3 does not supply these, and elsewhere says uniqueness is not claimed, and the case has near-duplicate columns, which make the objective flat along their swap. So the termination hypothesis is not one the paper can show holds on its own case. The text says the bound is "not a practical stopping guarantee" and that D.14 records fits that reach the iteration cap. I was left unsure whether any fit of record satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem 11.
11. Section 6.2, "certified": "For a sharp fit, certified means that the assignment is fixed, the residuals and the term gaps are within tolerance, no cell is empty and the margin is positive". Then "the returned point is polished ... Proposition 3 and Theorem 12 hold at the exact point that the fit approximates". So the certificate is a statement at an exact point that is not the returned point. D.14 then lists seed 8 as "certified" at τ = 50 with a largest exponent error 0.20 and only 68 percent of frontier rows on the right term. ## 7. Terms whose meaning I had to guess

- "Hyperbolic" (title): the level sets ∏ m_j^(w_j) = c, hyperbola-like in the metrics, a rectangular hyperbola only when the two exponents are equal. Nothing to do with hyperbolic geometry (the paper is clear about this against HoroPCA). I guessed correctly, I think.
- "Reach" β: the soft maximum of the scores along w (log-mean-exp, not log-sum-exp). Defined, but I had to see Theorem 4 to be sure of the 1/N.
- "Energy differential": log V. Defined.
- "Term share": fraction of near-frontier designs in the cell of the term. Defined.
- "Cell": the designs for which a term is the largest. Defined, but the definition has three versions (argmax of the model, the label of the alternation, the partition of A8). The text says they coincide for sharp fits.
- "Stable radius", "margin": defined.
- "Participation threshold" 10^-3, "numerically zero" 10^-12, "contending" 10^-9: defined in Definition 6, three conventions.
- "Slack": the mean slack F = -mean Φ_K; negative for designs beyond the surface. I took "slack" to mean how far a design is below the surface.
- "Polish": Newton steps after the solver. Guessed from context.
- "Standard scaler / robust scaler": scikit-learn's. Known.
- "MoE/MoP": systems-engineering terms, glossed in the text.
- "Tangent" in "which term is tangent": the term whose hyperplane touches the data's edge there. My guess.

## 9. Questions I am left with

- Is the K = 1 minimiser at τ = ∞ unique under any condition that can be checked from the data, or only generically? The paper observes it on two datasets.
- How does the contending set change if the log-metrics are centred at a different point or scaled differently? The paper reports one alternative scaler (robust: 8 metrics, 6 of 7).
- What guarantees, if any, does the polished returned point carry, given that Proposition 3 and Theorem 12 are stated at the exact point?
- Why does the alternation fail on four planted tables with the thinnest term having 5, 5, 6 and 11 frontier rows, and does a different initialisation recover them? G-14 reports that 5, 20 and 50 restarts did not.
- What does K = 2 add over K = 1 for the executives' decision in Section 9, given that the near-frontier band used is the K = 1 band?
- Is "weakly Pareto-efficient maximiser" of any use when the maximiser is just the best-scored design under a positive-weight sum?
- How do the first-order claims of Corollary 1 behave when conditioning_ is small (the paper says the shift is large there)?
