## 1. What this is about

A method for looking at a set of evaluated designs, each scored on several qualities, and learning from the data how those qualities pull against each other. The page calls this the "shape of a trade space." The pitch is that the usual way of deciding, from documents and tables, loses the detail of the individual designs. The alternatives are described as "bespoke or opaque." The method claims to be a model you can trust because it shows its reasoning, says where the data do not reach, and can be rerun.

## 7. Terms I had to guess

- posynomial: a sum of products of powers, with positive coefficients. Guessed from the page's own gloss.
- invariant form: a formula that keeps the same shape whatever the units or scaling of the metrics. Pure guess.
- metric: a quality score, positive, where more is better.
- trade space: the set of all the combinations of qualities the designs achieve.
- frontier: the edge of what is achievable, where you cannot improve one quality without losing another.
- term: one of the pieces added together in the fitted formula, each standing for one kind of trade-off.
- exponent vector: the list of powers in one term, which would say how strongly each metric enters it.
- reference surface: the fitted curve or surface that the designs are compared against.
- unsupervised: fitting without a stated target to predict.
