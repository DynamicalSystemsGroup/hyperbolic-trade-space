## 1. What it is about

A paper that reads a set of designs, each with several metrics, through one scalar: the invariant value V, a weighted geometric mean of the metrics, scaled so that V = 1 on a fitted reference surface. The surface is fitted as a posynomial (a sum of K monomials). K = 1 is the monomial case, which the paper builds; K = 2 and the several-term fit are where most of the stated difficulty sits. The worked case is a cubesat model with thirteen traded metrics. The paper also offers a principal-component reading of the designs, and says it claims nothing novel and does not replace PCA or Pareto analysis.

## 2. The main results, as I understand them

- The reference surface is where V = 1. Doubling every metric doubles V, which is why the form is called invariant (Theorem 1). The surface is explicitly not the Pareto boundary (Definition 1).
- With one term, the fit is a geometric program in the logarithmic variables. I take that to be convex, so any minimiser is global, though uniqueness is not claimed (Theorems 2 and 3). The exponents are read as elasticities, and the chart (position along the surface, level log V) is a reversible rewriting of the metrics (Theorems 5 and 6).
- With one term, a maximiser of V is weakly Pareto-efficient, and efficient when every exponent is positive (Theorem 9).
- With several terms, the fit is not convex in the terms. The paper gives three levels of guarantee and a stopping declaration: a descent statement (Theorem 11), a numerical-stationarity measure (Lemma 1), a conditional local-optimality result (Proposition 3). A soft fit has a second-order certificate (Theorem 12).
- On the cubesat, the two-term fits separate contending sets that a single term blends. Figure 8 gives the examples: pointing error against control effort, settling time against control effort, and depth of discharge alone.
- The PCA reading of the near-frontier chart is offered as a reading of how the sampling exercised trade-offs, not as a result.

These are my readings of the text, not a view on whether they hold.

## 3. What I would do with it next

I would read the hypothesis labels (A, I, R) and the appendix proofs for the statements I cannot check from here. I would read Tables 1 and 2 themselves, which I have only as captions. I would go to the cubesat section to see which metrics drive each term, and I would look for the reason the fit temperature is chosen where it is.

## 4. What I would say to a colleague

The method scores designs by a weighted geometric mean of their metrics, fits a posynomial surface to the designs, and reads each design by its distance from that surface and by where it sits along it. For one term the fit is well posed and the exponents are elasticities. For several terms you get local statements and a stopping rule, not a global answer. The chart is reversible, so no information is lost by the rewrite. The fit depends on a temperature parameter whose choice drives a lot, and the paper says so.

## 5. What the method does not do, and what the paper does not claim

From the text: it does not give the Pareto boundary; it does not rank designs (Table 2 caption: log V is a distance, not a ranking); it does not say the schedule or the risk, what to buy beyond the modelled parts, or what a larger fleet is worth. For several terms it does not claim global optimality, uniqueness, convergence of the alternation, or stationarity of the stop (Theorem 11). Questions Q1 to Q4 and the monotonicity of the search are open. The PCA reading is not a result. The figures are readings of the sampled designs, not part of the model (Figure 6 caption). The paper claims no novelty.

## 7. Terms whose meaning I had to guess, with my guess

- posynomial: a sum of monomials, with positive coefficients.
- monomial: a single term, with exponents on the metrics.
- geometric program: an optimisation problem of that form, convex after taking logarithms. I did not know this class before.
- invariant value V: the weighted geometric mean, scaled to equal 1 on the surface.
- reference surface: the set where V = 1.
- soft maximum, log-mean-exp: a smooth version of the maximum, controlled by a temperature.
- fit temperature tau: the parameter that makes the soft maximum sharper as it grows; infinity is the exact maximum.
- term temperature kappa: how sharply the terms meet in the posynomial.
- participation threshold: a small number below which an exponent counts as zero at the fit temperature; I took it to be 0.001.
- contending set and n-lemma: the set of metrics whose exponents are nonzero in the exact-maximum fit, and its size, as I read them.
- cell: the designs whose largest term is a given term.
- support: the set of nonzero exponents, in most uses.
- reach: how far the data extend in a term's direction.
- term share: the fraction of near-frontier designs in one cell.
- elasticity: the percent change in one metric per percent change in another, along the surface.
- ascent direction: the direction of steepest increase of log V.
- KKT conditions: first-order optimality conditions for constrained problems. I knew the phrase, not the conditions.
- critical cone, active face, positive multiplier, tangent space: constrained-optimisation vocabulary I could partly guess from the word "tangent".
- ridge: a penalty term added to the objective; I took it as a quadratic penalty.
- slack, mean slack F: a non-negative gap; I did not pin down F.
- effective sample size (n_eff): the usual effective number of samples under weighting; I did not see its formula.
- energy differential (for log V): unexplained; I took it as a label.
- tension (for negative association): a plain label for negative correlation.
- favours, shares, typical size, fixed total: the layout of Figure 8 suggests simplex coordinates normalised to a total.
- executives' measures: unknown; my guess is measures of effectiveness (MoE).
- primary regime: a parameter setting of the case; the caption gives "aggressive controller gains".
- generalized posynomial: a posynomial in exponentials of the exponents, I guessed.
- attribute (versus metric): a raw measure before conditioning, I guessed.
- near-frontier band: designs with |log V| under 1/tau.
- Pareto-efficient, weakly: no other design is better in every metric; weakly, none is strictly better in all.
- facet-normal reading, interior: unexplained; I took interior as the interior of the simplex.
- conditioned metric: a metric after a transform, I guessed.

## 9. Whether I would proceed to the whole paper

I would proceed. The structure is clear from the headings and the main results: a definition of the object, a short list of what is and is not claimed, and an open-questions list the paper names itself. The figures repay the time: Figures 1, 4 and 8 read well once their legends are understood, and the captions do most of the explaining. I would expect to stop at the hypothesis labels, at the several-term section, and at Theorem 12, where I could not follow the sign in the Hessian. The points above that are unresolved in the captions and the definitions (the two n-lemma rules, the overloaded "support" and "f_k", the missing tables, the Numerical reference) are where I would need the full text before I could say anything about a claim.
