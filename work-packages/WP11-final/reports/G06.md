## 1. What it is about

A method for summarising a table of designs that have been evaluated on several metrics. Each design is a row of log-metrics. The method fits a posynomial to the outer edge of that table: a surface V = 1, with V a weighted geometric mean of the metrics scaled so that it is one on the surface. The surface is built to be a soft support, so a few designs may lie beyond it. Each monomial term of the fit is read as an "n-lemma": the set of metrics that trade against each other on that piece, with exponents read as elasticities. A reversible chart puts each design at a position along the surface and a level relative to it. The worked example is a cubesat model with 13 attributes and 2,134 feasible designs. The paper says it claims no priority over the families it lists, and it presents the method as a model-based trade analysis rather than a replacement for PCA or Pareto analysis.

## 2. The main results, as I now understand them

- The surface V = 1 is the level set of a weighted geometric mean (Theorem 1). Its position is not the Pareto boundary. Designs are read against it, and elasticities come from it.
- A one-term fit is a convex program in log variables (Theorems 2 to 4). At infinite temperature it is a linear program. Every minimiser is global, but uniqueness is not claimed.
- With one term, the posynomial extension reduces to the monomial, and a maximiser of V with positive exponents is weakly Pareto-efficient (Theorems 8 and 9). The converse is not claimed.
- For several terms the fit is not convex. The paper gives a descent result, a numerical stationarity measure (the term gap), local optimality conditional on an assignment margin, and a second-order certificate for soft fits (Theorems 10 to 12, Lemmas 1 and 2, Proposition 3).
- On the cubesat table, the one-term fit gives a 7-metric contending set. Two terms split the tension into two sets with 8 and 7 metrics, with term shares 0.74 and 0.26. Dominance on all 13 attributes returns 2,134 of 2,134 designs, while on four metrics it returns 236.
- A worked decision reading (Table 2) prices the choices that the executives face. I checked the fleet-cost arithmetic in Table 2 and the text (fleet of 5 at 1,686,142 USD against fleet of 4 at 1,127,465 USD, a difference of about 0.56 M USD). It is internally consistent.
- Validation is limited. Planted tables are recovered in 16 of 20 cases (Appendix D.14). A synthetic HoroPCA comparison (Appendix H) is built around the method's own design. The paper states the reading of the HoroPCA comparison as a constructed test, not a benchmark.

## 3. What I would do with it next

I would first settle the prior-art question directly, by reading the Charnes, Cooper, Seiford and Stutz papers (1982 and 1983) and Banker and Maindiratta (1986) myself, since the register's entries for them are marked "read by title" and "read by abstract". Then I would run the one-term fit on a table of my own where the true edge is known and not log-linear, to see how far the piecewise structure and the chosen temperature drive the exponents. I would also check the stability of the term structure under a change of the 10 percent convention (Section 5, "a convention that keeps one extreme design from setting the surface").

## 4. What I would say to a colleague

The one-term fit is a soft-maximum, one-sided, log-linear envelope with a geometric-programming form. It is close to multiplicative DEA and to piecewise log-linear frontier estimation, which the appendix register itself describes in nearly the same words. The contribution I can see is the combination: several exponent vectors fitted at once to every design, a temperature convention, a reversible chart and a pull-back to attribute units. That combination may be worth having, but it is a combination of known pieces, and the main text does not make that comparison as sharply as the appendix does. The numerical support is careful, and the appendix is candid about where tables move. The interpretive claims (elasticities, "tension", "novelty") need more care than the prose gives them.

## 5. What the method does not do, and what the paper does not claim

Per the paper and my reading:
- It does not estimate the Pareto boundary. The paper says it "claims nothing as novel" and "does not replace PCA or Pareto analysis". It takes the Pareto boundary as a property of the system that is not observed (Appendix E, G-1).
- It gives no statistical consistency, no global convergence of the alternation (Q1, Q2), no identifiability of the active metrics under noise (Q3), and no guarantee that the surface recovers the Pareto boundary rather than the supporting envelope (Q4).
- The exponents are properties of the sample, not estimates with a sampling error (Section 10, Limits).
- The level V depends on the convention that 10 percent of designs lie above V = 1 (Section 5). The paper says this convention is a choice.
- K is the analyst's choice (assumption iii). Also, the paper's checks pick K = 2 for the case.
- Selecting a design is left to people. The paper says the method "prices the question and does not answer it".
- Interpretation and the adequacy of the hypotheses for a table are for a human (Section 10, "Verify and validate").

## 6. Points of confusion, imprecision or stopping, in the order I met them

1. Abstract: "A fitted posynomial reference surface models the data underlying the table, not the Pareto boundary". I expected a single object. The paper then calls three different things "frontier" (Definition 1: "Three objects carry the word frontier"), and the surface is called "reference" in one place and "frontier" in others. I had to keep the three objects apart by hand.

2. Section 1, Contribution: "On this case, with the number of designs and of metrics, dominance stops shortlisting: its nondominated set nears the whole table." I expected a point about the method. With 13 attributes, most random samples are nondominated, so the count is largely generic to dimension. The 13 attributes also include near-duplicates. Appendix C.5 says pairs at |Spearman| 0.99 or above are "named one axis", and the main text says the power margin and battery peak power ratio have Spearman "at least 0.97". The count is not adjusted for that.

3. Section 2, Pareto paragraph: "Dominance is a hard comparison that stops discriminating, on this case, as metrics are added." I agree with the observation, but it is a property of dominance, not of the method.

4. Section 2, Data envelopment analysis: "Our surface is piecewise log-linear, with constant elasticities within a term." Appendix G, the entry for Multiplicative DEA, says the shared element is "A piecewise log-linear fit to the data from above, each piece a product of powers". The main text does not cite Charnes et al. (1982, 1983) or Banker and Maindiratta (1986) at all. I found them only in Appendix G and the works cited. For a DEA reader this is the first place I would expect the comparison, and it is missing from the main text.

5. Section 2, the title: "The title's 'hyperbolic' is defined in Section 4 and Appendix A." Section 4 gives the rectangular hyperbola m1 m2 = const only when the two weights are equal, which is a special case of the level sets. Appendix G has another meaning of "hyperbolic" in the DEA literature, "The hyperbolic graph measure of efficiency". The title word is therefore a special case with a second meaning already in the field.

6. Section 3, "Words": "Three objects carry the word frontier (Definition 1)." This is the point at which the frontier vocabulary becomes three separate things.

7. Definition 3 (posynomial): "c_k = e^{-κβ_k} is the 1/κ power of a posynomial, a sum of monomials (Theorem 8)." I could not parse what the sentence says. The next sentence, "Term aggregation: the formula is the sum", and the phrase "The package's mean (κ=∞: the maximum)" also left me without a referent for "the package's mean". The definition of the term aggregation needed reading three times.

8. Definition 4: "one inside the band weighs within a factor e of the weight a design on the surface would have (Appendix B, L5.25)." I did not know what "weighs" means here, and I did not check L5.25.

9. Definition 6 (n-lemma): "the support (exponents above 10-9) of the exact-maximum (τ = ∞) fit ... f_k of equation (2)". The forward reference to equation (2) is in Section 6.2. There are three thresholds: 10^-9 for support, 10^-3 for participation (Section 5), and 10^-12 for "numerically zero". The sentence "a member below the participation threshold contends in the limit and carries no elasticity at the fit temperature" was the point where I had to stop. The name "n-lemma" then depends on the 10^-9 cut.

10. Figure 1 caption: "a corner, one metric binds alone; an edge, a dilemma of the two metrics at its ends; the interior, a trilemma." Clear, but the analogy is to support size, so the name is only as stable as the threshold.

11. Section 5, Conditioning: "Rescaling one metric changes which term is tangent, so a stable fitted direction answers that question and is not a context-free discovery." This sentence is clear and important, and I was glad to see it.

12. Section 5, Simplifying assumptions: "(vi) A log-convex envelope: Φ_K is convex in u; no K represents non-log-convex structure." The same object is called "log-convex" here and "convex in u" elsewhere. Later, in Section 7, "Ascent may reach only the log-convex part of the Pareto boundary". I could not tell whether "log-convex" in Section 7 refers to the same property.

13. Section 6 codes: "F5 and F10, the maximum is attained, and F12, the minimum is attained, restrict nothing." The fallback codes are not defined in the main text. I had to find them in the appendix to know that F is a fallback and I is an inherited result.

14. Theorem 2: "for the facet-normal reading, w_j > 0 for every j there (R1's relation at the fit temperature ...)" and "R1 ... does not imply that the finite-τ minimizer is interior." I did not know which "interior" is meant (of the simplex, or of the face).

15. Theorem 5, Proof 8: "Identifying these rates with the fit needs Theorem 2's hypothesis." I could not tell which rates are meant.

16. Theorem 6: "(Z, log V) is a bijection too (at K > 1 with Φ_K)." The K > 1 case is stated without the reason it holds.

17. Theorem 7: "the offset enters only through Σ_j w_j = 1." Terse, but correct as far as I could follow.

18. Corollary 1 (noise ridge). I first read the ridge coefficient as τ^2/2 from the rendered text. I then checked the MathML of the proof body, and it is \tfrac{\tau}{2} w^T Σ w, which agrees with Appendix B L18 and Proposition 2. Not an error. The proof is conditional on "noise small" and the hypotheses list omits A10, which the appendix uses for L18.

19. Propositions and labels: Proposition 3 and Corollary 2 are both labelled "(L14)". The reader cannot tell which lemma script backs which statement from the label alone.

20. Section 6.2, the mean slack: "F(W) = -(1/N) Σ Φ_K(u_i)" with the phrase "how far the surface lies above a design". Because designs beyond the surface have positive Φ, the slack is negative for them. ## 7. Terms whose meaning I had to guess

- "reference surface": the set V = 1 at finite τ, a fitted object, distinct from the Pareto boundary (my guess, from Definition 1).
- "supporting envelope": the τ = ∞ fit, the outer limit of the surface (guess, from Definition 1 and Appendix E G-1).
- "energy differential" for log V: a design's level relative to the surface, not a physical quantity (guess, from Definition 2).
- "reach β_k": how far the data extend along w_k (from Definition 7; I took it as the soft maximum of the scores).
- "share": the fraction of near-frontier designs in a term's cell (from Definition 7).
- "contending set" versus "participation": support above 10^-9 at τ = ∞ versus exponent above 10^-3 at the fit temperature (guess, from Definition 6 and Section 5).
- "mean slack": the negative of the mean level over designs at the fitted surface (guess; see point 20 above).
- "label" (the sharp assignment k(i)): the term that scores a design highest, used for the alternation (guess, from Section 6.2 and Appendix B.2).
- "term gap": the simplex first-order violation of a term's convex refit (from Lemma 1).
- "band" 1/τ: a near-frontier width in log V (from Definition 4).
- "certified" (solver status): a numerical, measured status, not a theorem (guess, from Section 6.2).
- "hole" and "dent" in a chart: low-density regions and surface shapes (guess; the stated rule for holes was not in what I read).
- "the package's mean": the log-mean-exp at κ = ∞ with a constant removed (guess).
- "one-term tangent" and "tangent": where the term's surface touches the data (guess).
