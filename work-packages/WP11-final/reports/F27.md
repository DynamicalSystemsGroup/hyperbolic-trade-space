## 1. What this is about

A table of designs, each scored on several metrics (strictly positive, larger is better), is fitted with a one-sided "reference surface": a posynomial whose logarithm is a soft maximum over K linear pieces ("terms"). Each term has an exponent vector on the probability simplex, read as elasticities (percent change in one metric per percent change in another) along that piece. Each design gets a position on the surface (a chart Z) and a level log V relative to it. The mathematical core is a one-term fit (K = 1), which is a geometric program in the log variables, and a several-term fit (K > 1), which is not convex in the terms and comes with three levels of guarantee, all local. The case is a cubesat attitude-control and power trade study: 3,000 sampled designs per regime, 2,134 (primary) and 2,355 (stock) feasible, 13 metrics, with a fit at K = 1 and K = 2 and a decision table of five candidate designs.

## 2. The main results, as I now understand them

- Theorem 1 (L1): V is homogeneous of degree one in the metrics, and V(m / V(m)) = 1.
- Theorems 2 and 3 (L3, L4): for K = 1 at finite tau the minimiser satisfies the simplex KKT conditions, and J_tau is convex in the exponents (not strictly; uniqueness is not claimed).
- Theorems 5 and 6 (L7, L8): on the surface, the elasticity of metric j with respect to metric i is -w_i / w_j, and the chart (Z, log V) is a bijection with the log-metrics.
- Theorem 7 (L9): under log-affine conditioning the pull-back to attribute units is exact.
- Theorem 8 (L10): the posynomial extension Phi_K lies within (log K)/kappa of the largest term, and tends to the maximum as kappa goes to infinity.
- Theorem 9 (L11): a maximiser of V is weakly efficient over the set maximised over, and efficient when every exponent is positive.
- Theorems 10 to 12, Lemmas 1 and 2, Proposition 3, Corollary 2 (L12 to L17): for K > 1, the alternation is descent (non-increasing mean slack), each term's gap bounds its sub-optimality, local optimality holds only within a stable radius, and a second-order test certifies soft fits. No global result.
- Proposition 2 and Corollary 1 (L6, L18, L20): Gaussian noise with covariance Sigma adds a ridge (tau/2) w' Sigma w to the log of the expected exponential, moving the exponents to first order.
- Case (Section 9, D.3, D.19): K = 1 has a 7-metric contending set (primary); K = 2 splits it into terms of 8 and 7 metrics, with peak slew power leading a term of its own (term shares 0.74 and 0.26); requirement pass rates and a 28-design shortlist with five named choices.

I did not verify the numbers of the case; I checked internal consistency where the text allowed it (see section 6).

## 3. What I would do next with it

First, settle which estimator defines the contending sets for K > 1 (item 6.5 below); everything in Section 9 that reads per-term exponents depends on it. Second, state the minimality and positivity premises of Theorem 2 and Proposition 3 at the finite-tau minimiser, not at tau = infinity. Third, name the set S in Theorem 9 and say what it is when the attainable set is unobserved. Fourth, test K > 1 on a planted front that is not log-convex, to show what the convexity of Phi_K rules out in practice, and on a planted case where a certified fit misses the answer. Fifth, give a threshold, or drop the word "small", for conditioning_. Sixth, state the contrast between regimes as a comparison of two design spaces, since the controller-gain ranges differ.

## 4. What I would tell a colleague

It is a convex-analytic one-sided fit, not a regression and not a Pareto method. For one term it is a geometric program with a clean KKT story and exact pull-back to attributes; the elasticities are readable. For several terms the author is explicit that the solver gives local, numerically checked stationarity, not optimality, and that a certificate there means the objective's second-order test holds, not that the boundary is right. The case is a demonstration on illustrative cost and hardware values. Read the claims in Sections 5 and 9 with the caveats of Appendix E.

## 5. What the method does not do, and what the paper does not claim

From the text, the paper does not claim: a global optimum or convergence for K > 1 (Q1); consistency or a sampling rate (G-11); that the fitted surface is the Pareto boundary (Q4, G-18); that elasticities are causal (Appendix A, "Not"); that the lemmas are formal verification (Appendix B); that the case's costs or hardware are real; a priority or novelty claim for the method (Section 1). It does not decide K, does not handle discrete design variables, and treats zero metrics by dropping the row.

From my reading, the paper's own tables show things the Discussion does not foreground. First, on the planted tables where the fit misses the answer (D.14), the four failing seeds are reported with status "certified" at temperature 50 and largest exponent errors of 0.11 to 0.34. So a certificate in this sense is compatible with a wrong answer; the main text says the certificate concerns the objective, but does not say this about the failing cases. Second, the noise-ridge prediction (D.18) overshoots the measured shift by factors of about 1.6 to 180 at the noise levels where it is not within the spread (for example 29.9 predicted against 0.167 measured at 10 percent, stock); the text says only that "the prediction exceeds the measurement". Third, the "relative improvement" reading of V - 1 (Section 10) depends on the metric scale set by the conditioning constants c_j and k_j, which the paper elsewhere says is a choice.

## 6. Where I was confused, stopped, or found something imprecise (in the order I met it)

6.1 Section 3, "Definition 1" and "Results". The Main results paragraph says "The fitted reference surface is where the invariant value ... equals one (Theorem 1)". Theorem 1 is the homogeneity identity V(m / V(m)) = 1. The surface is the level set of Definition 2 (V = 1 where the posynomial equals one). I expected the cross-reference to point to Definition 2. I could follow the logic once I read both, but a reader who follows the link lands on a different statement.

6.2 Symbol V. Definition 2 gives V(m) = e^{-beta} times the product of m_j^{w_j}, scaled so that V = 1 on the surface. Appendix A gives "invariant value | V(m) = product of m_j^{w_j}" without the factor, and Appendix B.1 (L1) states "V(m) = prod_j m_j^{w_j}" and then "V(m e^{-beta}) = e^{-beta} V(m)". So the same symbol is used for the scaled and unscaled function, and each reader must work out which is meant at each use.

6.3 Definition 6 and the cutoffs. "The contending set of term k is the support (exponents above 10^-9) of the exact-maximum (tau = infinity) fit to the term's cell". Elsewhere the participation threshold is 10^-3 (Section 5, Figure 4, D.3) and numerical zero is 10^-12 (Section 5, Appendix A). Three cutoffs for two notions. Probably harmless, but the definition is the one that names the n-lemma, so I would have expected one rule.

6.4 Theorem 2 and its ledger entry L3.9. The hypothesis reads "for the facet-normal reading, w_j > 0 for every j there (R1's relation at the fit temperature, Appendix B, L3.9)". The ledger entry for L3.9 says R1 "is a statement about the hard-support (tau = infinity) geometry and does not imply that the finite-tau minimizer of J_tau is interior". The text of Section 6.1 says the same. So the hypothesis is attributed to R1 but R1 is said not to supply it at the fit temperature. Separately, as I read L3, the equal-components conclusion on the support follows from KKT without positivity; the stronger conclusion that all components are equal (L3.22) does use positivity of each coordinate at the minimiser. I could not tell whether the hypothesis is meant as an assumption on the minimiser.

6.5 Definition 6 against Section 6.2 and Appendix D.13. Section 6.2 says: "Refitting a term on its own rows, the plain one-term fit on its cell, is ill-posed (observed on planted data, Appendix D.13); the refit of record is f_k over all rows. The contending set uses f_k at tau = infinity on the cell's rows alone (Definition 6)." D.13 reports that the own-rows refit at tau = infinity recovers 0 of 60 planted terms, with largest error 0.97. So the contending sets for K > 1, which the case reports (D.3), are defined by the estimator the paper calls ill-posed and does not recover planted exponents on its own evidence. I could not reconcile the two sentences; "f_k ... on the cell's rows alone" and "the plain one-term fit on its cell" may be different objects, but the text does not say how they differ. This is the most important point in my reading.

6.6 Corollary 1. The hypotheses list ends "noise small". That is not a hypothesis with a formal content; the lemma (L20) is first order in Sigma and gives no remainder bound. Also, the proof sketch calls J^R "the expected noisy objective". By L20's own statement and Appendix G-3 that object is the logarithm of an expectation, not the expectation of the logarithm; the two differ by a Jensen slack that the appendix says is "measured and not proved". The main text should say which object it means.

6.7 Theorem 7. "negative w_j alpha_j marks a cost attribute". The sign that marks cost is the sign of alpha_j (Section 5 says so). With w_j = 0 the product is zero and the attribute is still a cost. I read this as a slip.

6.8 Theorem 9 and Section 6.1. "A maximizer of V over a set S is weakly efficient". The set S is not named in the statement. Section 6.1 then says "a maximizer of V is weakly Pareto-efficient", which means S is the attainable set, which the paper says is not observed (G-1). Also the efficiency claim needs every exponent positive, and at K = 1 the case has six exactly zero exponents at tau = infinity (D.3). So the case never meets the efficiency hypothesis; only the weak statement is in play.

6.9 Theorem 10, part (ii). The main text says the soft model's slack "is bounded above by the fixed-weight objective". The ledger step L16.43 says "F <= G_alpha - E". I cannot find E defined in the supporting material I read; it may be the entropy term of L16.19 divided by kappa, but the reader is not told.

6.10 Section 5, item (iv): "Small K: the case needs three terms; a small K keeps each contending set readable." Read literally this says the case needs K = 3, which contradicts the K = 2 choice of Section 9 and the K = 2 result. I could not tell whether this is a leftover from an earlier design.

6.11 Section 5 and Section 9, R1. Section 9 says "R1 of Section 5 holds on both regimes: the exact-maximum optimum is one point, so the support is not a solver's choice." I looked for a computed R1 check in D.12, D.3, G-2, and the record tables, and did not find one. So R1 is asserted, and the inference "one point, so the support is not a solver's choice" is not shown. It needs a numerical line with the balanced point and the facet normal.

The algorithm described (labels, refit, stop when the mean slack rises) does not include the SLSQP polish that Appendix G-16 says was needed to close the term gap to 1e-12 ("SLSQP alone left 4.7e-5 at tau = 50"). ## 7. Terms whose meaning I had to guess

- n-lemma: the size n_k of a term's contending set, named by analogy with dilemma and trilemma. I took the figure caption's faces to mean the same thing.
- reach beta_k: the log-mean-exp of the term's scores over all rows, a support-function value. I read "reach" as a name for it, not as a distance.
- share: the fraction of near-frontier rows in a cell (Definition 7). The row share is a different thing and is labelled as such only in Appendix A.
- energy differential and level offset: both log V (see 6.34).
- contending set versus participants: the tau = infinity support versus the exponents above threshold at the fit temperature (6.22).
- balanced point: U^T p, the gradient-of-objective vector at the optimal dual weights (R1). I inferred it.
- support, in its several senses (6.34).
- certified: an implementation status that combines tolerances with the three levels; not a mathematical certificate in the sense of a proof (D.8). I guessed the word means "passes the numerical checks".
- package's mean: the log-mean-exp form of Phi_K, which subtracts (log K)/kappa, not the mean of anything (Definition 3). I read "mean" as a name for that form.
- hyperbolic: the level curves of the invariant, in general a product of powers (6.33).
- frontier, near-frontier: near-frontier is the band |log V| < 1/tau; frontier I took to mean the Pareto boundary or the fitted surface, depending on the sentence.
