## 1. What it is about

A method for trade studies over designs that each have several positive, larger-is-better metrics. The method fits an invariant value V, a weighted geometric mean of the metrics (exponents on the simplex), scaled so that V = 1 on a fitted reference surface. Each design gets a position on that surface and a level, log V, relative to it. For more than one term the fit becomes a posynomial (a maximum of several such means, with the designs assigned to a term by which term is largest). The worked example is a cubesat model with thirteen traded metrics. The paper says it claims nothing as novel and does not replace PCA or Pareto analysis.

## 2. The main results, as I understand them

- With one term, the surface is the hyperplane w·u = β in log-metrics u. The fit minimises a soft maximum over designs of the linear functions w·u_i. That is a convex function of w over the simplex, so any minimiser is global. Uniqueness is not claimed. I checked the gradient p-weighted formula and the simplex KKT reading (Theorem 2), and the second derivative τ Var_p (Theorem 3). Both are right.
- The exponents are elasticities on the surface (Definition 8, Theorem 5). A design below the surface reaches it by improving one metric by V^(-1/w_j), or all metrics by 1/V. I checked both.
- The chart (Z, log V) with Z = Bu is a bijection (Theorem 6). I checked Q orthogonal and the inverse map.
- The pull-back to attributes (Theorem 7) is an exact change of variables. Checked.
- A maximiser of V over a set S is weakly efficient, and efficient when all exponents are positive (Theorem 9). Checked, relative to S.
- For K > 1 the posynomial level Φ_K sits between max_k x_k and max_k x_k + (log K)/κ (Theorem 8, checked). The several-term fit is not convex in the terms (Theorem 10 (i), by an instance). The alternation does not raise a mean slack F (Theorem 11). Three levels of guarantee follow: descent (Level 1), a computable term gap that bounds suboptimality of each term's refit (Lemma 1, checked: 0 ≤ f_k(w) - f_k* ≤ G_k(w)), and conditional local optimality of the cells-fixed problem (Proposition 3). A stable radius for the cell assignment is Lemma 2 (checked: a change below γ/2 in each score keeps the assignment unique). Corollary 2 bounds the returned run's distance to the fixed-cell optimum (checked from Lemma 1).
- Noise: Gaussian noise on log-metrics adds a ridge (τ/2) w'Σw to the soft objective (Corollary 1, from Proposition 2, which I checked).
- Case: on the cubesat model at K = 2, the two terms separate two contending sets of eight and seven metrics, where K = 1 blends them. This is labelled numerical.

## 3. What I would do with it next

- Check whether the τ = ∞ minimiser over the simplex is unique for the case. The contending set (Definition 6) is its support, and an LP minimiser need not be unique.
- Measure how the exponents move as τ moves across the sweep, since τ is set by a sample convention (see point 6 below), and how many effective designs stand behind the fit at that τ.
- Find the value of κ used for the K = 2 figures. I could not find one.
- Test the near-collinear metric groups (power margin, energy balance and available power; battery peak power ratio and depth of discharge). I would see whether the exponents move among them under small perturbations, which Corollary 1 predicts.
- Run Lemmas 1 and 2 on a random instance and check the bounds.
- Read Appendix B, lines L15.17 to L15.22 and L16, for the finite termination and the instance.

## 4. What I would say to a colleague

The K = 1 part is a sound convex fit on the simplex with a clean normalisation. The elasticity, homogeneity and chart statements are standard and correct as worded. The several-term part is local and says so. The term gap certificate is the useful piece, because it is computable. The naming (n-lemma, dilemma, trilemma) and several figures need more care than they have. The case story depends on choices (the sample convention for τ, the κ value, the correlation reading) that I could not see in the material.

## 5. What the method does not do, and what the paper does not claim

From the paper's own statements: it claims nothing novel; it does not replace PCA or Pareto analysis; it does not say the schedule, the risk, what to buy beyond the modelled parts, or what a larger fleet is worth (Table 2 caption); it does not claim uniqueness of the fit; it does not claim stationarity or convergence of the alternation ("Stationarity of the stop, and convergence otherwise, are not claimed"); Q1 to Q4 and the monotonicity of the search are open. The level of the reference surface is fixed by a convention (about 10 percent of designs above V = 1), so it is not a property of the system, which the paper keeps apart from the Pareto boundary. The shortlist and the selection rules are in Appendix D.19, which I do not have.

15. Definition 1: "The supporting envelope is the τ=∞ fit, which none of the table’s designs exceeds." Which table is not identified, and the claim is about the sample, not the model. It holds by construction, since β at τ = ∞ is the largest score.

16. Definition 3: "The package’s mean (κ=∞: the maximum) subtracts the constant (log K)/κ from every level and changes neither exponents nor minimizers." As written, Φ_K is an unnormalised log-sum-exp, and the constant shift makes it the mean. The sentence does not say which of the two defines V in the figures, and it does not say which κ the K = 2 figures use. The shift changes the levels of individual designs, so it matters for the values quoted in Figures 6 to 8, even though the exponents do not change.

17. Definition 4: "The near-frontier band is |log V| < 1/τ." The factor-e statement that follows is consistent with a weight proportional to exp(τ log V). I checked that. The source is Appendix B L5.25, not in my material.

18. Definition 5: "Z = Bu ... is the position along the surface, free of overall scale." Z is a projection onto the complement of the all-ones vector, and does not depend on w. It is a chart of the whole space modulo scale, and it is a position on the surface only through the graph over Z defined by log V = 0. The phrase "along the surface" is a reading. See point 6.

19. Definition 6: "the support (exponents above 10-9) of the exact-maximum (τ=∞) fit to the term’s cell" and "f_k of equation (2) on the cell’s rows alone". Theorem 11 uses the same symbol with a different domain: "each term refit by f_k (its support over all rows less its cell’s mean)". So f_k is defined on the cell's rows in one place and on all rows in another. The support threshold of 10 to the minus 9 in Definition 6 also differs from the participation threshold of 0.001 used in Figure 4 and in Section 5.

20. Definition 7: "The reach β_k is how far the data extend in direction w_k. It is the soft maximum of their scores along it". The material does not say which soft maximum (log-mean-exp or log-sum-exp) J_τ is. The gradient formula is the same for both up to a constant, but the levels are not.

21. Definition 9: "the tangent space {v: w_k·v = 0} trades contending metrics at constant V". The tangent space also contains moves in non-contending coordinates, which trade nothing. Minor.

22. Theorem 2: "for the facet-normal reading, w_j > 0 for every j there (R1’s relation at the fit temperature, Appendix B, L3.9)". "Facet-normal reading" and R1 are not defined in my material, so I could not check the sentence.

23. Theorem 9: "A maximizer of V over a set S is weakly efficient (no design of S is better in every metric)." The efficiency is relative to S. The main results sentence "a maximizer of V is weakly Pareto-efficient" drops "over S", and the Pareto boundary in Definition 1 is over the attainable set, not the sample. The two should not be run together.

24. Theorem 10 (i): "the fit’s objective is symmetric but, in an instance (Appendix B, L16), lower at distinct terms than at the symmetric point, so it is not convex." The argument needs the comparison with the best symmetric point, not with one symmetric point. If f is convex and symmetric, its minimum over symmetric points equals its global minimum. A single symmetric point with a larger value than a distinct point does not contradict that. So "the symmetric point" has to mean the best symmetric value, or the instance does not show non-convexity. The rest of (i) is correct. For (ii), "bounded above by the fixed-weight objective" is the usual majorisation direction and I accept it, but "the fixed-weight objective" is not defined in my material.

25. Theorem 11: "With unique term minimizers, which Theorem 3 does not supply, and a fixed tie rule, the sharp alternation stops after finitely many iterations." A non-increasing F over a finite set of partitions does not by itself rule out cycling at equal F. An accepted iteration that leaves F unchanged must be shown to be a fixed point, or to strictly decrease, and the statement does not say which. The appendix (L15.17 to L15.22) would show it. The hypothesis of an exact minimiser of f_k is also not what a numerical solver gives, and the numerical side is only certified at Level 2.

26. Theorem 12: "the point is a strict local minimizer" when H = D - κM is positive definite on the tangent space of the active face. M is positive semidefinite, so H is not obviously positive, and the Theorem does not say in which variables H is taken. If the soft weights depend on w, their derivatives should enter H, and the statement does not mention them. The direction of the containment (the face's tangent space contains the critical cone) gives a sufficient condition, which is the right direction.

27. Corollary 1: "It is the shift of the minimizer of the expected noisy objective, not of one noisy fit, and it does not say whether the rates are right." The ridge uses the expectation of the exponential inside the logarithm (Proposition 2), and the material does not say that the expected objective is taken that way. "The rates" is not defined. A9 is not in my material.

28. Lemma 1 and Corollary 2. I checked both. The term gap is computable and certifies suboptimality, and Corollary 2 follows from it for a separable fixed-cell problem.

29. Lemma 2. I checked the Cauchy-Schwarz step: a score change below r times the largest norm of (u_i, -1) is below γ/2, so 2δ < γ. γ is defined under R2, which I do not have.

30. Proposition 3, "conditional local optimality". The cells are held fixed. Lemma 2 says the cells cannot change within the radius, so the result looks like an unconditional local statement. I could not tell whether the conditioning is needed, and the paper does not say.

## 7. Terms whose meaning I had to guess

- n-lemma, dilemma, trilemma, "8-lemma": the name of a contending set by its size; I guessed a face of the simplex.
- Contending set: the support of the τ = ∞ fit to a cell. Participation threshold: 0.001, below which an exponent counts as zero at the fit temperature.
- Fit temperature τ and term temperature κ: how sharp the soft maximum is across designs and across terms. Fit temperature is set by the 10 percent convention.
- Near-frontier band: |log V| < 1/τ.
- Reach β_k and term share: the soft maximum of scores along w_k, and the fraction of near-frontier designs in a cell.
- Cell: the designs whose largest term is that term.
