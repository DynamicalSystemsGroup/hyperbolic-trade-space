## 1. What this is about

As far as the first screen goes, this is a method for learning the shape of a trade space from a set of evaluated designs. The page is titled "hyperbolic-trade-space", by Michael Zargham. The abstract says it is "an unsupervised learning method which applies a posynomial invariant form, a sum of terms each a product of powers of the metrics, to learn the shape of a trade space from evaluated designs." The motivation is that trade studies are usually decided from documents and tables, which lose the detail of the designs that were evaluated. The page presents a model-based method as an alternative to what it calls "bespoke or opaque" approaches.

## 2. The main results, as I understand them

The first screen states no results in numbers. What it gives is a claim about what a model-based method needs before a decision-maker can trust it. It has to show its reasoning, say where the data do not reach, and let the analysis be rerun. I take the page to be saying that the method is built to meet those three conditions, but the first screen does not show how.

The figure is a triangular plot with "favours metric 1" at the top corner. It has shaded regions, a scatter of grey dots, and labels such as "all three trade" and "metric 3 trades against metric 1". It also has numbered markers (2 and 3 are visible; one is an orange diamond, one a green square). So the example is synthetic and three-dimensional in the metrics. The second caption describes a view of one design table three ways, with a black arrow showing one design's distance from a fitted reference surface.
