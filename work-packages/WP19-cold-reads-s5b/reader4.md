# Cold read, reader 4 (systems engineer, trade studies, no project context)

Protocol: figure-review.md (cold read, sentence test, ink as data, legibility, shows / does not show). Images seen without captions, in the order given. Note: for (e) the path as written (figure-candidates/textured-A-light.png) does not exist; I read ternary-cubesat-textured-A-light.png in the same folder.

## (a) ternary-cubesat-textured-B-light.png

1. Shows: a triangle with corners "settling time", "control effort", "depth of discharge". Nested closed contour lines, mostly centred left of middle, a few grey dots (big and small), a dashed orange-red curve running down the right side and along the bottom, and two tinted regions (green-ish large left, pale blue sliver at the right). A star at the bottom-right corner, a diamond left of centre, each with a numbered tag and a callout.
2. Meaning: dots are designs positioned by their shares of three metrics; thin lines are equal-value levels of some "invariant value V" (peaks near diamond and, separately, near star); dashed line is a boundary where "the leading term changes"; tints are the two sides of that boundary (the key does not say so, I inferred it); star = term 1, diamond = term 2 (the key says "exponent vector", which I read as "the point where that term alone would peak"). The key suffices for dots, lines, dashed line, star, diamond only if the reader already knows "term" and "V"; it does not explain the tints, the numbered tags, or "invariant". Plain words: partly.
3. Takeaway: the space splits into a large region where settling time trades against control effort and a small region near the depth-of-discharge corner where that one metric dominates.
4. Ink: the triangle, contours, dots, boundary and two markers are data; callouts and key are explanation, not decoration. About 15 to 20 percent of the right-hand contour area is a thin sliver that carries little.
5. Legibility: corner labels and callouts readable. Callout "depth of discharge alone" floats far from the star, its leader line runs nearly vertical and ends at the star at the very corner; the star sits on the vertex and half its body is clipped by the corner. Tag "1" overlaps dots. Contours bunch and overlap into near-solid black along the left and bottom edges. Dashed line is partly hidden by dots at the top and runs along the bottom edge where it is hard to separate from the border.
6. Ideas the data may not carry: that region 1 ("depth of discharge alone") is a trade-off at all: it is a single-metric region, not a trade. That "settling time trades against control effort" for a whole region: the label is an interpretation of the diamond's position, not shown by the plot. That designs concentrate in region 2 (dot density is a sampling artefact as far as I can tell). That the dashed line is a hard frontier of the data rather than of the model.

## (b) ternary-cubesat-trilemma-light.png

1. Shows: a triangle (settling time, pointing error, total mass) filled with one pale orange tint, concentric closed contours around the centre, scattered dots, one star in the middle with tag 1 and a callout that begins "all three trade".
2. Meaning: dots are designs; contours are levels around a single peak; the star is the peak. There is no key, no dashed line, no tints that differ. The callout text is cut off by the star ("all three trade" then clipped). Key does not suffice because there is none.
3. Takeaway: there is one balanced optimum in the middle and the metrics all trade against each other everywhere.
4. Ink: contours and dots are data. Roughly one marker and one label carry the point; the rest is a bullseye. A sentence ("one peak, in the middle") carries it as well; the figure fails the sentence test for the question asked (it shows no regions).
5. Legibility: the callout is truncated and overlaps the star and its tag; corner labels are fine; no key; contour lines crowd into black bands at the edges.
6. Ideas not carried: "all three trade" is asserted; a centred peak could also just be the symmetry of the ternary mapping, not evidence of a three-way trade. Dots do not visibly support the peak.

## (c) ternary-cubesat-textured-A-dark.png

1. Shows: same construction as (e) on a dark ground: triangle with corners pointing error (top), total mass (bottom left), control effort (bottom right); white contour lines; dark-teal region on the left and dark-blue region on the right, split by an orange dashed curve running from near the top vertex to the bottom-right corner; grey dots; a blue star with tag 1 on the right edge and a green diamond with tag 2 on the left; two callouts: "total mass trades against pointing error" (green) and "pointing error trades against control effort" (blue); key below.
2. Meaning: as (a): dots are designs, white lines are equal-V levels, dashed line is where the leading term changes, tints are the two leading-term regions (inferred), star and diamond are the terms' peaks. The callout colours match the marker colours, which helps. The key explains the symbols but not the tints or numbers; plain words: mostly sufficient.
3. Takeaway: on one side of the dashed line pointing error trades against control effort; on the other, total mass trades against pointing error.
4. Ink: nearly the whole triangle carries data (dots, contours, tint). Callouts and key are explanation. Little decoration.
5. Legibility: text high contrast; callout 2 box is dark with a faint border, less prominent than callout 1. The two tints (blue-black and green-black) are close in lightness and are hard to tell apart except next to the dashed line. Orange dashed line is clear except where it passes through dense dots near the top vertex and where it ends at the corner. Star is on the right edge and its outline touches the border. Leader line for callout 1 is short and clean; callout 2's is long.
6. Ideas not carried: that each region is characterised by a pair of metrics (the callouts state a trade-off the plot only implies through where the markers sit); that the dashed line is a "boundary between regimes" in the data when it is a boundary in a fitted model; dot density on the right near the top vertex looks like clustering.

## (d) ternary-planted-light.png

1. Shows: triangle with generic labels (metric 1 top, metric 2 bottom left, metric 3 bottom right). Three tinted regions: orange upper-left, blue right, green bottom, with abrupt straight-ish boundaries. Contours in each region with kinks at region boundaries. A star (tag 1) at the right edge, a diamond (tag 2) on the bottom edge, a square (tag 3) in the upper-left area. Three large boxed callouts: "all three trade", "metric 3 trades against metric 1", "metric 3 trades against metric 2". No key, no dashed line.
2. Meaning: tints appear to mark three regions each with its own peak (marker) and its own pair of trading metrics. Marker shapes and numbers presumably label three terms. Without a key I cannot confirm that tints are leading-term regions or that dots are designs; the key does not exist, so it does not suffice.
3. Takeaway: the space divides into three regions, each with a different trade-off pair, one of which involves all three metrics.
4. Ink: tints, contours, dots and markers are data. Three callout boxes are large and cover a visible share of the dots and contour lines in the middle of the figure (the "metric 3 trades against metric 1" box and the "all three trade" box sit over the data they describe). Generic metric names carry no meaning to a reader.
5. Legibility: corner labels and callouts are readable; the callouts cover data and sit between regions, so it is ambiguous which region each belongs to (the "metric 3 trades against metric 2" box straddles the boundary and sits near the bottom contours). The diamond is at the bottom edge and clipped; the star at the right edge is nearly hidden by contour ellipses.
6. Ideas not carried: "all three trade" for the upper-left region is only a label; nothing visible distinguishes it from a two-metric trade. The sharp tint boundaries imply a crisp partition. This is the one figure that shows three regions, but it reads as synthetic (planted) and a reader would not know whether this is the project's data or an illustration.

## (e) ternary-cubesat-textured-A-light.png

1. Shows: same figure as (c) in light mode: pale green region left, pale blue region right, orange-red dashed curve between them, black contours, grey dots, star (1) on the right edge and diamond (2) on the left, two callouts, key below.
2. Meaning: as (c). Callout and marker colours match (green with diamond, blue with star), the tint colours echo them, so the reader can connect each region with its callout without the key. The key covers dots, lines, dashed line, star, diamond; it does not say the tints are the leading-term regions or what "term 1" or "V" are. Plain words: sufficient for a reader who knows what a posynomial term is, not for others.
3. Takeaway: there are two regions, and in each the leading trade-off is between a different pair of metrics.
4. Ink: virtually all the frame is data; callouts and key carry the reading; no decoration to speak of. The empty strip right of the dashed line near the bottom is small.
5. Legibility: highest contrast of the five. Labels are readable; callouts are placed outside the triangle, away from the data; the dashed line is clear and thicker than the contours. Weak points: dots near the top vertex hide the dashed line there; the contour lines kink where they meet the dashed line (reads as a rendering glitch until you realise the leading term changes); star's leader line and tag are clean; the tag "2" is slightly off the diamond.
6. Ideas not carried: the callouts say "trades against" for each region, which is the author's reading of marker positions; the plot shows where the peaks are, not that a trade-off exists or its sign. The dashed line may look like a data boundary. A viewer might read dot density as the data supporting each region; the dots do not show that.

## Which would go in a document to show the model has regions with different leading trade-offs

Rank (best first):
1. (e) A-light: two regions, each with its own labelled trade-off pair, matching colours between tint, marker and callout, a key, callouts clear of data.
2. (c) A-dark: same figure; equal on content, slightly weaker on tint separation.
3. (d) planted: the only one with three regions and with the strongest "regions differ" message, but no key, generic names, callouts over data, clipped marker. Would need a key and real names to rank above A.
4. (a) B-light: shows two regions, but the second is a sliver and says "alone", which is not a trade-off; weaker for the stated purpose.
5. (b) trilemma: one region, truncated callout, no key; does not show regions at all.

## Remaining defects of the top two, in order of importance

(e) A-light:
1. The key does not say what the tints mean or what "term", "V" and "exponent vector" are; the reader has to infer that each tint is where one term leads.
2. The callouts assert trade-offs ("trades against") that the plot shows only indirectly; the reader cannot see the trade-off, only the two peaks.
3. Contours kink at the dashed line with no explanation.
4. Dots near the top vertex cover the dashed line; the star on the right edge is half on the border.
5. Callout 2's leader line is long and crosses the plot; tag "2" sits off its marker.

(c) A-dark:
1. All of (e)'s defects 1 to 4 apply (key, asserted trade-offs, contour kinks, dots over dashed line).
2. The blue-black and green-black tints are close in lightness; the regions are hard to tell apart away from the dashed line.
3. Callout 2 has a faint border on a dark box and looks less prominent than callout 1.
