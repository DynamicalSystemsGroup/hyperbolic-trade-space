What I took away, in my own words:
- It takes a table of evaluated designs (from simulation, a test campaign or an engineer's assessment) and tells you which of your metrics pull against each other, how many percent of one you give up for a percent of another, how far a design is from the edge of what your sampled designs reached, and where the table is empty. That would change a decision like which configurations to carry forward or where to spend more evaluations, but no page says that in an executive sentence.
- Most concrete piece for me: the requirements table. Six requirements each have a pass share and a "price" at the frontier; all six jointly pass for about a quarter of designs (25.7 percent primary, 22.2 stock); the median design that misses the unit-cost limit misses by about 35,200 USD per satellite, fleet cost by about 1,130,000 USD per fleet. It shows in numbers what a requirement costs. But the "price, bands" column is in "band widths" with no plain explanation, and the case is a cubesat demonstrator, so I cannot tell whether the dollar values carry over to my program.
- Cost to adopt, inferred: you need a table with one row per evaluated design, attributes made positive and larger-is-better (the "conditioning" step you do yourself), a Python environment, and someone to read the outputs; there is no hosted tool and no price. Install is from source for now.
- What it does not tell me (Limitations page, plainly written): it is not the Pareto boundary; exponents describe your sampled designs, not a causal trade; a hole in the table is not shown to be an opportunity; "V - 1 says how far a design lies beyond the fitted reference surface, not that it is wanted, feasible or safe"; the answer changes with how you scale the attributes ("Refit under a second reasonable conditioning and report what moves"); small samples give weak answers; predictions inherit model error. The "Three claims" paragraph in the paper's discussion says the same for the decision itself, but I did not open it.
- Not found: any statement of the benefit in time or money, any comparison of what a team did before and after, any adoption path beyond "install from source".

Did the page tell me whom it is for? No for me.
