# P6, the sceptical neighbour (paper, further-reading, theory, overview)

**1. Paper abstract.** (a) I wanted the claim and where the prior-art argument sits. (b) Pointers: "The paper
provides a literature review, formal results and a worked example"; the "paper branch" link; "Use the code" and
"Project home" cards. I expected the literature review to be here or one click away. It is not. The abstract promises
it, then points at a git branch and a build command. (c) The sentence should say plainly that the literature review
and the proofs are in the unpublished working paper, and how to read it. The branch link is fine for a developer. For
me it needs one line: "the prior-art section is in the working paper, not yet published". Without that I assume the
site is hiding it. (d) The abstract carries the novelty claims itself: "Pareto improvements increase energy, and a
design that maximizes energy is Pareto-efficient", and "only that the frontier is log-convex". Those are the claims I
would test, and there is no argument behind them on this page. (e) What "energy" means here, and whether "level sets
of a posynomial" is a frontier estimator or a scoring rule. "Pareto-efficient" is asserted for the maximizer only.
Nothing says whether it holds for the whole fitted surface.

**2. Further reading.** (a) The prior-art argument. (b) "HoroPCA: the nearest analog in setting; how this work
differs." I expected a comparison and got a link that lands on the abstract. Dead end. The DEA entry, "a frontier
estimator from efficiency analysis, the nearest neighbour outside engineering design", gives only a citation
(Charnes–Cooper–Rhodes 1978) and says nothing on how it differs. (c) The HoroPCA link should go, or become a sentence
stating the difference. The DEA line should say which way the difference runs: DEA is a nonparametric convex-hull
envelope, and this is a parametric log-linear surface with a soft maximum. A DEA person needs exactly that. (d) The
"how this work differs" comparison is the paper's job. The page is a bibliography, which suits a user. (e) Why there
is nothing on SFA, free disposal hull, or the Pareto-front estimation literature (Pareto-front fitting,
multi-objective surrogates). DEA is the only frontier estimator named. I also don't know why HoroPCA, a hyperbolic
embedding method, ranks as nearest "in setting"; I would call DEA nearest in substance.

**3. Theory in brief.** (a) A precise statement of the model that I could map onto DEA or SFA. (b) "the paper has
them" (the proofs), "Figure 1 of the paper", "The map is reversible". I expected proofs, or at least a lemma name.
The abstract page has neither a Figure 1 nor any proofs, so these links land on nothing. (c) For proofs, point to the
lemma scripts or name the claim. Drop "Figure 1 of the paper", or inline the n-lemma picture here. The reversibility
claim should name its condition ("strictly positive metrics") or its lemma. (d) None of the proofs are here, which is
right. But the three-way split of "frontier" (fitted reference surface, supporting envelope at tau = infinity, Pareto
boundary) is the most useful thing I found. It is nearly the prior-art argument, because it says the fit does not
identify the Pareto boundary. It should be flagged for me as the place where the method concedes the difference from
a true frontier. (e) V = 1 as a "normalized" posynomial root, and how β is set. Whether the offset is a free
parameter or comes from the soft maximum. How log V behaves as a DEA-style inefficiency score, and whether it is.
"Designs are chosen by an evaluator and not drawn from the attainable set" is a big caveat, and I don't see what it
does to the claim of being a frontier.

**4. Overview.** (a) A plain statement of what is new. (b) No paper pointers. Good. (c) Nothing to change on
pointers. (d) The PCA contrast, "trade-offs, not variance", is a framing claim and fine here. But it contrasts only
with PCA. A Pareto or DEA reader gets no sentence on how this differs from a frontier estimate they already know. (e)
"the positive combination of the metrics along which the data stop" is evocative but I can't turn it into a model.
"After a one-time normalization V = 1" is unexplained here.

**My take.** Is it new or a renaming? I can't tell. The model looks like a log-linear (Cobb-Douglas-type) frontier
fitted by a soft maximum, which is close to parametric DEA or deterministic frontier analysis with an entropic
smoothing. The n-lemma and white-space readouts may be the new part, but nothing here argues it. What would settle it
is the paper's literature review, specifically a side-by-side with DEA, the deterministic Cobb-Douglas frontier of
Aigner and Chu, and SFA: what is estimated, what is assumed, what is returned. I stopped at Further reading, where the
"how this work differs" link dead-ended on the abstract. Theory in brief is the nearest I got to substance. A
practitioner wants the "where the method misleads" page and the elasticity table, and has no need of a comparison to
DEA. A theorist wants the proofs. Neither is served by pointers that land on the abstract. A reader who is neither of
those would read "the paper has them" as reassurance, not as a missing piece.
