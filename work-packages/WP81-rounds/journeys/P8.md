**1. Root.** (a) I wanted to know in ten seconds whether this is for me. The page opens on a definition
("unsupervised learning method which applies a posynomial invariant form"), which tells me nothing about a decision.
(b) "Read the paper / the argument and the proofs." I expected the paper's argument in a form I could skim. "The paper
makes the argument and gives the proofs." I expected the full document. (c) Both pointers should be honest about the
paper's state, for example "working paper, abstract only". The "proofs" wording promises something I cannot open. Keep
one pointer, and state what it gives today. (d) The PCA comparison, the n-lemma, the estimator method names and "The
conformance checks pass" are the paper's or the docs' job. They are not an executive's. (e) "Posynomial", "n-lemma",
"terms enter and leave" and "characteristic point". Both figures failed to render and show only alt text, so I saw no
figure here. The first paragraph on "(1) what is possible, (2) what is preferable" is the only part that spoke to me.

**2. Paper (abstract page).** (a) A one-page case for the method. (b) "The paper provides a literature review, formal
results and a worked example." I expected to click through to those. There is no link, only a note that the paper
lives on a branch and builds with a `uv run myst build` command. That is useless to me. "Use the code" and "Project
home" are fine. (c) Say plainly "full paper not yet published". Drop the build command from a reader-facing page, or
put it in a footnote for engineers. Since every paper link lands here, the pointers elsewhere should say "abstract".
(d) The whole page is the paper's job, and it is one dense paragraph. Cubesat, SysML v2, thirteen metrics, Rust and
theorem provers are the author's agenda, not mine. (e) "Energy level sets", "log-convex", "generalized geometric
programming", "white space". The phrase I could use: "a point without data is a conjecture about a design, proposed by
a rule and settled by building and measuring it."

**3. Examples.** (a) A worked case showing the result and its use. (b) "Four public datasets" has no pointer to the

**4. Software projects (Kitchenham).** (a) This is mine: time, cost and scope, which is what my board asks about.
(b) No paper pointer. Good. (c) None. (d) The ladder, the influence analysis and the conditioning rules are

less schedule and budget slack, robustly, and the slacks moved together in most readings but not all. The cannot-show
paragraph is honest: one company, no quality measure, no "why".

**5. Limitations.** (a) What it will not tell me. (b) "Appendix E of the paper states what they do and do not
establish." I expected a page I could read. It leads to the abstract page and nowhere else. "Conditioning
(Conditioning)" and "closure effect" are docs links and fine. (c) Drop the Appendix E pointer. The sentence before it
("replace pencil-and-paper derivation, not formal verification") is complete without it. Or write "the paper's
appendix, once published". (d) The final paragraph about theorem provers and double-precision error is the paper's
job. (e) "Closure effect", "tangent", "elasticities", "balanced point". The bold lead-ins ("Novelty is not

**My take.** 1. Possibly. The Kitchenham-style finding is a decision input: if scope trades against schedule and
budget slack on my portfolio, I would see that before committing. What no page tells me is what adoption costs: the
data I must collect, who does the conditioning, how many designs or projects I need (141 worked), and what it gives
me over my existing portfolio review. 2. I stopped in Examples at the code blocks and read only the "What the fit
says" lines. I read Kitchenham in full and Limitations by its bold lead-ins. 3. An engineer wants the code blocks and
the proofs. A researcher wants the paper and Appendix E. Neither is served by an abstract-only link. They will want
the same sentences to say "paper, draft, here is where".
