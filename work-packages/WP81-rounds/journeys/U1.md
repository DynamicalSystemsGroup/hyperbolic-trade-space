# U1, the analyst with a table (README, root, home, getting-started, conditioning, fitting, tensions, chart)

A grep of the text files shows the word "paper" only on README, root and home. Getting started, conditioning,
fitting, tensions and chart have no paper pointers. [The root's missing figures were the staging's gap; see `README.md`.]

**0. README.** (a) I wanted to know whether this fits my table, and how to start. (b) "See the docs, the paper and
`examples/adcs/` for a case study." I expected the paper to be the case study or the method's justification. The
Layout list also says "`paper/`: the paper as two notebooks…" and "`site/` is the root page that links the docs and
the paper". (c) Change "the paper" to "the working paper (abstract online; build the full text from `paper/`)", or
drop it from the first paragraph. The Layout line about `paper/` is fine, because it is a repo map. (d) The Layout
list is for contributors (`claims_register.toml`, `work-packages/`, `CLAUDE.md`), not for me. It is harmless but
long. (e) `LogAffinePreconditioner(orientation=[1,-1,1])` and `select_temperature` were unexplained. "pretend column
1: smaller is better" helped. I did not know what the temperature is.

**1. Root page.** (a) I wanted to know what this is and to get to the code. (b) Two places. "The paper makes the
argument and gives the proofs." I expected full proofs. The card "Read the paper / the argument and the proofs". I
expected the full paper and got an abstract page. (c) Both overstate. Say "The paper (a working paper; abstract only
online) will make the argument and give the proofs". Or point "proofs" to the derivation scripts. The card should read
"Read the paper's abstract". If no pin is served, remove the card or mark it "in progress". (d) The long two-paragraph
framing is paper material: the two questions (possible versus preferable), the three conditions for trust, and "PCA
explains variance; this explains trade-offs". The two large figures are also paper-style: their captions describe
three-metric ternary and slice views, and I did not need them before fitting. The figures show as broken-image alt
text in my capture, which looks bad. The caption "n-lemma", "term" and "characteristic point" are not yet defined for
me. (e) The "n-lemma", "pieces of the curve" and "invariant form" wording. "The conformance checks pass" reads as
boasting.

**2. Docs home.** (a) I wanted a quick start for my table. (b) "The paper makes the argument for the method; the
project page links it, these docs and the package." I expected the argument to be the full text. (c) Cut the sentence
down to "For an analyst with a table of evaluated designs." The pointer to the paper can live in a footer or on the
Further reading page. "Links it" is also a confusing pronoun. (d) Nothing heavy. Six bullets of equal weight is a lot,
though. "Use it on my own table" is my route, and the rest are other readers' routes. (e) "Posynomial invariant form"
and "white space" are not clear on first read. The sample code on this page is the same as on Getting started, so it
is duplicated.

most, and it sits at the bottom. It says to condition first, which is right. The install is clone-from-source, which

**7. Chart.** (a) I wanted to know what the chart gives me, and the plots. (b) No paper pointers. (d) The white-space
functions are an advanced extra. Figure numbering appears again (Figure 5). (e) Why I would use `project` or

**My take.** Yes. I could install, condition (orientation and shift), fit (select_temperature), and read the
trade-offs from the heatmap and the contending set. Reading Getting started, Conditioning and Fitting in that order
does it. I found the pages in order, but I had to infer the order myself. I backtracked at the temperature: first
"tau" on Getting started, then Fitting, with no guidance on the real-data path. The root page's framing and big
figures slowed me before I reached any code. A reader after the argument or the proofs would want the paper pointers
kept, but with honest wording ("working paper, abstract online"). A systems engineer would want the framing
paragraphs on the root page, and I do not. A researcher would want the multi-term and tension-matrix sections.
