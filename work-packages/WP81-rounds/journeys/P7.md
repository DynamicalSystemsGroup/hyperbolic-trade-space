**1. Root.** (a) Wanted: what this is, and whether it helps with a trade study I would actually run. I stopped at
"constructed dataset ... three planted terms". That is a toy. I wanted a hardware case here. (b) Paper pointers: "The
paper makes the argument and gives the proofs" and the "Read the paper / the argument and the proofs" card. I
expected the full argument, with proofs I can skim, and the cubesat case written up like a design review. (c) Change:
"The paper is a working paper; its abstract is on the web, the full text builds from the repository." The card should
read "Read the abstract", or go until a pin is served. The proofs promise is the one that bites: I click and get one
paragraph. (d) Paper's job: "Principal component analysis (PCA) explains variance; this explains trade-offs" and
"n-lemma" are the paper's framing. "The conformance checks pass" is an engineering claim with no pointer. (e) Not
understood: the figures failed to render, only alt text showed. What "tension report" and "characteristic point" mean.
Why a triangle of shares is the right view of my trade.

**2. Paper (abstract page).** (a) Wanted: how the cubesat was built, the thirteen metrics and who chose them. (b)
Pointers: "The paper provides a literature review, formal results and a worked example". "The paper is iterated
towards release on the paper branch". "Future work has three thrusts". I expected the worked example as a section I
can read. Instead it is a one-line promise and a git build command. (c) Change: drop the literature-review and
formal-results sentence, or say "not published yet". Keep the build instruction; it is honest. Replace the Future work
sentence with a pointer to a page that exists. (d) Almost all of it is paper material: "Pareto improvements increase
energy", "a point without data is a conjecture", "Rust and ... theorem provers". None of this is documentation.
"Formal verification" next to "proofs scripted with SymPy" reads as overclaiming to me. (e) "Energy" level sets;
"white space within an occupied level set"; how "thirteen metrics" and "SysML v2 model" connect. I cannot see the
model from here.

**3. Examples (cubesat case).** (a) Wanted: the real test. Are the configuration variables, the units and the
coupling plausible? Where is the toy boundary? (b) Pointers are few. "the model and the data are in examples/adcs/"
is a path, which is good. The closing "From the chart back to a design" goes to the bridge, not the paper. I found no
case-construction sentence here pointing to the paper. The root's "documentation's example" link lands on the
synthetic three-metric case at the bottom, which is the toy, not the cubesat. (c) Nothing to change on pointers. The
page does not send me to the paper for construction, so it has to carry the construction. It gives a paragraph, and I
want more (see d). (d) Little paper content. "The triples are not findings" and the K = 2 choice are method reading,
which is fine. (e) What I would challenge, as a program person: "Two regimes of 3,000 designs differ in the sampled
gains" and "A row is one synthetic design". Sampling Kp and Kd uniformly in a box is not how a program picks gains.
Fleet size sits as one config variable beside wheel class. Fleet cost is the unit-cost-times-N product, so it is not
independent of unit cost. Both are among the "stakeholder measures". Power margin, energy balance and available power
are three overlapping power metrics. Settling time and pointing error are both outputs of one slew simulation. Six
requirements sit outside the fit, and "all jointly" passes 25.7%. Plausible, but the thresholds are not sourced. Unit
cost 35,200 USD over the limit, per satellite, with no cost model stated. A 12U with a 1.88k USD launch excess per
satellite looks like a made-up launch price. "The frontier that of the sampled box". Good, honest. "The model a
demonstrator" is the toy line, but it is one line at the foot. The K = 4 movement range 0.14 to 0.95 is stated
openly. I respect that. Not understood: "contending set", "pull-back", "participation threshold", "price in band
widths".

**4. Bridge.** (a) Wanted: given a chart point, can I get a buildable design? That is my real workflow. (b) Pointers:
"the search direction of Future work", "the outer loop of the search named in the paper's Future work", "(Appendix
B.6)" for the finding that the proposals reached the surface by spending the battery. Expected: a roadmap section and
the appendix with the numbers. Both land on the abstract. (c) Change: "Future work" should become "planned, not
shipped; see What this release ships below". The appendix link should go, or the finding should be stated on the page
with its numbers. I want to know how deep the discharge went. The battery finding is the best engineering sentence on
the site and it is cited to a page I cannot open. (d) Paper's job: the submartingale conjecture ("outward in
expectation, with a convergence result") is a research programme. The page does say it is open and "no conditions
... derived", but it still takes a paragraph. The "What this release ships" list is user documentation and is good.
(e) Not understood: why the level counts double in the distance, the "chart point", and a "pooled configuration"
nearest two white-space points. Also, on the cubesat the check "ran once on three proposals". That is n = 1 and not
a validation.

**My take.** The site says the model is a demonstrator and the designs are synthetic, but only in one line at the end
of the examples page. It does not say where the toy is (gain sampling, cost, launch price, fleet scaling). What the
model is stays unclear, because the SysML v2 model is named but not shown. I stopped at the root's constructed
dataset, and again at the paper link that gave one paragraph. I recovered on the examples page with its units,
requirements table and explicit "does not show" lines. A statistician or a student would read "n-lemma", "tension"
and "the triples are not findings" as the interesting part. I read them as vocabulary standing between me and the
hardware question.
