# P1, the accountable decision maker (root, paper, home, examples)

Note from the reader: the root and examples screenshots show broken or empty image areas (alt text only on root;
examples is a tiny full-page capture), so I judged figures by their captions and alt text. [The root's figures were
the staging's gap; see `README.md`.]

**1. Root.** (a) Wanted: what this is, what trades against what, whether to trust it. The first two paragraphs
deliver that well: "what is possible and what trades against what" versus "what is preferable", and three trust
conditions ("show its reasoning, say where the data do not reach, and let the analysis be rerun"). (b) Paper pointers:
"The paper makes the argument and gives the proofs": I expected the full argument and proofs. Card "Read the paper /
the argument and the proofs": same expectation. (c) Both land on an abstract only. Change to something like: "The
paper (an abstract is online; the full working paper builds from the repository) gives the argument and the proofs."
Better: drop the pointer from the body and keep one honest link labelled "Paper abstract". "Gives the proofs"
promises something I can't open. (d) Mostly user-facing. The PCA paragraph ("it is related to PCA, not a
replacement") is positioning, which is the paper's job, but it helps me place the method, so I would keep one
sentence. "The conformance checks pass" is a claim with no link to evidence. (e) I did not understand "n-lemma",
"characteristic point", "terms enter and leave it" or "log space". The figures' captions are dense. I also couldn't
tell what "the chart" is.

**2. Paper page.** (a) Wanted: the argument and why to trust it. (b) Pointers: the page is the paper; its own text
says "The paper provides a literature review, formal results and a worked example" and "Our proofs are scripted with
SymPy". I expected those to be here. They are not, and the note says to build it with myst from a branch. Cards: "Use
the code", "Project home". (c) Add one plain line at the top: "This is the abstract. The full working paper is not yet
published; it builds from the repository." The build instruction is for a developer, and I would not run it. Cut
"iterated towards release on the paper branch" or move it under a "For contributors" heading. (d) The abstract is
long, about 450 words, one paragraph. It is the paper's job and fine as an abstract, but it uses terms the root never
defined (HTS, "hyperbolic trade space", "energy", Pareto, thirteen metrics, SysML v2, TDA, Rust, theorem provers). The
future-work thrusts are a research programme. The root calls the method "tradespace" and "posynomial invariant form";
the paper says "hyperbolic trade space (HTS)". I wondered whether these are the same thing. (e) "Energy level sets",
"log-convex", "generalized geometric programming", "a conjecture about a design, proposed by a rule and settled by
building". The vocabulary shifts between root and paper.

**3. Docs home.** (a) Wanted: confirmation I'm in the right place and what to do next. (b) Pointers: "The paper makes
the argument for the method; the project page links it, these docs and the package." Link text "paper". I expected
the argument, but I get the abstract. (c) Say: "The paper's abstract states the argument; the full paper is a working
draft." Or remove it. The first line, "For an analyst with a table of evaluated designs", tells me I'm not the
audience. As a decision maker I'd leave. (d) Nothing argumentative, except "PCA finds where the data spread, this
finds where they stop", which is a good line. (e) "Contending", "elasticity", "white space", "reference surface", the
"temperature" parameter, and the code block. The figure shows a frontier line, but I can't tell what I should conclude
from it.

**4. Examples page.** (a) Wanted: a concrete case and its answer in plain words. "What the fit says" is the paragraph
I wanted, and it is short and good: all three stakeholder measures contend, two terms describe the regime, and about
a quarter of designs pass the requirements. (b) Paper pointers: none by name. The root's "two figures" are explained
here, which is good. (c) Nothing needed. (d) The K-choice sweep and the resampling "movement" check read as validation
work, which a paper would carry. In docs they belong in the "limits" or "choosing K" page, and I would link them from
here. Also "The triples are not findings." is a bare sentence, and I don't know what it refers to. (e) The metric list
with orientation signs, "7-lemma", slack, and the "price in band widths". The page is very long and mixes the cubesat
case, a synthetic case and four public datasets under one heading structure.

**My take.** The site told me what trades against what (root, first two paragraphs and the examples summary). It
told me whether to trust the method only by stating the three conditions; the evidence for them sits in the full
paper, which I can't read. I stopped at the paper page: an abstract, a build command, and a note about a branch. An
analyst wants the code blocks and the K-choice check on these same pages. A reviewer wants the proofs the root
promises. An engineer wants the cubesat case. None of them needs "the argument and the proofs" to be a link unless it
opens something.
