## Purpose

The supervisor requires the example case to be rich enough that the fitted model exposes tensions that were not
asserted a priori and that are recognizable and intuitive once explained (spec §8). The probe of the accepted WP4
dataset (`docs/superpowers/notes/adcs-tensions-probe.md`) found settling vs effort, pointing vs effort and pointing vs
mass (through star-tracker grade), with settling and pointing aligned, but not agility vs mass through wheel class:
mass was nearly inert in the contending set (exponent 0.006 at τ = ∞), torque limits bound only about 7 % of the time for
small wheels and under 1 % for large wheels, and wheel class moved the median settling time only from 301 s to 264 s.

The supervisor's directions, recorded in `WP4b-decisions.md` and the spec, are that WP4b be a coherent illustrative
satellite model, not a scenario tweak: "do both mass and power, sizing the solar panels impacts the power generation,
batterys for storage, mass and power effect the controls most naturally. can also add star tracker grade. the whole
sysml model for the satelite illustrative but it should make sense. and the modeling language with abstract and
concrete parts lines up really nicely with sampling the space (configuring) then running model testing to get the
measurements"; and "you may want to pull up-to-date opensysml and/or sysml-toolkit packages and make sure you use them.
you should get a lot for free. no need to go crazy just make a simple demonstrative cubesat config space using sysml."

WP4b therefore builds a small, simple, demonstrative cubesat configuration space in SysML v2 (`cubesat_tradespace.sysml`),
used through the `opensysml` Python client rather than merely written: the abstract parts, the enumerations and the
attribute ranges live in the model, the sampler reads them from it, concrete configurations are instantiated from it,
the mass and power accounting and the feasibility constraints are evaluated by it, and a Python `measure()` runs the
existing simulator to produce the measurements (the physics stays in Python). This mirrors spec §3: sample the
configuration, run the model test, obtain attributes. The case is a small satellite of the 6U to 12U class; the
geosat parameters remain in the untouched WP4 port as port verification only. The dataset is regenerated and the case's
richness is checked in the spec's terms.

## Goals

- G1. An illustrative cubesat model whose design variables are solar-panel sizing, battery capacity, wheel class, star-tracker grade and the controller gains, with explicit physical couplings to the controls, at cubesat scale.
- G2. A SysML v2 configuration-space model, used at runtime through `opensysml`: abstract part definitions, enumerations and ranges in the model, concrete configurations instantiated from it, accounting calculations and feasibility constraints evaluated by it.
- G3. Measured attributes that the model can justify, nine in all (the supervisor: "all of the above i think. the point was to get a rich set of relevant phenomena so we can discover trade offs"): settling time, pointing error, total mass, control effort, four power and energy attributes (sunlit power margin, orbit-average energy balance, eclipse energy reserve, peak slew power draw) and the eclipse depth of discharge, plus feasibility flags.
- G4. A slew scenario large enough that torque limits bind, so that wheel class trades agility against mass.
- G5. A new committed, seed-stable dataset beside the original, and tests that make the case's richness a checked property (the probe's acceptance conditions as tests).
- G6. The probe note records the diagnosis, every modelling choice and lever tried, the results, and the physical explanation of each exposed tension.

## Non-goals

- No change to the simulator port `examples/adcs/adcs_sim.py` (physics, inertia function, controller): it stays byte-identical and its regression pins stay at the source's geosat parameters as port verification only; the cubesat accounting lives in the new model and evaluation layer, and panel and bus parameters flow into the existing inertia function through its existing parameters.
- No change to the estimator, solvers, coordinates, the analysis toolkit (WP2, WP3) or the notebook (WP7).
- No rewrite or replacement of the WP4 deliverables: the original CSV, its tests and the generator's default behaviour are unchanged.
- No calibration to real hardware and no claim of engineering fidelity: every new number is labelled illustrative, anchored to public cubesat component classes only for plausibility.
- No orbit propagation, thermal, structural or full power-system model: the power and energy accounting is a coarse budget. No SysML behaviour, state or requirement modelling beyond what the configuration space and the model test need ("no need to go crazy").
- No dependency on `sysml-toolkit`: it is a Rust toolchain whose Python binding builds with maturin and is not installable from PyPI under that name (the PyPI name `sysmlv2` is an unrelated placeholder); it is mentioned as optional tooling only.
- No selection of which tensions should appear beyond the conditions of R-13.

- Attributes *(default, with the supervisor's answers to OD1 and OD2 recorded in `WP4b-decisions.md`)*: `settling_time_s`, `pointing_error_deg`, `total_mass_kg`, `control_effort` (∫‖τ‖dt), `power_margin_w` (sunlit power margin: orbit-average sunlit generation minus housekeeping, sensor and peak slew loads, watts, larger is better), `energy_balance_wh` (orbit-average energy surplus: generation over the sunlit fraction minus total energy used over an orbit, watt-hours, larger is better), `eclipse_reserve_wh` (usable battery energy, capacity × maximum depth of discharge, minus eclipse demand, watt-hours, larger is better), `peak_slew_power_w` (peak electrical power drawn by the wheels and sensors during the slew, watts, smaller is better), `dod_eclipse` (eclipse demand divided by battery capacity, smaller is better); the WP4 column `adcs_mass_kg` is kept for continuity as the ADCS hardware mass. Raw columns are in physical units; the probe conditions them as in R-13. Designs where a larger-is-better quantity is not positive are infeasible, flagged and excluded from the fit like unsettled designs.
- Couplings to the controls *(default)*: (1) panel span, panel mass and battery mass change the bus and panel parameters passed to `inertia_tensor`, hence the inertia, hence settling time, control effort, gravity-gradient torque and the pointing budget; (2) available wheel power P_avail = generation − housekeeping − sensor loads caps the usable wheel torque at min(maxTorque, sqrt(P_avail / (3c))), passed as the wheel's `maxTorque` to `make_config`; (3) wheel class sets maxTorque and maxMomentum; (4) star-tracker grade sets the pointing floor, the sensor mass and the sensor load.
