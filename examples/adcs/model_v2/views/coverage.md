## Coupling between subsystems (a design structure matrix)

Rows feed columns, in the form of the design structure matrix of the MDAO literature (an XDSM without its process line). A configuration row names the configuration attributes the column subsystem's calcs read, as the model names them; a subsystem row names the coupling variables the column subsystem reads from it; the diagonal names the subsystem's own calcs (the measurement is the simulator); the last two columns name the traded metrics and the requirements read off each subsystem. The subsystems are the three groups of the behavioural view, the analyst's grouping of the metrics; which calc belongs to which is the analyst's decomposition (`SUBSYSTEM_OF` in `coverage.py`); a requirement is listed under the group of the calc it reads, so a requirement that reads no traded metric is listed here and stands apart in the behavioural view; every cell is read from the model. The constants of the environment, the scenario, the budgets and the fixed parts (bus, antenna, IMU) are not shown.

The controls and the power subsystem feed each other (cells on both sides of the diagonal): the usable wheel torque is a quantity of the configuration, computed before the slew, and the peak torque and the integral of the squared torque are read after it, so the model resolves the loop in one pass, with no fixed-point iteration.

## Requirements that read no traded metric: the reason

A requirement no traded metric's quantity feeds stands apart in the behavioural view. Each carries a computed reason, printed here and in the view's caption from the same computation (`box_reasons`): the solver's verdict on the model's claim that it holds at every in-range configuration, and the Spearman correlation of its quantity with the traded metrics over the feasible designs of each regime of the data of record, the quantity and the metric both oriented larger-is-better (one axis at 0.99, tracks at 0.95).

- `BatteryPeakPowerRequirement` holds at every in-range configuration (the model's claim `BatteryPeakPowerRequirementHoldsInRange`, proved by the solver); its quantity, the battery peak power ratio, tracks the depth of discharge (Spearman +0.9703 primary, +0.9753 baseline).
- `PowerRequirement` holds at every in-range configuration (the model's claim `PowerRequirementHoldsInRange`, proved by the solver); its quantity, the available power, is one axis with the energy balance (Spearman +0.9996 primary, +0.9997 baseline).
