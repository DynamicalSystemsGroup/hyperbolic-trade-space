History and acknowledgements

This method did not come from an academic group. In our own account, it came from an engineering firm, founded in 2017 as BlockScience and now the Dynamical Systems Group, that did requirements, architecture, design, simulation and analysis for clients, all software-defined, studying the spaces of feasible designs of their systems under uncertain, strategic and adversarial users. Those systems were highly automated, with much value at risk and multipolar, contentious sponsorship: no single decision authority, design decisions litigated in public, legitimacy bearing on outcomes.

Public work shows the rest: a 2018 state-space framework tied to control tools; cadCAD simulation; Generalized Dynamical Systems, Part I, and its software gds-core; data science with stochastic simulation for design; parameter selection under uncertainty, grown from a published model; requirements traceability for SysML v2 models.

Credit, each from a public source: Joshua Jodesty implemented cadCAD and Markus Koch designed it (AUTHORS.txt); Danilo Bernardinelli (a physicist, by his profile) and Emanuel Lima (a listed maintainer) extended it; Jamsheed Shorish (trained in economics) co-wrote Part I and Kris Paruch (a mathematician, in his words) co-wrote Economic Games as Estimators; Rohan Mehta wrote gds-core, which credits the MSML and bdp-lib of Sean McOwen as its architectural inspiration; Andrew Clark is thanked in AUTHORS.txt. With thanks also to Jakob Hackel and Andrew “Octopus” Penland. Control, economics, physics, mathematics: what a convex program, elasticities, a frontier and an estimator rest on.

Zargham's record is in optimization and control: two papers using geometric programs, in IEEE Transactions on Control of Network Systems, 2014 (354 citations on Google Scholar, 12 November 2026) and on arXiv, 2013; the form of Boyd et al. is the one this method's frontier takes. He serves on the NumFOCUS Advisory Council, the firm is a community sponsor, and his SciPy 2026 talk has a paper forthcoming. This package is a scikit-learn-conformant estimator by design.

Today the team works on and with OpenMBEE and OpenSysML; this project is part of a synthesis effort to make what we learned accessible.
