History and acknowledgements

This page acknowledges the people and the human lineage of this work, which the paper’s citations cannot carry: it is the synthesis and publication of a long-running endeavour with many contributors. In our own account, this work came from a contract research and engineering firm, founded in 2017 as BlockScience and now the Dynamical Systems Group. We perform requirements, architecture, design, simulation and analysis for clients, all software-defined, studying the spaces of feasible designs of their systems under uncertain, strategic and adversarial users.

Before the firm: Zargham’s convex optimization (2014) and, with Victor Preciado, geometric programs, posynomial like this paper’s frontier, for protecting networks (2014). With Preciado and Zixuan Zhang came the 2020 journal version of the 2018 state-space framework.

cadCAD is the work of 19 contributors; its AUTHORS file (2018) credits, among others, Joshua Jodesty, Michael Zargham, Markus Koch, Matthew Barlin, Danilo Bernardineli and Emanuel Lima, and it was introduced in a firm post (2019).

Jamsheed Shorish co-wrote Generalized Dynamical Systems (2022) and its video series; he and Kris Paruch wrote the parameter selection article (2021). Andrew “Octopus” Penland led workshops on MAIDs, GDS and cadCAD under a contract with BlockScience (2024). Rohan Mehta wrote gds-core and gds-axelrod (2026), and gds-core credits Sean McOwen’s MSML (2023). Zeke Medley, whose summer internship, in our own account, focused on block diagrams as a compositional algebra and the formal methods associated with them, worked with Danilo Bernardineli on Block diagrams of dynamical systems (2024). Andrew Clark wrote with Alexander Mihailov and Zargham a community-currency model (2023), developed with the Red Cross and Grassroots Economics, which applied related methods in modeling, simulation and analysis.

Zargham serves on the NumFOCUS Advisory Council. The team works with OpenMBEE, Zargham as a maintainer across OpenSysML; in our own account, this project is part of a synthesis effort to make our learnings accessible.

In our own account, Jakob Hackel and Carolina Riascos-Álvarez worked on client projects we cannot name, as did others we thank but cannot describe here. In the same account, some projects, the RAI controller among them, were cleared for public release, special cases where the work was highly public: its design record (2021) and Zargham’s talk at a workshop in Edinburgh (ICMS), October 2024.
