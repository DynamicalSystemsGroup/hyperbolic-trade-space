# The abstraction-gaps ledger

One record of every place where a formal object and its implementation part, with the bridging
assumption, what cannot be proved, the sensitivity evidence and the interpretive consequence. The
policy these rows are read under is stated once, on the method page ("Map and territory" in
`docs/method.md`); the rows do not restate it. The words used for the objects are the register's
(`docs/superpowers/notes/notation.md`). The derivation ledger (`derivation/axioms.py`) records what
the lemmas assume; this ledger records where the code and the data stand relative to those
assumptions.

Ids G-1 to G-18 are fixed: other documents cite them. G-11 to G-14 are the reviewer's open questions
Q2, Q3, Q4 and Q1 (`work-packages/external-review-2026-11.md`, item E-10); G-14 is marked not
pursued. Q4 is answered only where the boundary is planted (G-13), and a loss at its floor does
not identify the boundary between the observations (G-18). A row has six columns. Cubesat numbers in the rows are on the data of record, the feasible designs of each regime (2,134 primary, 2,355 baseline of the 3,000 drawn), unless a row names another table.

- **Formal object**: the mathematical statement or object, with the lemma or ledger entry that
  states it.
- **What the implementation does**: the code path, with its constants.
- **Bridging assumption**: what must hold for the implementation to stand for the object.
- **What cannot be proved**: the part of the bridge no derivation closes.
- **Sensitivity evidence**: measured numbers, with pointers to the spikes, tests and cubesat
  records, or to a recipe below (M1 to M5) for numbers measured for this ledger.
- **Interpretive consequence**: how a result is read in light of the row.

## Measurement recipes

Numbers marked M1 to M5 were measured for this ledger on 2026-11-08 with the calls below (M3 and M5 again on 2026-11-12, on the thirteen-metric data of the feasible designs of each regime); each is
a few seconds of compute. The cubesat metrics are those of `examples/adcs/case.py`
(`load_study("primary")` on `examples/adcs/data/v2/adcs_designs_cubesat_gains.csv` and
`load_study("stock")` on `examples/adcs/data/v2/adcs_designs_cubesat.csv`, the baseline regime of
this ledger; thirteen conditioned metrics of the feasible designs).

- **M1.** $\tau=5$, $\sigma=0.2$, $w=(0.2,0.3,0.5)$. For seeds $s=0..4$ and each $N$: clean
  `make_frontier_data(N, 3, exponents=w, frontier_fraction=1.0, random_state=s)`; noisy
  $X\exp(\sigma\,\varepsilon)$ with `RandomState(100+s).standard_normal`; shift =
  `HyperbolicTradeSpace(temperature=5).fit(noisy).log_offset_[0]` minus the clean one; ratio to
  $\tau\sigma^2\lVert w\rVert^2/2$.
- **M2.** `make_frontier_data(N, 4, exponents=[0.4,0.3,0.2,0.1], noise=0.05, random_state=s)`,
  $s=0..4$, `HyperbolicTradeSpace(temperature=tau).fit`; error = largest absolute exponent
  deviation from the planted vector; median over seeds.
- **M3.** `case.fit_study(study, K)` (`HyperbolicTradeSpace(temperature=tau, n_terms=K,
  random_state=0)` at the temperature the fraction-above criterion selects at each K; $41.72$ at $K=1$
  and $113.22$ at $K=2$ in the primary regime); read `termination_`, `conditioning_` and `exponents_`.
- **M4.** `make_frontier_data(400, 4, exponents=[0.6,0.4,0,0], noise=0.05, random_state=0)`; fit
  with `temperature=20, penalty="reweighted_l1", alpha=0.01`; set metric 4 to 0 in a random 10% or
  30% of rows (`default_rng(0).choice`) and refit; compare the exponents.
- **M5.** $K=1$ fits of each cubesat regime at $\tau$, $\tau/2$ and $2\tau$ ($\tau=41.72$ primary,
  $45.68$ baseline), refitted with `k_terms.fit_k(study.M, 1, tau, spec.seed)`; the change of the base fit's
  largest exponent, and the number of exponents above $10^{-3}$.
